# US (and US-market-focused) concentrated long-only value and quality managers: track records compared with Buffett

Research date: 2026-11-24. Every figure is dated. "Net" means after all fees to the investor. "Gross" means before fees. "Self-reported" means the manager's own letters or decks, usually unaudited. "Verified" means audited fund filings, SEC-registered mutual fund reports or GIPS composites. "Secondary" means press, blogs or aggregators.

Note on method: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so some items could not be followed up. They are listed under Gaps. Where a primary PDF was available (Russo 2026 Ivey deck, Pabrai January 2020 letter, Nomad 2013 letter, Polen Q3 2026 commentary, Baron Q4 2025 letter), figures were taken directly from it. Multiples and CAGRs marked "computed" were calculated here from the cited inputs.

## 1. What is the Buffett yardstick, and how should these managers be compared with it?

### Takeaway
Berkshire's stock compounded at 19.8% a year from 1965 to 2025, against 10.5% for the S&P 500 total return. That is roughly 61,000x against about 440x (computed). Academic work shows the record was helped by about 1.6x leverage (insurance float) applied to cheap, safe, high-quality stocks. An unlevered long-only fund that earns about 15% net over 30 or more years is therefore an exceptional result, even though it falls well short of Berkshire's raw number.

### Cited Findings
- Greg Abel's first letter (covering 2025) reports Berkshire stock at a 19.8% annualized return for 1965–2025, against a 10.5% annualized total return for the S&P 500. Book value per share grew at an 18.1% CAGR. Over the past decade the stock returned 14.3% a year, slightly behind the index — [Morningstar](https://global.morningstar.com/en-ca/stocks/berkshire-hathaway-greg-abel-goes-conservative-with-his-debut-annual-letter-shareholders)
- Per-share market value rose 6,099,294% from 1964 to the end of 2025, against 46,061% for the S&P 500 with dividends — [Yahoo Finance / 24/7 Wall St](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/warren-buffett-berkshire-delivered-39-011245841.html)
- The 2024 letter gave 19.9% a year for 1965–2024, against 10.4% for the S&P 500 — [NBC / CNBC syndication](https://www.nbcdfw.com/news/business/money-report/warren-buffetts-return-tally-after-60-years-5502284/3832965/)
- "Buffett's Alpha" (Frazzini, Kabiller, Pedersen, NBER WP 19681, Nov/Dec 2013) finds the following. Berkshire's Sharpe ratio of 0.76 is "higher than any other stock or mutual fund with a history of more than 30 years". Average leverage was about 1.6-to-1. Berkshire's alpha becomes insignificant once Betting-Against-Beta and Quality-Minus-Junk factors are controlled for. Its public stock picks did better than its wholly owned businesses — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w19681)

### Inferences
- Computed: 19.8% for 61 years gives about 61,000x, and 10.5% gives about 440x. These match the 6.1-million-percent figure above.
- Long-only managers do not use leverage. A fair comparison is excess return over the index across very long periods, plus durability and scale, rather than the 20% headline. On that basis Berkshire's edge was about 9 points a year over 60 years. No long-only manager below comes close to that combination of margin and duration.

### Gaps
- I did not open the primary Berkshire 2025 letter, so the figures above come from secondary coverage of it.
- Buffett Partnership (1957–1969) figures were not re-verified in this session.

## 2. Track records of the named managers, mostly living

### Takeaway
Among living managers with long records, the strongest verified or self-reported net figures are these:
- Baron Partners Fund: about 15.9% net, 1992–2025, about 150x. It uses leverage and is very concentrated in Tesla and SpaceX.
- Will Danoff, Contrafund: 14.1% from 1990 to January 2026, about 107x, on $177B of assets.
- Dev Kantesaria, Valley Forge: about 15% net since 2007. This is a press-reported figure.
- Li Lu, Himalaya: 26.4% for 1998–2010. This figure is unverified and old.

Nomad (Sleep and Zakaria) has the best documented excess return: 18.4% after performance fees, against 6.5% for MSCI World, over 2001–2013. But the partnership closed in 2014. Most other famous names, including Sequoia, Oakmark, Russo, Pabrai, Polen and Spier, compounded at 10–13.5% net, only a few points above the index or level with it.

## 3. Historical benchmarks (retired or deceased) and less-famous long-record managers, including rankings

### Takeaway
Historical figures with documented records:
- Lou Simpson: 20.3% against 13.5% at GEICO, 1980–2004.
- Peter Lynch: 29.2% at Magellan, 1977–1990.
- Walter Schloss: 15.7% net to limited partners against 11.2%, 1956–2000, or 662x.
- Shelby Cullom Davis: about 23% a year as a personal investor, 1947–1994.

Simpson is the closest analogue to Buffett in both style and size of edge. Among less-famous names:
