# Asia's long-term capital compounders vs. Buffett (China/HK/Taiwan, Japan, Korea, India, Singapore/SE Asia)

Research date: 2026-11-24. Yardstick (from the assignment brief, not re-sourced here): Berkshire about 20%/yr for about 60 years vs about 10% for the S&P 500, so roughly 10 percentage points a year of excess return, net, at very large scale.

**Labelling conventions used throughout:**
- **NET** means after management and performance fees. Mutual-fund NAV returns are net.
- **Self-reported** means from the manager or a press claim. **Verified** means a regulated NAV series (SEBI/AMFI, SFC-authorised fund, Japanese investment-trust report, or similar).
- **CAGR** means time-weighted compound annual growth rate. **IRR** means money-weighted.
- **"Computed"** means my own arithmetic on cited cumulative figures. It is not a published number.

Note on method: the web-search quota ran out partway through. Some candidates (Qiu Guolu, Taiwan, Helios fund-level returns, Vijay Kedia, Ashish Kacholia, Ashish Dhawan, Kenneth Andrade, Lee Chae-won's current vehicle) could not be researched further and appear under Gaps.

## Q1. China / Hong Kong / Taiwan: Zhang Lei, Duan Yongping, Dan Bin, Qiu Guolu/Feng Liu, Lin Yuan, Zhu Shaoxing, Cheah Cheng Hye, Li Ka-shing, Taiwan

### Takeaway
The only Greater-China records that are long, net, verified and benchmark-beating are mutual-fund records:
- **Value Partners Classic Fund (Cheah Cheng Hye):** 13.2%/yr net in USD over 33 years, against 8.1% for its index.
- **Fullgoal Tianhui Growth (Zhu Shaoxing):** about 15–16%/yr net in RMB over 20 years, against about 8% for the CSI 300.

Both beat their markets by roughly 5–7 points a year, well short of Buffett's absolute rate. Both have lagged recently.

Zhang Lei's oft-quoted ~40% is a self-reported, unaudited figure for 2005–2015 only. Duan Yongping, Dan Bin, Feng Liu and Lin Yuan have no verifiable long-run net record. Their records are either personal capital or private-fund series that swing wildly.

## Q2. Japan: "Japan's Warren Buffett" candidates (BNF, Sparx/Shuhei Abe, Sawakami, Hifumi, others)

### Takeaway
No Japanese investor found has a verified, long, Buffett-scale net record:
- The "Japan's Warren Buffett" label goes to Wahei Takeda, a confectionery entrepreneur turned stake-holder with no published return series.
- BNF is a day trader whose numbers are unverified folklore.
- The best verified long records are modest. Hifumi Toushin made about 13.6%/yr vs TOPIX TR about 10.2%/yr (JPY, net, 2008–2026) but has badly lagged over 5–10 years.
- Sparx's US Japan Fund made about 7.8% vs 6.8%/yr (2003–2024).
- Sawakami Fund trails TOPIX.

### Inferences
- **Excess return, the Buffett test:** Berkshire beat the S&P 500 by about 10 points a year for about 60 years. The best verified Asian excess is about 5–7 points a year over 20–33 years (HDFC Flexi Cap about +6.4 INR, Zhu about +7, Value Partners about +5, VIP about +7). On relative skill these are Buffett-like for roughly half his span. In absolute USD terms none exceeds about 15% except Quantedge.
- **Currency matters a great deal for India:** an 18.9% rupee CAGR becomes about 15% in USD. The NSE 500's about 12–13% USD returns over 20–25 years show India's market itself was a high-return market, so local-benchmark alpha (not the nominal number) is the right test.
- **Recency:** several Asian long-record funds (Value Partners Classic over 5 years, Zhu Shaoxing 2022–24, Hifumi over 10 years) have lagged recently. Their long-run CAGRs owe much to early decades, a pattern shared with Berkshire's own decay.
- **Most Buffett-like single person:** Prashant Jain, with about 28 years on one fund, mega-scale, a verified daily NAV, and benchmark-beating net returns. Runner-up is Zhu Shaoxing, who still runs the fund he took over in 2005. Cheah's Value Partners has the longest verified USD-denominated net record but lower absolute returns.
