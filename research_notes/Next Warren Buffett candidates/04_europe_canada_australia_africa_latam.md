# Buffett-comparable compounders outside the US: UK/Europe, Canada, Australia/NZ, South Africa, Latin America

Research date: 2026-11-24. Yardstick (from the assignment): Berkshire Hathaway compounded about 20% a year for about 60 years, against about 10% for the S&P 500, so roughly a 10-point annual margin.

Labels used throughout:
- **NET** means after management and performance fees. **GROSS** means before them. Where a source does not say, I write "basis unclear".
- **SELF** means the manager's own published figures (fund factsheet, annual report, letter). For regulated funds these come from audited NAVs, but the firm still chooses what to present. **3P** means a third party (Morningstar, FE, AIC, press, Wikipedia).
- **[calc]** means I derived the annualised figure from published cumulative numbers. These are my arithmetic, not published figures.
- **[est]** means I made an assumption, usually a foreign-exchange rate I did not verify. Treat these as rough.

Process note: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. After that I worked only from primary documents I could fetch directly (factsheets, annual reports, letters). Several regional names below are therefore flagged as gaps rather than researched.

## UK / Continental Europe: who has a long, high, verifiable record (Smith, Train, Bolton, Anderson, Woodford, Seilern, Comgest, Carmignac, Paramés, listed investment companies)?

### Takeaway
No living European manager with a scaled, open fund currently comes close to Buffett.

- **Anthony Bolton is the closest historical match.** He made 19.5% a year NET in GBP over 28 years (1979–2007), against roughly 13.5% for the market. That is a margin of about 6 points.
- **Francisco García Paramés is next.** He made about 15.7% a year in EUR over about 21.7 years at Bestinver (1993–2014).
- **Terry Smith's Fundsmith Equity has slipped to about 13% a year NET over 15.7 years.** The margin over MSCI World has narrowed to under 1 point after five straight years of underperformance and a bad 2026.
- **Nick Train, Neil Woodford, Seilern, Comgest, Carmignac, RIT and Caledonia** all have long records, but at roughly 8–11% a year (Woodford's peak record was about 13%). That is good but not Buffett-like. Woodford is the cautionary tale.

### Inferences
- **Bolton is Europe's best documented Buffett-comparable record**, though at a smaller margin than Buffett: about 6 points over 28 years against Buffett's 10 points over 60 years. It is NET, in an open-ended fund that became the UK's largest, so it was scaled. It is also finished, so it cannot be invested in.
- **Fundsmith shows how a strong record fades.** About 15–18% a year in its first decade has become about 13% over 15.7 years, and the margin over the index has shrunk from several points to under 1. With heavy outflows and a self-declared style change (momentum, 51% turnover), the record is now arguably discontinuous.
- **Train, RIT, Caledonia, Comgest, Carmignac and Seilern are "good, durable" compounders at 8–11%.** None is Buffett-like. RIT's 10.6% over 37 years is roughly half of Berkshire's rate.
- **Paramés's 15.7% is the best continental record,** but it is about 21 years long, and the follow-on firm (Cobas) broke continuity with poor early years.
- **Scottish Mortgage under Anderson (about 13.7% vs 7% [calc] over about 22 years)** has a Buffett-sized relative margin of about 6–7 points. But the absolute level is lower, and it relied heavily on the growth/tech cycle. Its post-2021 drawdown was not researched here (gap).

## Canada: Chou, Cundill, Rochon/Giverny, Mawer, Shannon, Jarislowsky, Watsa (as PM)

### Takeaway
**François Rochon (Giverny) has Canada's strongest live record:** about 14.7% a year in CAD over 32.5 years, against 9.9% for his benchmark. But it is a model family-accounts portfolio, gross of management fees, and he lagged badly in 2025.

- **Peter Cundill's 15.2% a year over about 33 years (1975–2007)** is the best historical Canadian figure, but rests on secondary sources.
- **Francis Chou's long record has decayed to about 9.8% a year** (1986–2025), below the S&P 500 over the last 10–20 years.

## Australia / New Zealand: Neilson (Platinum), Douglass (Magellan), Arnold (Hyperion), WHSP, "Peter Morgan", and 20+ year managers with 15%+ net

### Takeaway
I found no verified Australian fund with a **20+ year NET record at 15%+ a year.**

- **Kerr Neilson** made 13% a year after fees over 23 years (vs 6.8%). Platinum International has since fallen back to 10.7% since 1995, with huge outflows and a takeover by L1.
- **Geoff Wilson's WAM Capital** shows 14.5% a year since 1999, but that is GROSS (before fees, expenses and tax).
- **Higher NET figures** (L1 Long Short about 20%, Ophir Opportunities about 25%) cover only about 12 years.
- **Magellan's Hamish Douglass** is cautionary: about 10–11% a year since 2007, he resigned from the board in 2022, and FUM fell sharply.
- **"Peter Morgan" appears to be a mix-up.** Hunter Hall was founded by **Peter Hall**.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **"Most Buffett-like" verdict:**
  - **On numbers alone, Dynamo Cougar.** Its USD NET record (about 19% vs about 7% over 33 years) is the only one in scope that matches Berkshire on both level and margin. It has to be heavily discounted for scale (about US$1bn [est]), front-loading (1993–94) and being closed.
  - **On length and level, Allan Gray (1974–2026).** It is unmatched: 52 years at about 23% ZAR GROSS, far longer than any other active record found. In USD it is about 15.5–16% [est], with a margin of about 6–7 points. Because it is GROSS and a mandate composite, it is not a single NET fund record.
  - **On cleanliness (single fund, NET, scaled, developed-market currency, independently reported), Anthony Bolton.** 19.5% over 28 years in GBP, but finished.
  - **On investability today, Orbis Global Equity.** 11.8% USD NET over 36.7 years, open, scaled, with strong 2025–26 numbers. Its margin of about 3.4 points is well short of Buffett's.
- **Pattern:** nearly every regional "super-record" shows **decay in the most recent decade.** Allan Gray trailed the JSE over 10 years; Coronation and Mawer New Canada roughly matched or lagged their benchmarks over 10 years; Chou badly lagged; Fundsmith has lagged five years in a row; Platinum's since-inception figure fell from about 13% to 10.7%; Dynamo's BRL five-year figure trails the Ibovespa. That is consistent with the size, fee, competition and style headwinds that also slowed Berkshire after the 1990s.
- **Local-currency illusion:** in ZAR and BRL, nominal 20%+ records shrink by about 4–7 points a year when converted to USD. Only Dynamo (published in USD by the manager) and Orbis (USD-based) survive the conversion at high levels on manager-published numbers. The Allan Gray, Coronation and Verde USD figures are my estimates.
