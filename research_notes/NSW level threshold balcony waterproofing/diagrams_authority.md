## Q1. Which public documents reproduce or adapt AS 4654.2 figures for door sills and level thresholds, and what do the figures show?

### Takeaway
Two government documents reproduce AS 4654.2:2012 door-sill figures under licence from Standards Australia, and both are free to download:
- **QBCC Technical Feature (13 Feb 2017)** reproduces Figure 2.8 Options 1 and 2 (sub-sill with a step-down), Figure 2.9 (the level threshold with a grated gutter) and Table A1 (membrane termination heights).
- **The NSW Guide to Standards and Tolerances 2017** (NSW Fair Trading, now hosted by Building Commission NSW) redraws them as Diagram 14.06 (step-down upstand) and Diagram 14.06B (level threshold with grated gutter).

The AS figures themselves give almost no numbers. The only dimensions are "see Table A1 for height", a 10 mm minimum flashing gap and a 5x5 mm fillet. All numeric guidance comes from Table A1 (40 to 180 mm by wind class) and from later NCC 2025 text.

### Inferences
- In Australia, the only standards-based level-threshold diagram in public circulation is AS 4654.2 Figure 2.9. It shows a recessed gutter in the substrate at the door, a full-width grate, membrane and flashing under the sill up to a waterstop, and a drain to stormwater. It appears in two authority reproductions: QBCC 2017 and the NSW Guide 2017, Diagram 14.06B. Neither gives gutter width, depth or falls, so these must come from the hydraulic or façade designer.
- The conventional step-down comparison is AS 4654.2 Figure 2.8 (Options 1 and 2, QBCC 2017) and NSW Diagram 14.06. In both, the step height is not a fixed number: it is whatever Table A1 requires for the site wind class. For most Sydney suburban sites (N2/N3) that is 50–70 mm, inferred from Table A1 because the site wind class was not researched.
- "Level threshold" in these diagrams means the finished surfaces are flush. It does not mean the structure is flush: every compliant detail still has a recess, either a gutter formed in the substrate (Fig. 2.9) or a set-down slab with a raised finish (Swinburne Fig. 9, Multiplex 17223, BRANZ Figs 1–2).

### Cited Findings
- **Building Commission NSW / NSW Fair Trading:** see Q1, items 2, 3 and 7.
- **NSW (NCC 2025, Volume One F1D4), the new structural step-down rule**, from the primary ABCB page:
  - "A concrete roof, balcony, podium or similar part of a building must have" overflow provision, "a minimum fall of 1:80 in the plane of the substrate", "a minimum 70 mm step down from the surface level of the internal floor", and "a monolithic hob that is integral with the structural substrate, at least 70 mm high". The hob is not needed where the perimeter drains directly to a gutter.
  - Note 1: the overflow "must be installed not less than 20 mm below any adjacent internal floor".
  - Note 5: the 70 mm step-down "is not intended to refer to the minimum waterproofing termination height".
  - Explanatory information: "F1D7(1)(b), by reference to AS 4654.2, may require in some circumstances a step down greater than 70 mm."
  - F1D10: the surface finish must be "self-draining" or "directly fixed to a membrane".
  - F1D2: F1D3–F1D7 and F1D10 do not apply to "timber decking or other perforated flooring" to a balcony, or to a balcony "located directly above ground".
  - The only figure on that NCC page is Figure F1D11 (climatic zones). **There is no step-down or threshold diagram.**

## Q3. What solutions do the diagrams recommend, and what dimensions do they show?

### Takeaway
The public diagrams converge on a small set of principles:
- Keep a recess in the **structure**, as a set-down slab or a gutter formed in the substrate, even when the finished floor is flush.
- Put the falls in the structural substrate (≥1:100 under AS 4654.2; 1:80 under NCC 2025).
- Carry the membrane up and under the sill to a waterstop or sub-sill tray, or, where no upturn is possible, under the sill and into a grated gutter drained to stormwater.
- Set the overflow below the internal floor or sill (NCC 2025: ≥20 mm below the internal floor; Multiplex: ≥25 mm below the sill).

Explicit dimensions are scarce. Most diagrams defer heights to Table A1.

### Cited Findings

**(1) Structural set-down / recessed slab with a raised finish so finished levels align**
- Swinburne/HIA Fig. 9 (pods and pavers) and Fig. 10 (screed and tile): an integral set-down at the door with falls in the slab; the membrane on the falls and up the set-down face. **No mm values.** — [Swinburne/HIA 2024 p.37](https://hia.com.au/-/media/files/our-industry/advocacy/projects/water-leakage-of-concrete-balconies-in-apartments---an-investigation/swinburne-university-investigation-of-water-leakage-in-residential-apartment-concrete-balconies.pdf)
- Multiplex 17223 "double set down – preferred option": sill on an intermediate ledge between the internal slab and the lower external slab; 10 mm perimeter expansion gap; ≥10 mm gap between tiles and sub-sill. **The set-down depths themselves are not dimensioned.** — [EFSG/Multiplex handbook p.90](https://efsg.det.nsw.edu.au/mpx_construction_handbook_waterproofing.pdf)
- NCC 2025 F1D4: minimum 70 mm step-down; may need to be more than 70 mm where AS 4654.2 requires it. — [NCC 2025 Part F1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f1-water-management)
- BRANZ BU583 Figs 1–2: membrane 100 mm below internal finished floor level, with a raised removable surface above (Q4). — [BRANZ BU583](https://d39d3mj7qio96p.cloudfront.net/media/documents/BU583-Waterproof-decks.pdf)

**(2) Full-width grated channel or gutter at the door threshold connected to the outlet**
- AS 4654.2 Fig. 2.9 / NSW Diagram 14.06B: "Gutter formed in substrate"; "Grate to the width of or greater than the opening"; non-ferrous grate frame support; "Drained to stormwater system". **No channel width or depth is given.** — [QBCC 2017 p.3](https://www.conpro.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/QBCC-Technical-Feature-Waterproofing-requirements-doorways-to-balconies-130217.pdf); [NSW Guide 2017 p.59](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2025-07/nsw-guide-to-standards-and-tolerances-2017.pdf)
- BRANZ Build 151 (NZ): 12 mm continuous gap between grating and threshold; channel fall ≥1:200 to the outlet; exterior fall ≥1:40 away. — [Build 151 p.43](https://buildmagazine.org.nz/assets/ebooks/B151/files/basic-html/page43.html)
- Multiplex/EFSG plant-room rule: "Incorporate a ramp and grated drain to door openings where a hob cannot be installed." — [EFSG/Multiplex handbook p.27](https://efsg.det.nsw.edu.au/mpx_construction_handbook_waterproofing.pdf)

**(3) Door sill on a sill tray / sub-sill, with the membrane turned up behind or under it**
- AS 4654.2 Fig. 2.8 Options 1 and 2, as reproduced by QBCC 2017:
  - Option 1: sub-sill flashing with a PVC sill flip and a 10 mm minimum gap.
  - Option 2: sub-sill installed before the door, sealed between sill, packer and membrane before drilling; waterstop angle returned.
  - Upturn height per Table A1.
  - [QBCC 2017 p.1](https://www.conpro.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/QBCC-Technical-Feature-Waterproofing-requirements-doorways-to-balconies-130217.pdf)
- NSW Diagram 14.06: subsill flashing with a weephole in the track subsill, a waterstop angle and a 5x5 mm minimum fillet or bond breaker. — [NSW Guide 2017 p.58](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2025-07/nsw-guide-to-standards-and-tolerances-2017.pdf)
- AS 4654.2 cl. 2.8.3(a), quoted by Building Commission NSW: "Sub-sill flashing shall be included as part of the membrane system." — [Building Defects Library v1.1](https://www.nsw.gov.au/media/91517/download)
- Multiplex 17223: sub-sill end dam, packers, backer rod and sealant, and a secondary membrane under the tiles. — [EFSG/Multiplex p.90](https://efsg.det.nsw.edu.au/mpx_construction_handbook_waterproofing.pdf)

**(4) Drainage mat / pedestal systems**
- Swinburne Fig. 9 (pods and pavers over a set-down slab) and Fig. 13 (adjustable pedestals, open joints draining to the membrane below). — [Swinburne/HIA 2024 pp.37, 39](https://hia.com.au/-/media/files/our-industry/advocacy/projects/water-leakage-of-concrete-balconies-in-apartments---an-investigation/swinburne-university-investigation-of-water-leakage-in-residential-apartment-concrete-balconies.pdf)

- NCC 2025 F1D10: the surface finish must be "self-draining" or directly fixed to the membrane, which covers open-jointed pedestal pavers. — [NCC 2025 Part F1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f1-water-management)

- AS 4654.2 cl. 2.5.2: falls "no flatter than 1 in 100", in the structural substrate or a screed over it. — [Building Defects Library v1.1](https://www.nsw.gov.au/media/91517/download)
- NCC 2025 F1D4: 1:80 minimum in the plane of the substrate. — [NCC 2025 Part F1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f1-water-management)

- Multiplex 17221: overflow "minimum 25 mm below top of sill or lowest opening height"; 25 mm minimum spitter overhang; membrane returned ≥25 mm into the spitter. — [EFSG/Multiplex p.88](https://efsg.det.nsw.edu.au/mpx_construction_handbook_waterproofing.pdf)
- Swinburne: "the overflow level must be below the waterproofing line". — [Swinburne/HIA 2024 p.37](https://hia.com.au/-/media/files/our-industry/advocacy/projects/water-leakage-of-concrete-balconies-in-apartments---an-investigation/swinburne-university-investigation-of-water-leakage-in-residential-apartment-concrete-balconies.pdf)
- AS 4654.2 cl. 2.11: membrane turned into the overflow. — [Building Defects Library v1.1](https://www.nsw.gov.au/media/91517/download)

**Upturn heights (step-down route)**
- AS 4654.2 Table A1: 40, 50, 70, 100, 150 and 180 mm for N1–N6 (C1–C4 map to 70–180 mm). — [QBCC 2017](https://www.conpro.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/QBCC-Technical-Feature-Waterproofing-requirements-doorways-to-balconies-130217.pdf)
- Building Commission NSW: Table A1 is informative, and the termination height "should be determined by the façade engineer". — [Building Defects Library v1.1](https://www.nsw.gov.au/media/91517/download)
- Multiplex/EFSG: outboard-edge hob ≥150 mm above finished tile level. — [EFSG/Multiplex p.19](https://efsg.det.nsw.edu.au/mpx_construction_handbook_waterproofing.pdf)

**Overhang / cover:** no Australian authority diagram found shows a roof overhang or cover dimension as a threshold mitigation.

### Inferences (construction principles these diagrams teach)
1. **Water management is designed in the structure, not the finish.** Set-down, falls and gutter recess are all cast into the slab (Swinburne; NCC 2025 F1D4; AS 4654.2 cl. 2.5.2). The flush threshold is a finish-level outcome built over a recessed structure.
2. **There are two compliant ways to finish the membrane at a door:**
   - (a) Turn it up behind or under the sill to the Table A1 height above the external finish, with a sub-sill flashing or tray. This is the step-down route: Fig. 2.8 and Diagram 14.06.
   - (b) Where the finishes are flush, fix it under the sill and terminate it into the stormwater system through a recessed grated gutter at the door. This is the level route: cl. 2.8.3(b), Fig. 2.9 and Diagram 14.06B.
3. **Waterstop angle plus sealant** at the internal edge of the sill appears in every Australian detail. The waterstop returns to the external face (the dashed line in the figures).
4. **Grate at least as wide as the opening**, non-ferrous, on a frame, removable for cleaning, with the main deck falling away from it. The grate catches door-line water only; the main outlet still drains the field.
5. **Overflow below the critical level** (internal floor or sill) by ≥20–25 mm, with the membrane turned into it.
6. **Pedestal or open-joint finishes** need drainage at membrane level (variable-level inlets or sub-surface drainage) and a membrane laid to falls underneath.
7. A **10 mm gap** between tiles and the sub-sill or flashing, and **fillets or bond breakers** at internal corners (5x5 mm minimum in NSW Diagram 14.06) protect the membrane at movement points.

### Gaps
- None of the Australian authority diagrams dimension the gutter width or depth, the outlet size at a door grate, or the set-down depth under a flush finish. Those numbers would have to come from manufacturer literature (out of scope; another researcher is covering it) or the project designer.

## Q4. Timber-framed (Class 1, light-framed) balconies over habitable rooms: any level-threshold diagrams for timber or fibre-cement substrates?

### Takeaway
No Australian authority or industry-body diagram of a level threshold on a timber or fibre-cement balcony substrate was found. The best authority-type equivalent is BRANZ Bulletin 583 (NZ, April 2015), Figures 1–2. They show a level threshold on a timber-framed, plywood-substrate deck: the membrane is set down 100 mm below the internal finished floor level, and a removable raised surface (proprietary pedestals and panels, or timber slats on battens) brings the walking level up flush with the door sill.

### Cited Findings
- **BRANZ BU583, Fig. 1 "Level threshold with proprietary raised surface"** (page 3; file `authority/branz_bu583_page3_fig1_fig2_level_threshold_decks.png`):
  - Door frame on a **sill support bar**, with packing, air seal and door reveal.
  - A **"100 mm minimum membrane upstand/threshold"** dimensioned from the internal finished floor level down to the membrane on the deck.
  - **Timber fillet** at the internal corner; **"membrane on roof laid to falls"**.
  - **Proprietary decking panels** on **proprietary supports** (pedestals) at door-sill level.
  - [BRANZ BU583 PDF](https://d39d3mj7qio96p.cloudfront.net/media/documents/BU583-Waterproof-decks.pdf)
- **BRANZ BU583, Fig. 2 "Level threshold with timber-slatted surface":** the same 100 mm set-down and membrane upturn. "Timber decking screw fixed or in panels to allow removal" sits on 75 × 50 mm H3.2-treated framing with H3.2-treated blocking over the membrane, which is laid to falls. — [BRANZ BU583](https://d39d3mj7qio96p.cloudfront.net/media/documents/BU583-Waterproof-decks.pdf)
- **BU583's summary of E2/AS1 (NZ acceptable solution):** "a substrate of H3 CCA-treated, CD structural grade plywood at least 17 mm thick"; "150 mm minimum membrane upstands"; "a 35 mm minimum clearance… at the bottom of the cladding"; "a minimum threshold at doors of 100 mm – where provision for level access is to be provided, a removable raised walk-on surface shall be provided"; "a 40 m2 maximum area"; "a 1.5° (1:40) minimum fall"; "butyl or EPDM membranes only"; "no changes in surface level except into gutters". — [BRANZ BU583](https://d39d3mj7qio96p.cloudfront.net/media/documents/BU583-Waterproof-decks.pdf)
- **Licence note for BU583:** © BRANZ. The PDF was publicly reachable on a BRANZ CDN link with no login, but BRANZ Bulletins are normally a subscription or paid product. Treat it as reference only and confirm the terms before redistributing.
- **Australian code position for timber or light-framed balconies:**
  - NCC 2025 F1D4's 70 mm step-down applies to a "concrete roof, balcony, podium". F1D2 exempts "timber decking or other perforated flooring" and balconies "directly above ground" from F1D3–F1D7 and F1D10.
  - Part F1 is in Volume One (Class 2–9).
  - [NCC 2025 Part F1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f1-water-management)
- For Class 1, BPC SD-02 lists the relevant Volume Two DtS clauses as H2D2, H2D6 and H2D8 (external waterproofing via AS 4654.2). — [BPC SD-02, via search snippet](https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/resource-hub/practice-notes/SD-02-balcony-design-and-stormwater-drainage)
- The AS 4654.2 door figures reproduced by QBCC and NSW (Figs 2.8 and 2.9) are drawn on **concrete** substrates only. — [QBCC 2017](https://www.conpro.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/QBCC-Technical-Feature-Waterproofing-requirements-doorways-to-balconies-130217.pdf); [NSW Guide 2017](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2025-07/nsw-guide-to-standards-and-tolerances-2017.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a light-framed Class 1 balcony over a habitable room, the transferable authority principle is the BRANZ one. Drop the structural deck (joists and sheet substrate laid to falls) well below the internal floor. BRANZ uses 100 mm, compared with the AS Table A1 values of 50–70 mm for typical N2/N3 Sydney sites. Bring the finished walking surface back up with a removable raised surface on pedestals or battens.
- A **directly bedded tiled finish** on a level threshold over timber has no authority diagram. In AS terms it could only follow the Fig. 2.9 approach: a recessed grated gutter formed in the substrate at the door, with membrane and flashing under the sill to a waterstop. That would need a dropped joist bay or a formed gutter in the framed substrate, which is a designer or manufacturer detail.
- NCC 2025's 70 mm concrete step-down does not apply to Class 1 timber decks, but AS 4654.2 (Table A1 upturn heights and cl. 2.8.3) still applies through Volume Two H2D8.
