## 1. Falls: substrate/membrane and finished surface; ponding; outlet locations; falls away from doorways

### Takeaway
- **AS 4654.2-2012 Clause 2.5.2** makes two things mandatory:
  - Water must drain to the outlet and must not be retained on the finished surface, except residual water held by surface tension.
  - The fall must be in the structural substrate, or formed by a screed over the structural substrate.
- The well-known **"1 in 100"** figure is only an informative NOTE ("should be no flatter than 1 in 100").
- For a level threshold, the key falls rule is in **Clause 2.8.3 Note 3 / Figure 2.9**: the deck surface should fall *away* from the grate in front of the door.
- **NCC 2025 F1D4(b)** goes further for concrete balconies in Class 2–9 buildings (Volume One). It sets a **1:80 minimum fall in the structural substrate itself** and says a tile bed or screed is not a structural substrate.

### Cited Findings
- **Clause 2.5.2 "Falls" (AS 4654.2-2012), verbatim:**
  - "Falls in finishes shall ensure water drains to the drainage outlet. Water shall not be retained on the finished surface with the exception of residual water remaining due to surface tension."
  - "The fall shall be in the structural substrate, or formed by a screed over the structural substrate."
  - "NOTE: Falls for surface drainage should be no flatter than 1 in 100."
  - Source: [AS 4654.2-2012 text](https://sarah.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4654.2-AS-2012-Waterproofing-membranes-for-external-above-ground-use-Design-and-installation1.pdf)
- **"For falls, see Clause 2.5.2"** is noted on almost every AS 4654.2 figure, including Figs 2.2, 2.3, 2.5, 2.6, 2.7, 2.8(a)–(c), 2.13 and 2.14. None of the figures gives a numeric fall other than by this cross-reference — [AS 4654.2-2012 text](https://sarah.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4654.2-AS-2012-Waterproofing-membranes-for-external-above-ground-use-Design-and-installation1.pdf)
- **Clause 2.6.1:** sheet membranes are laid starting "at the lowest point and be in the same direction as the fall of the supporting substrate" (Figure 2.1, "Lap joint for sheet membrane—details") — [AS 4654.2-2012 text](https://sarah.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4654.2-AS-2012-Waterproofing-membranes-for-external-above-ground-use-Design-and-installation1.pdf)
- **Clause 2.13 (planter boxes):** "Falls in the base of the planter shall be in accordance with Clause 2.5.2." — [AS 4654.2-2012 text](https://sarah.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4654.2-AS-2012-Waterproofing-membranes-for-external-above-ground-use-Design-and-installation1.pdf)
- **Falls away from a door (level-threshold case):**
  - Clause 2.8.3 Note 3: "Ideally, the deck surface should fall away from the grate, and additionally the grate should be to the width of or greater than the opening."
  - Figure 2.9 is labelled "Deck surface to fall away from grate".
  - Source: [AS 4654.2-2012 text](https://sarah.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4654.2-AS-2012-Waterproofing-membranes-for-external-above-ground-use-Design-and-installation1.pdf)
- **Secondary sources treat the informative 1:100 note as mandatory.**
  - The Conpro interpretation says the fall "must not be less than 1 in 100 (10mm per 1m)". It also turns Note 3 into "The deck surface must fall away from the grate" — [Conpro interpretation of AS 4654.2](https://www.conpro.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Interpretation-of-external-Std-for-Melbourne.pdf)
  - The standard itself uses "should" and "Ideally" in informative notes.
  - The NCC 2025 public-comment material also describes 1:100 as "the deemed-to-satisfy figure in AS 4654.2" (search snippet of [Conpro NCC 2025 PCD notice](https://www.conpro.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Proposed-changes-to-NCC-2025-for-Waterproofing-notice.pdf)).
- **Ponding as a defect (Victoria):** VBA Guide 13.07 says waterproof decks and balconies "are defective if water ponds (with the exception of residual water remaining due to surface tension) or does not drain to the outer edge or a stormwater inlet". This repeats Cl 2.5.2 — [VBA Guide to Standards and Tolerances 2015](https://www.builderassist.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Guide-to-Standards-and-Tolerances.pdf)
- **NCC 2025 Volume One F1D4** (concrete roof, balcony, podium or similar):
  - It requires "(b) a structural substrate graded to a minimum fall of 1:80 in the plane of the substrate".
  - Notes: "A tile bed, screed or topping is not a structural substrate, except within planter boxes".
  - The explanatory note says D4D2 may limit gradients to no steeper than 1:40, which matters for accessible paths.
  - Source: [NCC 2025 Vol One Part F1 (ABCB)](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f1-water-management)
- **NCC 2025 adoption timing.** A CPD provider says NSW adopts NCC 2025 from 1 May 2027, and Victoria, WA and the ACT adopted it from 1 May 2026 — [Nexis CPD (updated 24 Oct 2026)](https://nexiscpd.com.au/external-waterproofing-roofs-decks-planters/). Another secondary source reports different Tasmania/NT status. Confirm NSW timing with the NCC/NSW researcher.

### Inferences
- **Fall rules for NSW today (NCC 2022 still in force):**
  - The AS 4654.2 baseline is outcome-based: no retained water, and the fall formed in the substrate or a screed. 1:100 is the recommended minimum, not a "shall".
  - Designers and certifiers commonly treat 1:100 as the deemed-to-satisfy minimum.
  - After NSW adopts NCC 2025 (reported as 1 May 2027), concrete balconies in Class 2–9 buildings will need **1:80 in the structural slab**.
- **Build-up depth at the door.** The finished surface must fall away from the door/grate.
  - Over a 2 m deep balcony, 1:100 gives a 20 mm rise across the balcony and 1:80 gives 25 mm.
  - At a level threshold, the high point of the finished surface is at the door. So the drop to the outlet must be gained by lowering the substrate and/or the outlet, not by raising the doorway.
- **Falls feed directly into threshold depth.** If the fall is in the slab (NCC 2025 style), the slab must already be set down at the door by at least the build-up thickness. The fall then increases away from the door.

### Gaps
- No public source quotes any **DR AS 4654.2:2026** fall wording. Whether the draft changes 1:100 to a mandatory figure, or aligns with NCC 1:80, is unknown.
- The NCC 2022 Volume Two (houses, Class 1) equivalent fall requirement was not checked here. This is assumed to be covered by the NCC researcher.

## 2. Membrane upward terminations: heights, and what they are measured from

### Takeaway
- **Mandatory rule (Clause 2.8.1.1):** where the termination is meant to stop water entry, the membrane height "above the finished surface level" must be enough to stop water, including wind-driven water, flowing over the top.
- **The numbers** are in **informative Appendix A, Table A1**. They depend on wind class and are measured **above the finished (external) level**:

| Wind class (AS 4055) | ULS wind speed (Vh,u) | Termination height |
|---|---|---|
| N1 | 34 m/s | 40 mm |
| N2 | 40 m/s | 50 mm |
| N3 / C1 | 50 m/s | 70 mm |
| N4 / C2 | 61 m/s | 100 mm |
| N5 / C3 | 74 m/s | 150 mm |
| N6 / C4 | 86 m/s | 180 mm |

- If stormwater retention is designed into the area, the upturn must be above the overflow level.
- AS 4654.2-2012 contains **no single fixed "150 mm" wall upturn, no "50 mm step-down" and no "25 mm" door figure**.

### Cited Findings
- **Clause 2.8.1.1 "Height", verbatim:** "Where the membrane termination is to prevent water entry, the finished height of the membrane above the finished surface level shall be sufficient to prevent water, including wind driven, flowing over the top of the membrane. NOTE: For information on termination heights, see Appendix A." — [AS 4654.2-2012 text](https://sarah.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4654.2-AS-2012-Waterproofing-membranes-for-external-above-ground-use-Design-and-installation1.pdf)
- **Appendix A "Vertical upward termination heights" (Informative):** "The vertical heights may be determined by either of the following methods:
  - (a) Vertical upward termination to be at a height above finished level not less than specified in Table A1.
  - (b) Where stormwater retention is designed into the waterproofed area, the height of the upturn to be above the overflow level."
  - Source: [AS 4654.2-2012 text](https://sarah.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4654.2-AS-2012-Waterproofing-membranes-for-external-above-ground-use-Design-and-installation1.pdf)
- **Table A1 "Vertical upward termination heights"** gives the values in the table above. Its columns are wind class Regions A and B (non-cyclonic, AS 4055), wind class Regions C and D (cyclonic, AS 4055), ULS wind speed Vh,u (AS/NZS 1170.2) and termination height (mm) — [AS 4654.2-2012 text](https://sarah.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4654.2-AS-2012-Waterproofing-membranes-for-external-above-ground-use-Design-and-installation1.pdf)
- **Cross-checks on Table A1:**
  - Conpro: "In wind class N1 the termination height must be not less than 40mm" — [Conpro interpretation](https://www.conpro.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Interpretation-of-external-Std-for-Melbourne.pdf)
  - QBCC reproduces Table A1 under licence. It describes it as specifying "the minimum upward termination heights for a waterproofing membrane above the finished floor level of a deck or balcony", for both cyclonic and non-cyclonic regions. The table itself is an image and its values were not extracted — [QBCC Technical Feature 13 Feb 2017](https://www.conpro.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/QBCC-Technical-Feature-Waterproofing-requirements-doorways-to-balconies-130217.pdf)
- **The appendix is informative.** The preface states "An 'informative' appendix is only for information and guidance". The binding text is only the performance sentence in 2.8.1.1 — [AS 4654.2-2012 text](https://sarah.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4654.2-AS-2012-Waterproofing-membranes-for-external-above-ground-use-Design-and-installation1.pdf)
- **Flashing details (Clause 2.8.1.3)** that eat into the height available at a door or wall:
  - Pressure seal flashing:
    - mechanical fixings at max **150 mm** centres;
    - min **15 mm** lap from the bottom of the fixing to the bottom of the flashing;
    - sealant to the wall;
    - "a minimum **10 mm gap** between the bottom of the flashing and finished level".
  - Over-flashing:
    - reglet min **15 mm**;
    - lap from top of sealed reglet to bottom of fully bonded membrane min **75 mm**;
    - min **10 mm** gap to the finished level.
  - Sheet membranes are anchored along the top or bottom edge and finished with over-flashing or cover-flashing (2.8.1.2, 2.8.1.3).
  - Figures: Figure 2.2 "Typical vertical upward termination—Detail of over-flashing for liquid or fully bonded sheet membranes"; Figure 2.3 "…Detail of pressure seal for sheet membrane".
  - Source: [AS 4654.2-2012 text](https://sarah.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4654.2-AS-2012-Waterproofing-membranes-for-external-above-ground-use-Design-and-installation1.pdf)
- **Other fixed numbers in AS 4654.2-2012 that are often confused with upturn heights:**
  - sheet-membrane vertical downward termination "minimum of 100 mm from the junction" (2.8.2.1, Fig 2.4(a));
  - fully bonded balcony membrane "may be terminated at the drip groove" (Fig 2.4(b));
  - gutter termination metal angle vertical leg min **35 mm** (2.8.2.3, Fig 2.7);
  - planter box membrane to "100 mm above the soil or fill level" (2.13);
  - continuous membrane over joints unbonded for first **100 mm** (2.9);
  - cavity parapet weep holes at max **1.2 m** centres, and a DPC if the parapet is more than **300 mm** above roof cladding (Fig 2.6).
  - Source: [AS 4654.2-2012 text](https://sarah.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4654.2-AS-2012-Waterproofing-membranes-for-external-above-ground-use-Design-and-installation1.pdf)
- **Hobs and skylights:**
  - Hobs around plant rooms "shall be treated as upward terminations… in accordance with Clause 2.8.1" (2.12).
  - Skylight upturns should be to the Table A1 height above the finished roof surface (2.8.5).
  - Source: [AS 4654.2-2012 text](https://sarah.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4654.2-AS-2012-Waterproofing-membranes-for-external-above-ground-use-Design-and-installation1.pdf)
- **Conflicting numbers in secondary sources:**
  - A balcony-membrane guide cites a "150 mm above the finished balcony surface" upstand under Appendix A ([Chalkline](https://chalkline.com.au/practical/balcony-membrane)). 150 mm is only the **N5/C3** value in Table A1.
  - Other NSW contractor pages (seen as search snippets, not fetched: e.g. [Atomic Projects](https://www.atomicprojects.com.au/blog/balcony-waterproofing-as-4654-requirements-nsw), [Danrae](https://danraegroup.com.au/balcony-waterproofing-australian-standard-as4654/), [Hills Waterproofing](https://hillswaterproofing.com.au/balcony-drainage-regulations-nsw/)) quote "100 mm at all wall junctions", "50 mm step-down below internal floor" and "25 mm step at door threshold".
  - **None of these figures appears in the AS 4654.2-2012 text.**
- **VBA Diagram 13.06** ("Upstand required to doorways opening onto external decks"):
  - It labels the height as "See Table 2.1 of AS 4654". This is a mis-citation of Table A1.
  - It shows a "5x5 mm min fillet or bond breaker". AS 4654.2 Cl 2.7 note gives 15 × 15 mm for liquid membranes and 40 × 40 mm for sheet.
  - Source: [VBA Guide 2015](https://www.builderassist.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Guide-to-Standards-and-Tolerances.pdf); [AS 4654.2-2012 text](https://sarah.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4654.2-AS-2012-Waterproofing-membranes-for-external-above-ground-use-Design-and-installation1.pdf)
- **NCC 2025 F1D4(c) (Vol One, concrete):**
  - It requires "a minimum 70 mm step down from the internal floor structural substrate surface level to the adjacent external structural substrate surface level, where applicable".
  - Note: the 70 mm "is not the AS 4654.2 waterproofing termination height. Where AS 4654.2 requires a greater height, the membrane can be extended above the 70 mm concrete set down".
  - F1D4(d) requires a 70 mm monolithic perimeter hob, unless the edge drains to a gutter.
  - Source: [NCC 2025 Vol One Part F1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f1-water-management)

### Inferences
- **The datum is the finished external surface (top of tile), not the slab.**
  - On a level-threshold balcony, the Table A1 height is measured from the tile surface outside the door.
  - With finished levels equal inside and out, the external tile surface is at internal FFL. So a Table A1-compliant upturn would have to rise 40–70 mm (N1–N3) above internal FFL at the door. That cannot be done without a raised sill, hob or a sill that sits proud of the floor.
  - Hence the standard's alternative route: Clause 2.8.3(b) and Figure 2.9 (see Q3).
- **NSW wind classes (general knowledge, not sourced here).** Most of NSW is non-cyclonic Region A, and residential sites are commonly classed N1–N3 under AS 4055. That implies Table A1 heights of about 40–70 mm, and up to 100 mm (N4) on exposed coastal or elevated sites. The report writer should confirm the site wind class.
- **NCC 2025 slab step-down and finished levels.** The 70 mm step-down is substrate-to-substrate. Finished levels can still be flush if the external build-up (bedding, screed or pedestals plus tile) is thicker than the internal finishes by about the step-down. A level *finished* threshold therefore does not automatically conflict with F1D4(c). But it still cannot give a Table A1 upturn above the finished external level at the door.

### Gaps
- No source showed whether DR AS 4654.2:2026 changes Table A1 values, makes Appendix A normative, or adds a fixed minimum (for example, aligning with NCC 2025's 70 mm).
- AS 4654.2 does not define "finished surface level" in Clause 1.3. Its meaning (top of tile versus top of drainage layer under open-jointed pavers) is not stated.

## 3. Door sills and thresholds: Clause 2.8.3, Figures 2.8 and 2.9, set-downs, hobs, water-stops, level thresholds and the grated channel

### Takeaway
- **Clause 2.8.3** is the only clause dealing with doors and windows onto external waterproofed areas. It has two mandatory rules:
  - (a) a sub-sill flashing must be part of the membrane system;
  - (b) where internal and external finished floor levels do not allow an upturn, the membrane "shall be fixed under the sill and terminate in the stormwater system".
- The informative notes add the rest:
  - openings "should" have a set-down or hob (Note 5);
  - where there is none (e.g. wheelchair access), "a gutter should be formed into the substrate immediately in front of the opening" (Note 6);
  - the grate should be at least the width of the opening, with the deck falling away from it (Note 3).
- **Figure 2.9** is the level-threshold detail. **Figure 2.8 (Options 1–3)** covers upturn/set-down details.

### Cited Findings
- **Clause 2.8.3 "Doors and windows onto external waterproofed areas", verbatim:** "For doors and windows onto external waterproofed areas, the following apply:
  - (a) Sub-sill flashing shall be included as part of the membrane system (see Note 1).
  - (b) Where the internal and external finished floor levels do not allow an upturn, the membranes shall be fixed under the sill and terminate in the stormwater system (see Note 2)."
- **Clause 2.8.3 Notes, verbatim:**
  - "1 For typical detail of sub-sill flashing, see Figure 2.8."
  - "2 For typical detail of membrane fixed under the sill and terminating in the stormwater system, see Figure 2.9."

  - "4 Typical details of external terminations at external opening doors and at wall openings are shown in Figure 2.8 and Figure 2.9."

- **Full text of AS 4654.2-2012, all figures.** Third-party hosted copy, probably unauthorised, so do not present it as an official source: https://sarah.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4654.2-AS-2012-Waterproofing-membranes-for-external-above-ground-use-Design-and-installation1.pdf

### Inferences
- **For a level (flush) threshold, the deemed-to-satisfy route under AS 4654.2-2012 is:**
  - Clause 2.8.3(a), mandatory: a sub-sill flashing as part of the membrane system.
  - Clause 2.8.3(b), mandatory: membrane fixed under the sill and terminating in the stormwater system.
  - The informative Figure 2.9 arrangement:
    - a gutter/channel formed *in the substrate* directly in front of the door;
    - a grate at least the width of the opening, on a non-ferrous frame;
    - a water stop returned to the external face;
    - the deck falling away from the grate.
- **The grated channel is not strictly mandatory.** The text says "shall terminate in the stormwater system", and the gutter appears in "should" notes. But it is the only method the standard shows, and regulators (QBCC, WA) present it as *the* alternative to a set-down. In practice it is the expected detail.
- **Without the channel, a level threshold needs a performance solution.** A level threshold with no channel at the door, relying only on falls away from the door, does not follow Fig 2.9.
- **Figure 2.8 is not a level-threshold detail.** All three options dimension a Table A1 height above the external finished floor, so they assume the external finished level sits below the sill/waterstop top by at least the Table A1 height. Option 1, "Opening higher than sill upward termination", is the step-down/set-down detail.
- **Where the level-threshold channel needs depth.** The gutter is "formed in substrate", so the slab must be recessed locally (or the whole balcony set down) deep enough for:
  - membrane;
  - channel body and fall;
  - grate frame;
  - tile bed and tile.
  - AS 4654.2 gives no number for this depth. It comes from the channel manufacturer.

### Gaps
- AS 4654.2-2012 gives no dimensions for the Fig 2.9 gutter or grate (width, depth, hydraulic capacity, distance from the door face).
- It is unknown whether DR AS 4654.2:2026 adds numeric requirements for level thresholds or makes the gutter mandatory. The draft text was not publicly accessible.
- AS 2047 sill-height and water-penetration test provisions were not retrieved. Only the cross-reference in Cl 2.8.3 Note 7 was confirmed.

## 4. Drainage outlets, puddle flanges and overflows (enclosed balconies with hobs, parapets or upstands)

### Takeaway
- **Drains (Clause 2.10).** The membrane must connect to stormwater either by a turn-down into the outlet inlet or by a flange/clamping ring (puddle flange). The drain needs a sump, pit, grate or cage. Where the finish sits above the membrane, a variable-level inlet should allow sub-surface drainage at membrane level.
- **Overflows (Clause 2.11).** The membrane must be turned into the overflow, and the finished floor level must not reduce the outlet's design flow. AS 4654.2-2012 gives **no overflow height relative to the door sill or internal floor** and **no minimum number of outlets**. Its only link between overflow and door height is Appendix A(b): where water retention is designed, the upturn must be above the overflow level.
- **Sizing (AS/NZS 3500.3:2018 Cl 3.8).** Balcony drainage is sized for a 20-year ARI, and overflow for a 100-year ARI.
- **Overflow height (NCC 2025 F1D4 Note).** The overflow invert must be at least **20 mm below the adjacent internal floor structural substrate**.

  - Notes: "1 The flange may be part of the inlet to the stormwater system or a separate item fitted on site. 2 Where the finished surface is above the level of the membrane, a variable level inlet or grate is used to provide surface drainage. 3 The variable level inlet should allow sub-surface drainage at the membrane level."

- **Nexis CPD (24 Oct 2026):** every enclosed balcony, deck or planter with upstands on all sides needs an overflow that "operates before water reaches the door sill or the top of the membrane", and overflows should be visible — [Nexis CPD](https://nexiscpd.com.au/external-waterproofing-roofs-decks-planters/)
- **Victoria defect test:** VBA Guide 13.07 says decks "are defective if they are not provided with adequate drainage and provision for overflow" — [VBA Guide 2015](https://www.builderassist.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Guide-to-Standards-and-Tolerances.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The overflow problem at a level threshold.** On an enclosed balcony with a flush threshold, the internal finished floor is essentially at the external finished level. Any ponding depth before overflow therefore reaches the sill immediately.
  - The overflow has to be set *below* the finished external level, which in practice means at the drainage-channel or membrane level.
  - Or the balcony perimeter must be open (no full-height hob, or drained edge/gutter, as in Fig 2.7) so water spills away before reaching the door.
  - This links Appendix A(b) and NCC 2025's 20 mm-below-internal-substrate overflow note.
- **The door channel and overflow.** The Fig 2.9 door channel is drained to stormwater, but AS 4654.2 does not say it can act as the overflow. If the channel outlet blocks, a separate overflow at a lower level than the door (or an open edge) would be needed to meet Cl 2.11 and NCC F1D4(a).
- **Membrane-level drainage.** With a membrane below the tile bed, the variable-level inlet in Cl 2.10 Notes 2–3 is what drains water that passes through the tiles. A level threshold makes this sub-surface drainage more critical, because the tile surface has no freeboard.

### Gaps
- AS 4654.2 has no minimum number of outlets or outlet size. No source found a numeric rule (e.g. "two outlets or one outlet plus overflow") in AS 4654.2 or the AS/NZS 3500.3 balcony clause.
- The AS/NZS 3500.3 edition currently called up for NSW (2021 vs a reported 2025 edition) and its exact balcony clause wording and numbering were not verified. Only the 2018 text was read.
- The BPC Victoria SD 02 overflow section could not be retrieved (403).

## 5. Membrane systems and build-up depth: bonded vs unbonded, screeds, sub-surface drainage, bond breakers, movement joints, membrane under vs over the screed

### Takeaway
- **AS 4654.2-2012 allows both arrangements:**
  - membrane under a bonded or unbonded screed/bedding (falls in the slab);
  - membrane on top of a falls screed laid over the slab ("formed by a screed over the structural substrate").
- **Bonded toppings/bedding** need movement joints, ideally 10 mm wide at 4.5 m centres.
- **Unbonded structural screeds** should be at least 50 mm, mesh-reinforced, over a double slip sheet.
- **Continuous membranes over joints** must be unbonded for 100 mm.
- **NCC 2025 F1D7(2)** removes the "membrane on screed" option for concrete in Volume One. The membrane must go directly on the structural substrate, which pushes build-up depth (and the falls) into the slab.

### Cited Findings
- **Clause 1.4 membrane systems:**
  - (a) ballasted;
  - (b) fully bonded, including liquid membranes;
  - (c) inverted roof membrane assembly (IRMA);
  - (d) mechanically fixed;
  - (e) partially bonded.
  - Clause 1.5: systems are "exposed" or "protected".
  - Source: [AS 4654.2-2012 text](https://sarah.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4654.2-AS-2012-Waterproofing-membranes-for-external-above-ground-use-Design-and-installation1.pdf)
- **Definitions:**
  - 1.3.3 "Drainage cell: A durable material used to overlay the membrane to protect it from physical damage and allow the transfer of moisture to relieve hydrostatic pressure. NOTE: Timber and timber-based products are not acceptable…"
  - 1.3.9 Protection board (same timber exclusion).
  - 1.3.11 Screed: "A layer of material (usually cement based) of defined minimum thickness, which sets in situ and which may be interposed between the structural substrate and bedded finish."
  - Source: [AS 4654.2-2012 text](https://sarah.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4654.2-AS-2012-Waterproofing-membranes-for-external-above-ground-use-Design-and-installation1.pdf)
- **Clause 2.5 substrates:**

  - tile and slate underlay not to be used externally as a waterproofing system;

  - 2.5.3.2 (partially bonded): if moisture is above 8%, venting should be used.
  - Source: [AS 4654.2-2012 text](https://sarah.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4654.2-AS-2012-Waterproofing-membranes-for-external-above-ground-use-Design-and-installation1.pdf)
- **Clause 2.6.1 laps:**
  - bituminous sheet: side laps 70 mm, end laps 150 mm;
  - synthetic rubber: factory joints 40 mm, field side laps 50 mm, field end laps 100 mm;

- **Clause 2.12 changes in direction or upstands:** the system must accommodate differential horizontal shear movement between vertical and horizontal substrates. - **Clause 2.15.2 bonded or partially bonded systems:**
  - "Where the topping or bedding mortar is to be bonded to the membrane, sufficient movement joints shall be provided…"

  - "Where the topping or bedding mortar is structurally sufficient not to require bonding to the substrate, a double slip sheet shall be laid over the membrane to separate it from the screed."
  - Notes: "1 A structural unbonded screed should be of a minimum thickness of 50 mm with lightweight mesh reinforcing. 2 In some instances, additional movement joints may be required…"

- **NCC 2025 Vol One:**
  - F1D7(2): "On concrete surfaces, the membrane must be installed directly on a structural substrate complying with F1D5".
  - F1D4 note: a tile bed, screed or topping is not a structural substrate.
  - F1D6: exposed joints on the ridge/high point, protected per AS 4654.2 Section 2.9, and not beneath planters.
  - Source: [NCC 2025 Vol One Part F1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f1-water-management)
- **QBCC (11 Aug 2025)** summarises the NCC change as "Waterproofing membranes must now be applied directly to the structural substrate, not on top of screeds or toppings" — [QBCC article](https://www.qbcc.qld.gov.au/resources/article/waterproofing-done-right-what-you-need-know-2025)
- **Nexis CPD** says falls on concrete balconies should be formed in the slab, and "the membrane surface needs its own fall to the outlet". It contrasts with AS 4654.2-2012 Cl 2.5.2, which allows the fall in a screed over the substrate — [Nexis CPD](https://nexiscpd.com.au/external-waterproofing-roofs-decks-planters/)

### Inferences
- **Build-up options at a level threshold:**
  - **(i) Membrane directly on a sloped, set-down slab** (required by NCC 2025 for Class 2–9 concrete).
    - Build-up above the membrane is either a bonded tile bed or screed (movement joints about 4.5 m), or an unbonded mesh-reinforced screed of at least 50 mm over a double slip sheet. Then tile.
    - The slab set-down must hold the bedding/screed plus tile, plus the channel at the door.
  - **(ii) Membrane on top of a falls screed** (allowed by AS 4654.2-2012, and for houses under NCC 2022/Vol Two, subject to NCC checks).
    - Build-up: tile adhesive and tile directly on the membrane.
    - This is the thinnest option, but the tile surface is the only drainage plane, and the 2025 NCC disallows it for Volume One concrete.
  - **(iii) Pedestal or open-jointed pavers over a drainage cell/protection board on the membrane.**
    - Consistent with the drainage cell definition and the variable-level inlet notes.
    - Water drains at membrane level, so a level finished surface is easier while the membrane stays lower. AS 4654.2 does not specifically detail pedestals.
- **The 50 mm unbonded-screed note** is a key depth driver if an unbonded system is chosen. Together with tile and bed (around 10–20 mm), it suggests about 60–70+ mm above the membrane before falls are added. That broadly matches NCC 2025's 70 mm structural step-down. This is my inference; no source states it.

### Gaps
- AS 4654.2-2012 gives no figure for pedestal systems, drainage mats under tiles, or linear-drain integration beyond Fig 2.9.
- Manufacturer-specific build-up thicknesses were not researched (e.g. ARDEX, Mapei, Davco/Sika, Tremco, Wet Seal, Gripset).

## 6. AS 4654.1-2012 (Materials): what bears on balcony and threshold design

### Takeaway
- AS 4654.2 Clauses 1.6, 2.3 and 2.4 require all materials to comply with AS 4654.1, and require the system to be designed as Class I, II or III movement under AS 4654.1.
- **Classes by elongation at break (AS 1145.3):**
  - Class I: below 60%;
  - Class II: 60–299%;
  - Class III: 300% or more.
- Products are tested for cyclic movement (Appendix B) and durability (Appendix A), covering water immersion, detergent and heat ageing, and are designated exposed or non-exposed.

### Cited Findings
- **AS 4654.2 Cl 1.6:** "Materials used in the design and installation… shall comply with AS 4654.1… Each membrane shall pass the appropriate test of the service conditions to which it will be exposed."
- **AS 4654.2 Cl 2.3:** design and installation "shall be Class I, Class II or Class III movement in accordance with AS 4654.1".
- **AS 4654.2 Cl 2.4.2:** service conditions are UV (where exposed), heat ageing, operating temperature (the note says Australian membranes may see −15 °C to +85 °C), bioresistance, water immersion and chemical resistance.
- Source for all three: [AS 4654.2-2012 text](https://sarah.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/4654.2-AS-2012-Waterproofing-membranes-for-external-above-ground-use-Design-and-installation1.pdf)
- **AS 4654.1 classes as quoted in an accredited test report:**
  - "Requirement for Class III (high extensibility): ≥300% elongation at break; … Class II (medium extensibility) 60-299%…; Class I (low extensibility) <60%".
  - The tests listed are: bond strength (ASTM C794), acceptance of cyclic movement (AS 4654.1 Appendix B, "CSIRO moving joint test"), durability (Appendix A, Tables A1/A4: control, water immersion, detergent immersion and heat ageing elongation), and water vapour transmission (ASTM E96).
  - Source: [XTec Gen test report for Fosroc Nitoproof 410 (issued 07/01/2025)](https://www.fosroc.com.au/sites/default/files/resources_file_storage/Fosroc_Nitoproof_410_AS4654.1.pdf)
- **Store status:** the Standards Australia store listing for AS 4654.1-2012 shows it as pending revision (search snippet) — [Standards store AS 4654.1-2012](https://store.standards.org.au/product/as-4654-1-2012); [Nexis CPD](https://nexiscpd.com.au/external-waterproofing-roofs-decks-planters/)

### Inferences
- **Specification point.** For a level-threshold balcony, the membrane under tiles will usually be a non-exposed (protected) system. The movement class should suit differential movement at the slab-to-door-frame/sill junction and over the channel recess. AS 4654.2 Cl 2.12 bond-breaker and shear requirements apply at the channel upturns.

### Gaps
- The AS 4654.1 text itself was not read; class thresholds come from a test report quoting it.
- It is unknown whether the AS 4654.1 content is being folded into **DR AS/NZS 4858:2026** (see Q8).

### Inferences
- **The 4.5 m spacing appears in both standards.** AS 3958 (tiling) and AS 4654.2 (bonded screed) both point to about 4.5 m movement-joint spacing for external tiled balconies. On a typical NSW apartment balcony under 4.5 m, perimeter joints (including at the door channel and frame) may be the only ones needed. This should be confirmed against AS 3958:2023 clause numbering.
- **AS 3740 at the door.** AS 3740 is relevant only on the internal side of the door, if the internal room is a wet area. It has no bearing on the external threshold detail.

## 8. Revision status: is AS 4654.2 being revised, and what changes are proposed for thresholds?

### Takeaway
- AS 4654.2-2012 (second edition; first published 2009; prepared by former committee BD-013) is still the current edition, listed as "pending revision". No amendment has been issued.
- Secondary sources report that **DR AS 4654.2:2026**, a redline draft under committee **BD-038**, was open for public comment **10 September – 12 November 2026**, so comment has now closed.
- The Standards Australia comment portal (checked 25 Nov 2026) separately lists **DR AS/NZS 4858:2026 "Waterproofing membranes for wet areas and external above-ground use"** (BD-038), with comment closing **30 Nov 2026**. That looks like a combined materials standard that would cover what AS 4654.1 covers.
- **No public source describes the draft's threshold provisions.**

- **What the drafts may mean:**
