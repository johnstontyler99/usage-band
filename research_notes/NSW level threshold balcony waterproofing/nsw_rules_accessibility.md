# NSW rules, regulator guidance and accessibility requirements for balcony waterproofing at level (flush) thresholds (current to 25 November 2026)

Notes compiled 25 Nov 2026. Each item is labelled LAW (Act, Regulation, or the NCC as adopted), GUIDANCE (regulator publication, not binding on its own), ENFORCEMENT (an order or decision), or SECONDARY (consultant, law firm or industry commentary). legislation.nsw.gov.au and AustLII returned HTTP 403 to this researcher. ## Q1. What guidance have Building Commission NSW and NSW Fair Trading published on balcony and external waterproofing and on thresholds?

### Takeaway
Building Commission NSW has no standalone "balcony waterproofing" or "level threshold" guide or code of practice. Its main technical publication is the **Building Defects Library (v1.1, June 2025)** for Class 2, 3 and 9c buildings. The Library treats balcony waterproofing as an NCC F1D5 / AS 4654.2 matter and lists these defects: no puddle flange termination, no overflow, ponding or inadequate falls, and inadequate membrane termination at sliding doors. On upturn height at doors it says AS 4654.2 sets no fixed height and that **"the height of membrane termination should be determined by the façade engineer."** Under AS 4654.2 cl 2.8.3(b), where floor levels do not allow an upturn (the level-threshold case), the membrane must be fixed under the sill and terminate in the stormwater system.

### Inferences
- For a level-threshold balcony in NSW, the regulator's own reference point is AS 4654.2 cl 2.8.3(b): under-sill fixing and termination into the stormwater system. Because the Commission says upturn height "should be determined by the façade engineer", a builder should expect a certifier or Building Commission auditor to look for an engineered, documented termination detail at the door (sub-sill tray, termination into a grated channel or drain, overflow) rather than reliance on a nominal step.
- The Yattenden order shows the Commission treating missing or undersized overflows, inadequate falls and lack of drainage "at the door" as RAB Act *serious defects*, which can be enforced by rectification orders. A flush threshold with no drainage at the door line is the kind of detail that draws an order.
- NCC D3D16(d) expressly allows a sill up to 190 mm onto an external balcony. The NCC therefore does not itself drive level thresholds in Class 2 private units. The drivers are accessibility policy, client and market demand, and council DCPs (see Q5–Q6).

## Q2. Does the Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020 / Regulation 2021 treat waterproofing as a building element, and what must a regulated design for a level-threshold balcony show?

### Takeaway
Yes (LAW). "Waterproofing" is a **building element** under s 6(1)(b) of the DBP Act. Balcony waterproofing in a Class 2 building (and in Class 3 and 9c from 3 July 2023) needs a **regulated design** prepared and declared by a registered design practitioner, lodged on the NSW Planning Portal. A registered building practitioner then lodges a building compliance declaration before the OC. The regulator's minimum content for external waterproofing (balconies, roofs, planters) is set out in the Design Practitioners' Handbook (July 2023). Plans must show the membrane areas, balcony gradients and floor waste/overflow locations. Sections and details must show membrane extent, **termination of the membrane** and overflow details, at 1:50 general scale and 1:20, 1:10 or 1:5 for details. Door sill heights and flashings fall under the separate "building enclosure" design category.

A "regulated design" is "a design, including a plan, specification or a report detailing a design, that is prepared for a building element, or for a performance solution, for building work." — [Design Practitioners' Handbook, July 2023 ed., Better Regulation Division, Dept of Customer Service (PDF)](https://nswdfsi-web.squiz.cloud/__data/assets/pdf_file/0010/1120501/Design-practitioners-handbook-final.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a Class 2 balcony with a level threshold, the regulated design should at minimum comprise:
  - membrane extent and type;
  - balcony falls (in the substrate, no flatter than 1:100 under AS 4654.2);
  - drain or grated channel locations;
  - overflow location and sizing;
  - a large-scale (1:5 or 1:10) section through the door sill showing the under-sill or sub-sill tray, its termination into the stormwater system (AS 4654.2 cl 2.8.3), and the structural slab set-down or rebate that produces a flush finished level.

  The building-enclosure design should cover the door frame, flashings and sill heights. The structural/architectural setout should show the slab set-down and falls. Coordinating three regulated designs (waterproofing W, building enclosure B, load-bearing L) is the main documentation risk at a flush threshold.
- Because the Commission says upturn height "should be determined by the façade engineer", a façade-class or architectural practitioner should state the termination height or the under-sill alternative in the declared design. Otherwise the builder may carry the design risk under the s 37 duty of care (see Q3).

## Q3. How does the Residential Apartment Buildings (Compliance and Enforcement Powers) Act 2020 apply to balcony waterproofing defects, and what other remedies are in play?

### Takeaway
The RAB Act lets Building Commission NSW audit Class 2 (and, since July 2023, Class 3/9c) buildings, issue stop-work, rectification and prohibition orders (which block the OC), and treat waterproofing failures as **"serious defects"**. Balcony overflow, falls and door-drainage defects have been the subject of published orders. Separately, the DBP Act s 37 statutory duty of care, as interpreted in *Pafburn* (HCA 2024), is non-delegable and cannot be apportioned. From **1 December 2026**, new apartment buildings with more than two SOUs no longer need Home Building Compensation insurance and move to a strata building bond or decennial liability insurance.

## Q4. What do the Home Building Act 1989 and the EP&A certification regulations require on licensing, statutory warranties and mandatory inspections, and what 2024–2026 reforms affect waterproofing?

### Takeaway
- **Licensing:** residential waterproofing is "trade work" needing a contractor licence (waterproofing class) where the labour and materials exceed **$5,000 incl. GST**. Fair Trading's own list says it is **not** "specialist work", which would need a licence at any value; some training providers claim otherwise.
- **Warranties:** waterproofing is a statutory **"major element"**, but a waterproofing defect is a 6-year "major defect" only if it meets the s 18E consequences test. Otherwise the warranty period is 2 years.
- **Inspections:** the mandatory critical stage inspection (Development Certification and Fire Safety Reg 2021, s 61) covers waterproofing **"in a wet area"** only: every Class 1 building, and at least 10% of wet-area rooms in Class 2/3/4. **No mandatory critical stage inspection covers external or balcony waterproofing.**
- **Reforms:** the Building (Approvals and Practitioners) Act 2026 (assented 14 Aug 2026) will repeal the DBP Act and the certification regulation once proclaimed. It does **not** replace the Home Building Act.

### Inferences
- Because s 61 inspections cover only wet-area waterproofing, a balcony membrane at a level threshold can be covered by screed and tiles **without any mandatory certifier inspection**, for both Class 1 and Class 2. Builders should build in their own hold point: an independent or façade-engineer inspection, flood test, photos, and the applicator's certificate to AS 4654.2. For Class 2, the regulated design and building compliance declaration effectively require that evidence before OC.

## Q5. Which accessibility rules drive level thresholds in NSW (ADG / Housing SEPP, LHDG, AS 4299, AS 1428.1, NCC LHDS status, Premises Standards and NCC Part D4), and what are the exact numeric limits?

### Takeaway
No NSW statute requires a level threshold to a Class 2 private balcony.
- NSW has **not adopted** the NCC 2022 Livable Housing Design Standard: Part H8 for houses and Part G7 for Class 2 are blank for NSW.
- ADG Objective 4Q-1 (20% of apartments to LHDG Silver) is **design guidance**, and in any case LHDG Silver governs the *dwelling entrance* and *internal entry-level doorways*, not balcony doors.
- The real drivers are council DCP **adaptable housing (AS 4299)** controls, typically 10–25% of units, which require accessible private open space, plus AS 1428.1 threshold limits applied by access consultants.

Key numbers: AS 1428.1 abutting surfaces max **3 mm** vertical, or **5 mm** if bevelled/rounded. Threshold ramp max rise **35 mm**, max length **280 mm**, gradient **1:8**. Step ramp max rise **190 mm**, gradient **1:10**, max length **1900 mm**. LHDG Silver entrance **5 mm** max, with a ramped threshold allowed where the step is **>5 mm and <56 mm**. NCC D3D16(d) allows a sill up to **190 mm** onto an external balcony where Part D4 accessible entry does not apply.

### Inferences
- For a NSW Class 2 project, the legally binding accessibility drivers for a flush balcony threshold are usually **council DCP adaptable housing controls**. These apply AS 4299 Class C to a percentage of units, AS 4299 cl 3.5 requires "easy access" to private open space, and access consultants translate that into AS 1428.1 limits: about 3–5 mm level transition, or a threshold ramp up to 35 mm at 1:8 within 280 mm. ADG 4Q-1 is persuasive guidance. LHDG Silver does not itself cover balcony doors.
- The practical design envelope for an "accessible" balcony threshold is therefore a finished-level difference of 0–5 mm (bevelled), or up to 35 mm with a 1:8 threshold ramp, plus a balcony fall of about 1:40–1:100 away from the door. This collides with traditional step-down and membrane upturn practice, so the AS 4654.2 cl 2.8.3(b) under-sill and stormwater-termination route, with a grated channel at the door, becomes the default waterproofing detail.
- For common-area terraces and podiums in Class 2, NCC D4 / AS 1428.1 can apply as LAW, so level or ramped thresholds to common outdoor areas are mandatory rather than optional.

- I found no NSW Government announcement (to Nov 2026) reversing the LHDS opt-out, though the Parliament answer says the Government is monitoring.
- The Premises Standards s 2.1 scope and its application to Class 2 common areas are unverified from the primary source.

## Q6. Which NSW council DCPs require flush or level balcony access, and what have NCAT and the NSW courts decided on balcony waterproofing and thresholds?

### Takeaway
Council DCPs typically require a share of units (around 10–25%) to be adaptable to AS 4299, and access reports for those DAs commit to low-level balcony thresholds, for example a 10 mm step-down with a threshold ramp and 1:40 fall (Lane Cove) or a maximum 35 mm step-down (Sydney SSD project). No DCP clause found expressly mandates a "flush" balcony threshold. No NSW judgment found turns specifically on a "missing step-down at a level threshold". The relevant decisions concern balcony waterproofing generally:
- *Ashton v Stevenson* (NCAT Appeal Panel, 2019): a balcony leak was a waterproofing *element* but not proven to be a *major defect*.
- *The Owners – SP 92226 v 1A Eden Pty Ltd* [2026] NSWSC 563: no barrier under balcony doors and inadequate overflows; $2.5 m judgment, but limited recovery.
- *The Owners – SP 99960 v SPS Building Contractors* [2024] NSWSC 687: balcony waterproofing and sliding doors; disputed defects not systemic.

### Inferences
- 1A Eden ("no barrier under the balcony doors") is the closest NSW authority to a level-threshold failure. It shows the remedy risk shifting to owners when the developer is insolvent, which underlines why the RAB Act, the bond and decennial liability insurance exist.
- In NCAT/HBA claims, a leaking flush threshold will not automatically attract the 6-year major-defect period. Claimants need evidence of uninhabitability or similar consequences (*Ashton v Stevenson*). Builders' exposure under the 2-year period is still real.
- Councils, through their DCPs, are the main *legal* source of "level" balcony access in NSW. Access reports lodged with DAs become consent documents, so a stated "10 mm step-down" or "max 35 mm" becomes a consent requirement the builder must meet and the waterproofing design must accommodate.

### Gaps
- No current (2024–2026) council DCP clause was found that expressly mandates a *flush* balcony threshold. The examples are AS 4299 percentage controls plus access-report commitments.
- Full citations and judgment texts were not verified for *Ashton v Stevenson* (NSWCATAP citation not given in the source) or *1A Eden* [2026] NSWSC 563.
- I found no NSW decision specifically on whether omitting a step-down at a balcony door is itself non-compliant where an under-sill or grated-drain detail was used.
