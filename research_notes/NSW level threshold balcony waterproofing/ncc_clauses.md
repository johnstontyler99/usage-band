## Q1. Which NCC edition is in force in NSW as of November 2026? Adoption dates, transition, and NSW variations relevant to waterproofing and balconies

### Takeaway
As of November 2026, **NCC 2022 (as amended) is still the edition in force in NSW.** NSW has deferred NCC 2025 to **1 May 2027**, even though NCC 2025 was published nationally on 1 May 2026.
- **Waterproofing variation:** NSW says it will let owners doing remedial work on existing apartment buildings choose either the NCC 2022 or the NCC 2025 waterproofing rules. I could not find that variation in the published NSW Schedule 5 of NCC 2025.
- **Other NSW variations:** NSW has no variation to the external-waterproofing clauses themselves (Vol One F1 / Vol Two H2D8). The NSW variations that matter here are NSW H2P3 (rising damp, Class 1) and the opt-out from Livable Housing Design (Part H8 / G7).

### Inferences
- **New work in NSW now:** For a construction certificate or CDC for new work in November 2026, the applicable DTS rules are NCC 2022:
  - Class 2–9: Vol One F1D2/F1D4/F1D5 and F3.
  - Class 1 and 10: Vol Two H2P2/H2D8.
- **Effect on level thresholds:** The stricter NCC 2025 Class 2 balcony set-down rules (F1D4, see Q2) are **not yet mandatory in NSW**. They will become mandatory for applications under NCC 2025 from 1 May 2027.
- **The remedial-works choice:** Because it applies only to remedial work on existing apartment buildings, it does not cover new level-threshold balconies.
- **NCC 2025 as evidence now:** A designer could still use the NCC 2025 F1 DTS provisions as supporting evidence in a Performance Solution under NCC 2022. That is a design choice, not a requirement.

## Q2. NCC Volume One (Class 2–9): which Performance Requirements and DTS provisions cover external waterproofing, what they say, and what NCC 2025 changed

### Takeaway
**NCC 2022 (in force in NSW):**
- F1D5 requires a membrane to AS 4654.1/AS 4654.2 on any "roof, balcony, podium or similar horizontal surface part of a building".
- F1D4 requires exposed joints to be protected per AS 4654.2 Section 2.9.
- F1D2 exempts timber or perforated decking and surfaces directly above ground.
- The NCC 2022 text sets **no fall, step-down, hob or overflow figures**; these come only from AS 4654.2.
- The weatherproofing Performance Requirement is F3P1, and AS 2047 glazing is required under F3D4.

**NCC 2025 (in NSW from 1 May 2027):**
- Parts F1 and F3 merge into "Part F1 Water management", with a single F1P1.
- New DTS F1D4 requires, for concrete roofs, balconies and podiums:
  - a **1:80 fall in the structural substrate**;
  - **overflow** with its invert ≥20 mm below the internal floor structural substrate;
  - a **minimum 70 mm structural step-down** from the internal floor substrate;
  - a **70 mm monolithic perimeter hob**.

### Cited Findings
**NCC 2022 Volume One (PRIMARY):** [NCC 2022 Vol One Part F1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f1-surface-water-management-rising-damp-and-external-waterproofing)
- **Part title:** "Part F1 Surface water management, rising damp and external waterproofing".
- **Performance Requirements:**
  - F1P1 Managing rainwater impact on adjoining properties.
  - F1P2 Preventing rainwater from entering buildings: "Surface water, resulting from a storm having an annual exceedance probability of 1%, must not enter the building." Exemptions: some Class 7/8 buildings; a garage, tool shed or sanitary compartment forming part of a building used for other purposes; open spectator stands and open-deck carparks.
  - F1P3 Rainwater drainage systems.
  - F1P4 Rising damp.
- **F1D1:** "Where a Deemed-to-Satisfy Solution is proposed, Performance Requirements F1P1 to F1P4 are satisfied by complying with F1D2 to F1D8." Under (2), a Performance Solution must determine the relevant Performance Requirements per A2G2(3) and A2G4(3).
- **F1D2 Application of Part ("New for 2022"):**
  - "(1) F1D4 and F1D5 do not apply to a roof with a covering complying with F3D2(a) to (d)."
  - "(2) F1D3 to F1D5 do not apply to a balcony, podium or similar horizontal surface part of a building— where the flooring is of timber decking or other perforated flooring; or which is located directly above ground."
- **F1D3 Stormwater drainage:** "Stormwater drainage must be designed and constructed in accordance with AS/NZS 3500.3."
- **F1D4 Exposed joints ("New for 2022"):**
  - Clause: "Exposed joints in the drainage surface on a roof, balcony, podium or similar horizontal surface part of a building must— be protected in accordance with Section 2.9 of AS 4654.2; and not be located beneath or run through a planter box, water feature or similar part of the building."
  - Note: an exposed joint "is a construction joint, control joint, expansion joint, contraction joint or movement joint and includes an exposed joint which is directly below a drainage surface."
  - Explanatory information: joints should be at "a ridge or high point of the structural substrate, where possible". Where movement exceeds about 10 mm, consider "a hob with a minimum height of 50 mm formed within the structural substrate for the full length of both sides of the exposed joint" with a discontinuous membrane per AS 4654.2 Section 2.9.
- **F1D5 External waterproofing membranes (2019 ref: F1.4):** "A roof, balcony, podium or similar horizontal surface part of a building must be provided with a waterproofing membrane— consisting of materials complying with AS 4654.1; and designed and installed in accordance with AS 4654.2."
- **No numbers in the NCC 2022 text:** The F1 text (including the guide and explanatory material on the page) gives **no figure for falls (1:100 or 1:80), overflow, step-down or termination height**. All of these sit inside AS 4654.2. There is also **no 50 mm step-down exclusion in Vol One**, unlike Vol Two H2D8.

**NCC 2022 Volume One Part F3 Roof and wall cladding (PRIMARY):** [NCC 2022 Vol One Part F3](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f3-roof-and-wall-cladding)
- **F3P1 Weatherproofing:** "A roof and external wall (including openings around windows and doors) must prevent the penetration of water that could cause— unhealthy or dangerous conditions, or loss of amenity for occupants; and undue dampness or deterioration of building elements." Limitations: some Class 7/8 buildings; garages and the like; open spectator stands and open-deck carparks.
- **F3V1 Weatherproofing (Verification Method):** Covers **external walls** only. It is verified when a prototype passes the test and the wall "has a risk score of 20 or less" (Table F3V1a), "is not subjected to an ultimate limit state wind pressure of more than 2.5 kPa; and includes only windows that comply with AS 2047". The guide says F3V1 "is not a mandatory component of the NCC".
- **F3D2 Roof coverings:** Clause (e) allows "an external waterproofing membrane complying with F1D5".
- **F3D4 Glazed assemblies:** "the following glazed assemblies in an external wall, must comply with AS 2047 requirements for resistance to water penetration: Windows. Sliding and swinging glazed doors with a frame, including French and bi-fold doors with a frame. Adjustable louvres. Shopfronts. Window walls with one piece framing." Exclusions include frameless sliding/swinging doors, site-built and one-off untested windows, and second-hand and heritage windows.

**NCC 2025 Volume One, Part F1 Water management (PRIMARY):** [NCC 2025 Vol One Part F1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f1-water-management)
- **F1P1 Managing rainwater impact on the building and adjoining properties:**
  - (1) Water "must be redirected to a drainage system to prevent— unhealthy or unsafe conditions, or loss of amenity for occupants within the building; and undue damage to internal surfaces and other building elements; and undue damage or nuisance to other buildings and any other property".
  - (2) covers 5% AEP and (3) covers 1% AEP rain events.
  - (4) "Water resulting from a rain event in (2) and (3), subject to wind action with an annual exceedance probability of 4%".
  - Note: "building elements include external walls, openings around windows and external doors, roofs, balconies, podiums, attached awnings, similar horizontal surface parts of a building and stormwater overflow systems".
- **F1V1 Weatherproofing:** Retained for external walls, with a risk score ≤20 and "includes only windows that comply with AS 2047". The 2.5 kPa ULS limit did not appear in the 2025 text I extracted.
- **F1D1:** F1P1 "is satisfied by complying with F1D2 to F1D15".
- **F1D2 Application of Part:**
  - "(1) F1D4 to F1D7 and F1D10 do not apply to a roof with a covering complying with F1D12(a) to (d)."
  - "(2) F1D3 to F1D7 and F1D10 do not apply to— timber decking or other perforated flooring to a balcony, podium or similar horizontal surface part of a building; or a balcony, podium or similar horizontal surface part of a building which is located directly above ground."
  - "(3) ... do not apply to horizontal or near horizontal surfaces created by window hoods, spandrels, tops of parapets and other features."
  - (4): "a horizontal element includes reference to a near horizontal element."
- **F1D4 Provision of drainage and grading to external areas (key new clause):**
  - Clause: "A concrete roof, balcony, podium or similar part of a building must have— (a) a drainage system complying with F1D3 that incorporates sufficient overflow provision: and (b) its structural substrate graded with a minimum fall of 1:80 in the plane of the substrate to suit the drainage system required by (a); and (c) a minimum 70 mm step down from the surface level of the internal floor structural substrate to the adjacent surface level of the external structural substrate, where applicable; and (d) a monolithic hob that is integral with the structural substrate, at least 70 mm high, installed at the outer perimeter, except where the perimeter of the roof, balcony, podium or similar part of the building drains directly to a gutter."
  - Notes:
    - "The invert level of overflow provision referred to in F1D4(a) must be installed not less than 20 mm below any adjacent internal floor structural substrate."
    - "a tile bed, screed, topping or similar component is not considered a structural substrate except within planter boxes".
    - "F1D4(d) does not prevent penetration of a hob for the provision of overflow."
    - "The minimum 70 mm step down reference in F1D4(c) is not intended to refer to the minimum waterproofing termination height specified in AS 4654.2. Where AS 4654.2 requires a termination height greater than 70 mm, the waterproof membrane termination can be extended by suitable means above the 70 mm concrete set down."
  - Explanatory information: "F1D7(1)(b), by reference to AS 4654.2, may require in some circumstances a step down greater than 70 mm. Similarly, other provisions may also specify gradient requirements. For example, D4D2 may prevent gradients steeper than 1:40."
- **F1D5 Substrate materials:** Concrete roofs, balconies and podiums "must comply with AS 3600", and the substrate surface must be prepared per F1D7(1)(b).
- **F1D6 Exposed joints:** Joints must now "be located on the ridge line or high point of the structural substrate". In 2022 this was explanatory only. They must also be protected per AS 4654.2 Section 2.9, and must not sit under planters or water features.
- **F1D7 External waterproofing membranes:** "(1) A roof, balcony, podium or similar horizontal surface part of a building must be provided with a waterproofing membrane— consisting of materials complying with AS 4654.1; and designed and installed in accordance with AS 4654.2. (2) Where a membrane required by (1) is applied to a concrete roof, balcony, podium or similar horizontal surface, the membrane must be installed directly on a structural substrate complying with F1D5."
- **F1D10 Surface finishes (new):** "the flooring or surface finish of a roof, balcony, terrace, podium, or similar part of a building must be— self-draining; or directly fixed to a membrane complying with F1D7." Limitation: (a) does not apply to vehicular areas or ballast.
- **F1D14 Glazed assemblies:** This is the former F3D4 AS 2047 water-penetration requirement, now relocated into F1 with the same list of assemblies and exclusions.

**ABCB explanation of the change (PRIMARY):** [ABCB – NCC 2025 Water management changes](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/ncc-2025/ncc-2025-key-changes/ncc-2025-water-management-changes)
- "Part F1 and Part F3 have been merged into a single Part F1. Performance Requirements F3P1 and F1P1 to F1P4 have been consolidated and replaced with a single Performance Requirement, F1P1."
- Limitations for Class 7 and 8 were removed, and "A new provision for 10‑year deflection has been introduced" in Part B1.
- Rationale: "most water-related defects result from sub-surface water ... beneath finished surfaces of building elements such as balconies, podiums, concrete roofs ... Sub-surface water often passes through tiled systems, plain concrete, pavers, screeds or degraded membranes."

**ABCB consultation (PRIMARY):** The Consultation RIS estimated a net benefit of about $1 billion for Class 2 (BCR 4.3) and $2.7 billion for Classes 3–9 (BCR 5.2). — [ABCB – Enhanced waterproofing](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/enhanced-waterproofing)

### Inferences
- **NCC 2022 (current in NSW) and level thresholds:** Nothing in the Vol One text bans a level threshold. The only constraint is "designed and installed in accordance with AS 4654.2" (F1D5), so the level-threshold question turns entirely on what AS 4654.2 allows at door openings, for example termination heights, sub-sill flashings and drainage at the door. If the design departs from AS 4654.2, it needs a Performance Solution against F1P2/F3P1.
- **NCC 2025 (from 1 May 2027) and level thresholds:** The 70 mm step-down is measured **between structural substrates**, not finished surfaces. Screeds and tile beds are expressly "not considered a structural substrate".
  - So a level **finished** threshold is still compatible with DTS if the external concrete slab is set down ≥70 mm at the door. With 1:80 falls away from the door, the set-down increases further out.
  - The finish is then built back up to the internal finished floor level, for example with pedestal pavers, which qualify as "self-draining" under F1D10(a).
  - What NCC 2025 removes is the common practice of a **level structural slab** with screed falls and no structural set-down.
  - The note on F1D4 also says AS 4654.2 may demand more than 70 mm.
- **Meaning of "where applicable" in F1D4(c):** Most plausibly it limits the step-down to surfaces that adjoin an internal floor. This is my reading; it is not stated in the text.
- **Coverage of F1D4:** It applies only to **concrete** roofs, balconies and podiums. Framed or lightweight membrane balconies in Class 2 still need F1D7 and F1D10 but not the F1D4 set-down and hob.
- **Directly above ground:** A ground-floor terrace "located directly above ground" is outside F1D3–F1D7 and F1D10 under both editions, but F1P1/F3P1 performance still applies.

### Gaps
- **AS 4654.2 itself:** I did not read its clause text (paywalled). That covers its own falls requirement (secondary sources say ≥1:100 to drains), Section 2.8 door and window terminations, and Appendix A termination heights. These need verification from the Standard itself.
- **NCC 2025 Guide commentary:** I did not retrieve the ABCB Guide commentary on F1D4/F1D10 for NCC 2025 (for example, on what "directly fixed to a membrane" means for mortar-bed tiles).
- **Thresholds clause in NCC 2025:** I did not verify the NCC 2025 clause number or wording for D3 Thresholds. The 2022 D3D16 text is in Q4.

## Q3. NCC Volume Two / ABCB Housing Provisions (Class 1 and Class 10): which clauses cover external waterproofing of balconies and roof decks over habitable rooms, any Housing Provisions practice for balconies, decks or door sills, and NCC 2025 changes

### Takeaway
**For Class 1:** H2P2 (weatherproofing) is the Performance Requirement and H2D8 (external waterproofing) is the DTS.
- H2D8 calls up AS 4654.1/AS 4654.2 for flat roofs, roof terraces, balconies and similar surfaces "located above internal spaces".
- It **excludes** a concrete slab with a **minimum 50 mm step-down below internal floor level** and spaced timber decking.
- **H2D8 is word-for-word identical in NCC 2025.**
- The Housing Provisions contain no balcony-membrane or door-sill waterproofing recipe. They contain window installation rules (Part 8.2) and waling-plate and flashing rules for decks and balconies attached to walls (Part 12.3).

**For Class 10:** H2P2 applies only where the Class 10 contributes to the weatherproofing of the Class 1 building.

### Cited Findings
- **H2P2 Weatherproofing (NCC 2022, PRIMARY):** "A roof and external wall (including openings around windows and doors) must prevent the penetration of water that could cause— (a) unhealthy or dangerous conditions, or loss of amenity for occupants; and (b) undue dampness or deterioration of building elements." Limitation: "H2P2(a) does not apply to a Class 10 building except where its construction contributes to the weatherproofing of the Class 1 building." — [NCC 2022 Vol Two Part H2](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-two/h-class-1-and-10-buildings/part-h2-damp-and-weatherproofing)
- **H2D8 External waterproofing (NCC 2022; 2019 ref 3.8.1.3; PRIMARY):**
  - "(1) Performance Requirement H2P2 is satisfied for the design and construction of external waterproofing for roofing systems on flat roofs, roof terraces, balconies and terraces and other similar horizontal surfaces located above internal spaces of a building provided— (a) membranes used in the external waterproofing system comply with AS 4654.1; and (b) the design and installation of the external waterproofing system is in accordance with AS 4654.2."
  - "(2) The requirements of (1) apply to— (a) roofing systems other than those complying with H1D7(2) and (3); and (b) terraces, balconies and the like other than— (i) a concrete slab that has a minimum step-down of 50 mm below the internal floor level; or (ii) a suspended concrete slab— where the subfloor space is not used for habitable or non-habitable purposes; and that has a minimum step-down of 50 mm below the internal floor level; or (iii) spaced decking in conjunction with framing members that are suitable for external use."
  - Explanatory information: such spaces "can be susceptible to potential for water ingress", and AS 4654 provides "solutions for liquid and/or sheet membrane roofing systems on flat roofs, roof terraces, balconies and terraces located over habitable rooms ... A Performance Solution in accordance with A2G2 would need to be provided for other types of external waterproofing materials and designs."
  - [NCC 2022 Vol Two Part H2](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-two/h-class-1-and-10-buildings/part-h2-damp-and-weatherproofing)
- **NCC 2025 Vol Two H2D8 and H2P2 (PRIMARY):** The extracted text of H2D8 (1) and (2), its explanatory information, and H2P2 are word-for-word identical to NCC 2022. So **NCC 2025 did not change Class 1 external waterproofing**; the F1D4 step-down, hob and falls package is Volume One only. — [NCC 2025 Vol Two Part H2](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-two/h-class-1-and-10-buildings/part-h2-damp-and-weatherproofing)
- **H2V1 Weatherproofing (PRIMARY, summarised):** A verification method for **external walls** under H2P2, using the same procedure as Vol One F3V1. It requires a risk score ≤20, ULS wind ≤2.5 kPa, and only AS 2047 windows. Table H2V1a's risk factors include "decks/balconies". — [NCC 2022 Vol Two Part H2](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-two/h-class-1-and-10-buildings/part-h2-damp-and-weatherproofing)
- **H2D7 / H1D8 Glazing (PRIMARY):**
  - H2D7 says glazing complies with H2P2 through H1D8(1).
  - H1D8(1): windows and "sliding and swinging glazed doors with a frame, including French and bi-fold doors with a frame" must be "designed and constructed in accordance with AS 2047 ... and installed such that they comply with— AS 2047; and Part 8.2 of the ABCB Housing Provisions, provided that they are— in buildings that are within the geometric limits set out in clause 1.2 of AS 4055; and located in an area with a wind class of not more than N3."
  - [NCC 2022 Vol Two Part H1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-two/h-class-1-and-10-buildings/part-h1-structure)
- **Housing Provisions 2025 Part 8.2 Windows and external glazed doors (PRIMARY):**
  - 8.2.2 covers installation: no structural loads on the assembly; "A minimum 10 mm gap ... between the top of the window assembly and any load bearing framing element"; packing "clear of any flashing material".
  - Explanatory information: "Care must be taken to minimise any gaps between sills and external skins to prevent excessive ingress of water."
  - There are **no door-threshold set-down or membrane provisions**.
  - [ABCB Housing Provisions 2025 Part 8.2](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/housing-provisions/8-glazing/part-82-windows-and-external-glazed-doors)
- **Housing Provisions 2025 Part 12.3 Attachment of framed decks and balconies to external walls using a waling plate (PRIMARY):**
  - Waling plate size and fixing rules, with separate centres for "deck construction" and "tiled balcony construction" (for example, No. 14 screws at ≤450 mm for decks and ≤400 mm for tiled balconies into timber frames).
  - 12.3.3 Flashings: these "must be provided to bottom, tops and the sides of the junction of the waling plate and the external wall". Each flashing "extends not less than 150 mm beyond each side", laps are "not less than 75 mm in the direction of flow", and flashings are fixed "at least 25 mm under the cladding".
  - 12.3.4 sets bracing rules for decks more than 1 m above ground.
  - [ABCB Housing Provisions 2025 Part 12.3](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/housing-provisions/12-ancillary-provisions/part-123-attachment-framed-decks-and-balconies-external-walls-buildings-using)
- **Housing Provisions contents (PRIMARY):** There is a Part 10.2 "Wet area waterproofing" (internal wet areas), but no Housing Provisions Part for external above-ground waterproofing. H2D8 points straight to AS 4654. — [ABCB Housing Provisions 2025 (contents)](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/housing-provisions)
- **SECONDARY:** HIA says AS 4654.2 "provides Deemed-to-Satisfy external waterproofing requirements under F1D4 and F1D5 in Volume One and H2D8 Volume Two" (article dated 1 October 2024). — [HIA – Waterproofing membranes above ground Part 2](https://hia.com.au/resources-and-advice/building-it-right/australian-standards/articles/waterproofing-membranes-above-ground-part-2)

### Inferences
- **Level-threshold concrete balcony over a habitable room (Class 1):** It cannot use the H2D8(2)(b)(i)/(ii) exclusions, which need a ≥50 mm step-down. H2D8(1) therefore applies in full, so the design must follow AS 4654.2, including its door-opening termination details. Departures need a Performance Solution against H2P2.
- **Odd consequence of the wording:** Read literally, H2D8(2)(b)(i) means a concrete balcony with a 50 mm step-down is not required by DTS to have an AS 4654 membrane. A level one is required to. The step-down is treated as an alternative protective measure. This reading is mine; check it with a certifier.
- **Balcony not above internal spaces** (for example, over open ground, a carport or an open undercroft): H2D8(1) does not apply at all. H2P2 still applies to the adjoining door opening ("openings around windows and doors"), and the door must comply with AS 2047 under H1D8.
- **Class 10 decks:** Open spaced timber decks are excluded from H2D8 by (2)(b)(iii). A Class 10 deck or roof deck that forms the roof over, or contributes to weatherproofing of, the Class 1 dwelling is caught by H2P2. Housing Provisions Part 12.3 covers the flashing of waling plates on attached decks and balconies.

### Gaps
- **ABCB Guide commentary:** I did not find any on why H2D8 uses 50 mm (Vol Two) when NCC 2025 Vol One uses 70 mm.
- **Earlier Class 1 practice clauses:** I did not check whether the Housing Provisions (2022 or 2025) contain any further balcony or roof-deck "acceptable construction practice" beyond Parts 8.2 and 12.3, such as the Part 7 roof cladding and gutter provisions. The contents list shows none specific to balcony membranes.

## Q4. Does any NCC clause mention door thresholds, step-downs, set-downs, hobs or level thresholds for balconies? If not, how is a level threshold dealt with?

### Takeaway
Yes, but only indirectly.
- **NCC 2022 H2D8 (Class 1):** uses "a minimum step-down of 50 mm below the internal floor level" as an **exclusion** from AS 4654 compliance.
- **NCC 2022 Vol One F1D4:** mentions hobs only in explanatory guidance for movement joints.
- **NCC 2025 Vol One F1D4 (Class 2–9):** imposes a mandatory **70 mm structural step-down** and a **70 mm perimeter hob** for concrete balconies.
- **NCC 2022 Vol One D3D16 Thresholds:** an egress provision that expressly lets a door sill be up to 190 mm above an external balcony surface.

No NCC clause provides a DTS "level threshold" detail. A level threshold is DTS-compliant only if it meets AS 4654.2 door-termination requirements (and, for NCC 2025 Class 2, the 70 mm structural set-down). Otherwise it must be justified as a Performance Solution under A2G2.

### Cited Findings
- **H2D8(2)(b) (PRIMARY, Class 1, 2022 and 2025):** Excludes "a concrete slab that has a minimum step-down of 50 mm below the internal floor level" and a suspended concrete slab with an unused subfloor that "has a minimum step-down of 50 mm below the internal floor level". — [NCC 2022 Vol Two Part H2](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-two/h-class-1-and-10-buildings/part-h2-damp-and-weatherproofing); [NCC 2025 Vol Two Part H2](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-two/h-class-1-and-10-buildings/part-h2-damp-and-weatherproofing)
- **NCC 2025 F1D4(c)/(d) (PRIMARY, Class 2–9):**
  - "a minimum 70 mm step down from the surface level of the internal floor structural substrate to the adjacent surface level of the external structural substrate, where applicable".
  - "a monolithic hob that is integral with the structural substrate, at least 70 mm high, installed at the outer perimeter".
  - The 70 mm figure "is not intended to refer to the minimum waterproofing termination height specified in AS 4654.2".
  - AS 4654.2 "may require in some circumstances a step down greater than 70 mm".
  - [NCC 2025 Vol One Part F1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f1-water-management)
- **NCC 2022 F1D4 explanatory (PRIMARY):** Suggests "a hob with a minimum height of 50 mm formed within the structural substrate" at exposed joints moving more than 10 mm. — [NCC 2022 Vol One Part F1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f1-surface-water-management-rising-damp-and-external-waterproofing)
- **NCC 2022 Vol One D3D16 Thresholds (PRIMARY):**
  - "(1) The threshold of a doorway must not incorporate a step or ramp at any point closer to the doorway than the width of the door leaf unless— ... (c) in a building required to be accessible by Part D4, the doorway— opens to a road or open space; and is provided with a threshold ramp or step ramp in accordance with AS 1428.1; or (d) in other cases— the doorway opens to a road or open space, external stair landing or external balcony; and the door sill is not more than 190 mm above the finished surface of the ground, balcony, or the like, to which the doorway opens."
  - A state replacement version on the same page (tagged NSW) adds a limb for Class 9b entertainment venues: sill "not more than 50 mm above the finished floor level". The variation tagging in the page markup was not fully clear.
  - [NCC 2022 Vol One Part D3](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-one/d-access-and-egress/part-d3-construction-exits)
- **NCC 2025 F1D4 explanatory (PRIMARY):** "D4D2 may prevent gradients steeper than 1:40". This shows the ABCB anticipated interaction with accessible-path requirements. — [NCC 2025 Vol One Part F1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f1-water-management)
- **WA Building Commission, IB 037/2014, 24 March 2014 (government guidance, non-NSW; SECONDARY for NSW purposes):** Interprets AS 4654.2-2012 as follows:
  - "Where doors and windows are fitted onto an external waterproof area, they require a sub sill flashing to be included as part of the membrane system and the external finished floor level must finish below the termination height of the membrane. These vertical upward termination heights are specified in Table A1 of AS 4654.2-2012."
  - "Where circumstances do not permit the inclusion of a set-down or hob (e.g. for wheelchair access), a gutter should be formed into the substrate immediately in front of the opening."
  - It also notes that the AS 4654.2 requirements "may be in addition to water resistance requirements ... set out in AS 2047".
  - [WA Building Commission IB 037 – Waterproofing of external above ground building elements](https://wa.gov.au/system/files/2024-10/ib_037_waterproofing_external_above_ground_building_elements.pdf)
- **Termination heights (SECONDARY, search-result excerpt of HIA material; not verified on the fetched page):** "Appendix A of AS 4654.2 specifies minimum termination heights of the waterproofing membrane depending on wind classification. This can vary from 40mm for N1 wind class to 180mm for N6." The fetched HIA page confirms only that Appendix A "provides the minimum vertical termination height of the membrane where the membrane is to prevent water entry". — [HIA – Waterproofing membranes above ground Part 2](https://hia.com.au/resources-and-advice/building-it-right/australian-standards/articles/waterproofing-membranes-above-ground-part-2)
- **Performance Solution pathways (PRIMARY):**
  - Under NSW A2G2 (NCC 2025 NSW replacement), a Performance Solution is achieved by showing "compliance with all relevant Performance Requirements; or the solution is at least equivalent to the Deemed-to-Satisfy Provisions".
  - Assessment methods: evidence of suitability (Part A5), a Verification Method, Expert Judgement, or comparison with the DTS Provisions.
  - The process steps: "Prepare a performance-based design brief in consultation with relevant stakeholders", analysis, evaluation against acceptance criteria, and a final report.
  - [NCC 2025 Vol One Sch 5 NSW A2](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/5-new-south-wales/nsw-a2-compliance-ncc)

### Inferences
- **Under NCC 2022 (current in NSW), DTS route:** A level threshold is DTS only if the AS 4654.2 detail at the door is met. In practice that means the membrane and sub-sill flashing terminate above the external finished surface by the Table A1 / Appendix A height, which implies the external finish sits lower than the sill. Alternatively, AS 4654.2 might provide another protected-opening detail; I could not verify this.
- **Usual level-threshold arrangements:** A grated channel or gutter in front of the door (as the WA IB suggests), a structural set-down with raised pedestal pavers, or a covered or protected balcony. All of these need either an AS 4654.2-compliant detail or a Performance Solution, typically Expert Judgement against F1P2/F3P1 (Class 2) or H2P2 (Class 1).
- **No Verification Method:** None exists for balcony membranes or thresholds. F3V1/H2V1/F1V1 cover external walls only, so balcony Performance Solutions rely on Expert Judgement, evidence of suitability, or comparison with DTS.
- **Under NCC 2025 (Class 2 from 1 May 2027):** A level **finished** threshold is still achievable, provided the **concrete structure** is set down ≥70 mm (more if AS 4654.2 demands), the overflow invert is ≥20 mm below the internal structural substrate, and the finish is self-draining or directly fixed to the membrane. A level threshold on a level structural slab, with screed-built falls and no set-down, would be a DTS departure needing a Performance Solution.
- **D3D16 and level thresholds:** The 190 mm sill allowance shows the egress DTS does not force a level threshold at balcony doors. Whether D3D16 applies to doors inside a Class 2 sole-occupancy unit depends on Part D3's application rules, which I did not verify.

### Gaps
- **AS 4654.2 itself:** I could not verify its actual text: Section 2.8 door and window opening details, the Table A1 values for each wind class (only the 40 mm N1 and 180 mm N6 endpoints come from a secondary excerpt), or any figure for a level or "accessible" threshold.
- **Regulator guidance:** I found no NSW-specific regulator guidance (NSW Building Commission or Fair Trading) on level-threshold balconies in this pass.
- **Part D3 in NCC 2025:** The application of D3D16 to doors within SOUs, and the NCC 2025 clause number and wording, were not confirmed.

## Q5. Do the NCC weatherproofing provisions (AS 2047 for windows and external glazed doors) affect a level-threshold door? What does AS 2047 require on water penetration pressures, sill heights and sill drainage?

### Takeaway
Yes.
- **Class 2–9:** Framed sliding, swinging, French and bi-fold glazed doors in external walls must meet AS 2047 water-penetration resistance (2022 F3D4; 2025 F1D14).
- **Class 1:** The same doors must be designed, constructed and installed to AS 2047 (H1D8).
- AS 2047 sets the water-penetration test pressure at 30% of the positive SLS wind pressure, with a 150 Pa minimum. AGWA's reproduction of AS 2047 Table 2.3 gives 150–600 Pa depending on wind rating and exposure.
- Low or level-sill doors must still pass these pressures, and AS 2047 is **additional to** the AS 4654.2 membrane termination at the sill.
- I could not verify any AS 2047 clause text on sill upstand height or sill drainage.

### Cited Findings
- **Vol One, NCC 2022 F3D4 / NCC 2025 F1D14 (PRIMARY):** "the following glazed assemblies in an external wall, must comply with AS 2047 requirements for resistance to water penetration: Windows. Sliding and swinging glazed doors with a frame, including French and bi-fold doors with a frame. Adjustable louvres. Shopfronts. Window walls with one piece framing." Excluded: "Sliding and swinging glazed doors without a frame", site-built or one-off untested windows, second-hand and heritage windows, and others. — [NCC 2022 Vol One Part F3](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f3-roof-and-wall-cladding); [NCC 2025 Vol One Part F1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f1-water-management)
- **Vol Two H1D8(1) (PRIMARY):** AS 2047 design, construction and installation, plus Housing Provisions Part 8.2 for buildings within AS 4055 geometric limits and wind class ≤N3. H2D7 links glazing to H2P2 through H1D8(1). — [NCC 2022 Vol Two Part H1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-two/h-class-1-and-10-buildings/part-h1-structure); [NCC 2022 Vol Two Part H2](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-two/h-class-1-and-10-buildings/part-h2-damp-and-weatherproofing)
- **Verification Methods (PRIMARY):** F3V1/H2V1 (2022) and F1V1 (2025) apply only where the wall "includes only windows that comply with AS 2047". — [NCC 2022 Vol One Part F3](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f3-roof-and-wall-cladding)
- **AS 2047-2014 Table 2.3 Water Penetration Resistance Test Pressures, as reproduced by AGWA (SECONDARY):**

  | Window rating | Non-exposed (Pa) | Exposed (Pa) |
  |---|---|---|
  | N1, N2 | 150 | 200 |
  | N3, C1 | 150 | 300 |
  | N4, C2 | 200 | 300 |
  | N5, C3 | 300 | 450 |
  | N6, C4 | 450 | 600 |

  - The same guide reproduces Table 2.1 serviceability test pressures (N1/N2 400 Pa general; N3/C1 600 Pa; N4/C2 800 Pa ...) and Section 8 labelling: each window is marked with SLS and ULS pressure and "(d) The water penetration resistance".
  - [AGWA – A Guide to Window and Door Selection (v3)](https://www.agwa.com.au/common/Uploaded%20files/AGWA/Technical/AGWA%20Guides/AGWA-Guide-Selection.pdf)
- **AGWA NCC 2022 compliance overview (SECONDARY):**
  - Key criterion: "type testing of the window or door system demonstrates it meets (or exceeds) the Water Pentration [sic] Resistance (WPR) level equivalent to 30% of the positive SLS wind pressure (min 150 Pa)".
  - Separately: "Verification the requirements for AS 4654.2 for sill installation, water-stop angles and termination heights have been met".
  - Visual inspection items: "Waterproofing membrane, sill placement, waterstop angle and termination heights in accordance with AS 4654.2".
  - Its Vol One egress summary notes threshold ramps of "no more than a 1:8 gradient and a maximum rise of 35 mm" (AS 1428.1-based).
  - [AGWA – Compliance Guide V1 (NCC 2022)](https://www.agwa.com.au/common/Uploaded%20files/AGWA/Technical/AGWA%20Guides/AGWA%20Compliance%20Guide%20V1%20FINAL.pdf)
- **Testing (SECONDARY, single manufacturer page):** Water-penetration testing is to AS 4420.1. — [Proline – Australian standards](https://prolineqld.au/pages/australian-standards)
- **WA IB 037/2014 (government guidance, non-NSW):** Water penetration at doors "generally occurs due to poor sealing of the membrane to the sill, allowing wind-driven rain to be pushed back or soak through", and the AS 4654.2 requirements "may be in addition to water resistance requirements" of AS 2047. — [WA IB 037](https://wa.gov.au/system/files/2024-10/ib_037_waterproofing_external_above_ground_building_elements.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Test pressure as a water head** (my arithmetic: 1 Pa ≈ 0.102 mm of water head): 150 Pa ≈ 15 mm, 300 Pa ≈ 31 mm, 600 Pa ≈ 61 mm.
  - A door sill without enough internal upstand or drained-track design struggles to resist these pressures. This is why low or level-threshold sliding doors typically carry lower WPR ratings, or need special drained or compressed-seal sills.
  - The designer must confirm that the chosen level-threshold door's **tested and labelled WPR** meets the site's AS 2047 requirement (for example, ≥150 Pa for a non-exposed N2 site, ≥300 Pa for an exposed N3/C1 site).
- **Exposure:** Because exposed sites need higher WPR, an unprotected balcony door generally makes a level threshold harder than a covered or recessed one.
- **Two separate checks:** AS 2047 (the door product) and AS 4654.2 (the membrane-to-sill junction and termination height) are independent DTS requirements. Meeting one does not satisfy the other.

### Gaps
- **AS 2047 clause text:** I could not access it (paywalled). Unverified: any clause on minimum sill upstand height, sill drainage or weep design, exposure definitions, or any relaxation for low or accessible thresholds. The AGWA guide says sill placement, waterstop angles and termination heights are governed by AS 4654.2 rather than AS 2047.
- **Door leakage limits:** The exact leakage limits for doors under AS 4420.1 were not verified from a primary source.

## Q6. Liveable Housing Design Standard (NCC 2022 Part H8 / G7, LHDS): what it requires for step-free entrances and thresholds, NSW's status as of 2026, and whether it covers balcony doors

### Takeaway
**NSW has opted out.**
- NCC 2022 and NCC 2025 NSW Schedule 5 both state that Part H8 (Class 1a) and Part G7 (Class 2 sole-occupancy units) have "deliberately been left blank" and do not apply in NSW.
- Where H8 applies elsewhere, it requires a step-free path to, and "at least one level and step-free entrance door" into, the dwelling.
- It does **not** require step-free balcony doors. Balconies appear only in a measurement rule for the entrance-door exemption.

### Cited Findings
- **NCC 2022 Vol Two, NSW Part H8 (PRIMARY):** "This Part has deliberately been left blank. Part H8 does not apply in NSW, as livable housing design requirements do not apply to Class 1a buildings in NSW." — [NCC 2022 Vol Two Sch 5 NSW Part H8](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-two/5-new-south-wales/h8-livable-housing-design)
- **NCC 2025 Vol Two, NSW Part H8 (PRIMARY):** Identical wording; NSW continues the opt-out into NCC 2025. — [NCC 2025 Vol Two Sch 5 NSW Part H8](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-two/5-new-south-wales/nsw-h8-livable-housing-design)
- **NCC 2022 and 2025 Vol One, NSW Part G7 (PRIMARY):** "This Part has deliberately been left blank. Part G7 does not apply in NSW as livable housing design requirements do not apply to sole-occupancy units in a Class 2 building in NSW." — [NCC 2022 Vol One Sch 5 NSW G7](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-one/5-new-south-wales/g7-livable-housing-design); [NCC 2025 Vol One Sch 5 NSW G7](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/5-new-south-wales/nsw-g7-livable-housing-design)
- **H8P1 Livable housing design (national text, PRIMARY):**
  - Requires "(a) a continuous and step-free path to a dwelling entrance door from either— the pedestrian entry at the allotment boundary; or an appurtenant Class 10a garage or carport; or a car parking space ...; and (b) at least one level and step-free entrance door into the dwelling from the access path required by (a); and (c) internal doors and corridors on the ground or entrance level which facilitate unimpeded movement between spaces".

  - H8D2(3): where the entrance-door exemption is assessed, the level difference is measured "from the floor level at the entrance door, or if there is an attached deck, balcony or the like that provides a step-free connection to the entrance door, from the lowest point of that deck, balcony or the like above the surface beneath".
  - The word "threshold" does not appear in the Part H8 page text.
  - [NCC 2022 Vol Two Part H8](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-two/h-class-1-and-10-buildings/part-h8-livable-housing-design)
- **LHDS threshold details (SECONDARY, search-result summaries only; not verified against the LHDS text):** Where the standard applies, a step-free entrance may have up to a 5 mm vertical tolerance between abutting surfaces if rounded or bevelled, and thresholds over 5 mm and under 56 mm may use a ramped threshold. — [Chalkline – NCC H8 Livable Housing Design](https://chalkline.com.au/compliance/ncc-livable-housing-silver); [The Good Builder – Six states and territories now apply livable housing; NSW and WA are still out](https://thegoodbuilder.com.au/six-states-and-territories-now-apply-livable-housing-nsw-and-wa-are-still-out/)

### Inferences
- **NCC-driven accessibility:** In NSW there is no NCC (LHDS) driver for a step-free balcony door in Class 1a or Class 2 SOUs. A level threshold at a balcony is therefore a client or design choice, or a planning-instrument requirement, not an NCC accessibility requirement.
- **Elsewhere in Australia:** Even where the LHDS applies, it targets one entrance door, not balcony doors. A step-free balcony connection matters only if the balcony forms part of the step-free route to the entrance door (H8D2(3)).
- **Interaction with waterproofing:** If a Class 2 balcony door is on an accessible path required by Part D4 / AS 1428.1, the NCC 2025 F1D4 explanatory note signals the interaction (for example, D4D2 limiting gradients to 1:40).

### Gaps
- **NSW planning controls:** I did not verify NSW planning-level accessibility controls (for example, Apartment Design Guide / SEPP (Housing) objectives requiring a percentage of apartments to meet Livable Housing Guidelines Silver Level, or council DCP adaptable-housing requirements). These could require step-free balcony access in practice.
- **LHDS clause text:** The 5 mm / 56 mm threshold figures were not verified against the primary LHDS text.

### Inferences
- **Who settles level thresholds:** Official national guidance does not resolve how to do a level threshold. Under NCC 2022 in NSW the answer lies in AS 4654.2's door details. Failing that, a Performance Solution typically uses a drained channel or grate at the door, a structural set-down with raised or pedestal finishes, and a door with an adequate AS 2047 WPR rating. Under NCC 2025 (Class 2), the 70 mm structural set-down must be added unless a Performance Solution is used.
- **Weight of the WA bulletin:** It is persuasive but not binding in NSW. It is also dated 2014 and refers to AS 2047-1999 and older NCC clause numbers (F1.4 and 3.8.1.3).
