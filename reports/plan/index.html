Keep the 75 mm drop only as a drained gutter at the door, and pack the rest of the deck up to tile depth

With the door sill sitting on the inside floor and the tiles level with it, the membrane can't turn up the 40–50 mm above the tile that AS 4654.2 Table A1 suggests for N1/N2. So the door has to use cl 2.8.3(b): membrane fixed under the sill and run into the stormwater system through a grated channel in a recess at the door (Fig 2.9). Your structure is already 75 mm down, so leave a 160 mm strip at that level for the channel. Raise the rest of the deck on tapered firring and new sheet so tiles bond straight onto the membrane, falling away from the door. That keeps the deck light over the room below and puts the fall in the substrate.

Why this layout

  • Light: about 75 mm of screed over a framed floor would weigh roughly 150 kg/m² and hold water. Firring and sheet weigh a fraction of that, and tiles bond straight to the membrane.
  • Falls in the substrate: the tapered firring gives the fall the standard asks for, with no wet bed under the tiles.
  • The door detail is the standard's own: Fig 2.9 is a gutter formed in the substrate in front of the opening. Your 75 mm drop becomes that gutter.
  • No change to the inside floor: the sill sits on the subfloor as normal, on a sub-sill tray over the membrane.

The risk over a bedroom or living room is the channel outlet. Its pipe runs in the balcony joist space, which is the ceiling of the room below. Use solvent-welded pipe with no mechanical joints in that space, graded to the outer edge. Water-test it, and have the certifier inspect it before the ceiling is closed. If the side of the balcony allows it, an end-outlet channel discharging sideways avoids the ceiling void altogether.

Fallback if no channel fits

Pavers on pedestals with open joints, the membrane staying at −75 mm everywhere and turned up the step face and under the sill. This is how the New Zealand details (BRANZ BU583, Viking Buzon BZ03) do it. It isn't an AS 4654.2 figure, so treat it as a Performance Solution and get the certifier's agreement first.

Sequence, with hold points

  1. Agree the design before anything is cut

    Send your certifier this section: the cl 2.8.3(b) route with a Fig 2.9 channel. Choose a membrane system the maker rates for fibre-cement over timber framing (a high-movement Class III membrane). Choose the channel, then get the membrane maker's written OK for joining their membrane to that channel's flange. Ask the door supplier for the sill profile and a matching sub-sill tray.

  2. Measure

    Measure inside FFL, existing substrate levels at the door and the outer edge (string line), the opening width, internal finish thickness, and joist size, spacing and direction. Find where the downpipe is. If adding firring and sheet changes the load, get the engineer to check the joists.

  3. Framing

    Fix tapered firring for 1:100 minimum (1:80 better) away from the house. Stop it about 160 mm short of the step face (or the channel width plus 10 mm) to leave the recess. Add noggings so every sheet edge at the recess, and every joint, is supported.

  4. Plumbing rough-inCertifier inspection

    Fit puddle flanges for the channel outlet and the main deck outlet, an overflow through the edge upstand set below the door line, and solvent-welded pipe in the joist bay to the downpipe. Water-test the pipes. The stormwater connection is a critical stage inspection, so book it before the ceiling below is closed.

  5. Sheet

    Lay compressed fibre-cement sheet rated for tiled external decks, fixed to the maker's pattern, with joints over supports. Leave the recess floor at the existing substrate.

  6. Prepare

    Prime. Put bond breakers or fillets at every internal corner: both recess edges, the step face, and the wall junctions. Reinforce sheet joints and outlet surrounds as the system requires.

  7. Membrane

    Run one continuous membrane: across the deck, down into the recess, along its floor, up the step face, across the subfloor top under the sill, and up the waterstop line. At the house wall and any side walls, take it at least 50 mm above the finished tile (N2; 40 mm for N1), behind the cladding and flashings. Turn it into both outlets and the overflow. Check the film thickness.

  8. Sub-sill and waterstop

    Bed the aluminium sub-sill tray in sealant on the membrane over the subfloor. It needs a rear upstand (the waterstop), end dams returned at the jambs, and a front lip over the channel. Seal every fixing before drilling.

  9. Flood testHold point

    Plug the outlets, dam the door, and keep water below the subfloor top for at least 24 hours. Watch the ceiling below. Take dated photos of the sub-sill, waterstop, recess corners, outlets and overflow, and get the applicator's AS 4654.2 certificate.

  10. Channel

    Set the channel on bed or packers in the recess, with its outlet into the puddle flange and the membrane joined to its flange as the maker specifies. Grate top goes at inside FFL, full opening width plus about 100 mm each side. Keep at least 2 mm nylon separation between stainless and aluminium.

  11. Tile

    Adhere tiles direct to the membrane, falling away from the grate, with a perimeter movement joint and movement joints at no more than 4.5 m. Keep outlets clear and the grate removable. Check for ponding with a hose before grouting is finished.

  12. DoorCheck label

    Set the sill on packers in the sub-sill and seal it. Confirm the door's AS 2047 label shows at least 150 Pa for the sill you're actually fitting. Seal the inside finish to the sill. At handover, give the owner the photos, flood-test record, membrane certificate, and a note to keep the grate clear.

Measure or confirm these on site

  • Internal floor finish thickness, which sets the step face height (assumed 20 mm).
  • Sill profile: depth, height above the floor (assumed about 25 mm), and the sub-sill tray it accepts.
  • Channel's real overall depth and width, including bed. It must fit in 75 mm.
  • Whether the existing balcony sheet is flat or already falls, and what it is.
  • Joist direction (firring assumes joists run out from the house), size and spacing.
  • Edge condition: balustrade upstand with outlet and overflow, or open edge to a gutter.
  • Downpipe location for the pipe run.
  • Any DA condition requiring an accessible threshold (none applies to a NSW house by default).

Buy list to price

  • Tapered firring, noggings, and compressed fibre-cement sheet rated for tiled external decks.
  • Membrane system for fibre-cement over timber, with primer, bond breaker, reinforcing and joint tape.
  • Stainless threshold channel ≤55 mm overall, 65–100 mm grate, with outlet and flange (e.g. a Stormtech threshold series or similar).
  • Puddle flanges for two outlets, an overflow spitter, and DN pipe sized by the plumber.
  • Aluminium sub-sill tray with end dams and rear upstand, packers, sealant, backing rod, and nylon separation strip.
  • Door with an AS 2047 rating of at least 150 Pa.
  • External tiles and adhesive compatible with the membrane, plus a movement-joint sealant.
