# Recess the structure to keep thresholds level

You can build a tiled balcony with no step at the door in NSW, for a Class 1 house or a Class 2 apartment, but the step has to move into the structure. Under the NCC 2022 rules in force until NSW adopts NCC 2025 on **1 May 2027**, both H2D8 (Class 1) and F1D5 (Class 2) send you to **AS 4654.2-2012**. That standard has only one binding route for a flush door, **clause 2.8.3**: a sub-sill flashing within the membrane system and, where finished levels leave no room for an upturn, **the membrane fixed under the sill and terminated into the stormwater system**. In practice that means the Figure 2.9 detail: a **grated gutter formed in the slab across the full door width, with the deck falling away from it**. The familiar Table A1 upturn heights (**40 mm at N1 to 180 mm at N6**) are informative and are measured above the *external* finished surface, so a flush threshold cannot meet them; Building Commission NSW says the termination height "should be determined by the façade engineer". From 1 May 2027, NCC 2025 makes concrete Class 2–9 balconies have a **1:80 fall in the structural slab, a 70 mm minimum structural step-down at the door, a 70 mm integral perimeter hob, an overflow invert at least 20 mm below the internal structural floor, and the membrane laid directly on the slab**. That rules out the common level-slab-plus-screed build, but not a flush *finished* floor carried on pedestals, a thick bed or a threshold drain. Class 1 rules do not change. The door is a separate test: framed glazed doors need an AS 2047 water-penetration rating (at least **150 Pa**, and **300 Pa** on an exposed N3 site), and flush accessible sills often struggle to reach it. NSW law adds DBP Act regulated designs and declarations for Class 2, RAB Act orders that have already targeted missing door drainage and overflows, a contractor licence for waterproofing work over $5,000, and **no mandatory certifier inspection of an external membrane**, so the builder has to set its own hold point. NSW has opted out of the NCC livable housing provisions, so the push for level balcony doors comes from council adaptable-housing DCPs, access reports and clients, not from the NCC.

## NCC 2022 still governs, and it sets no numbers of its own

**NCC 2022 (as amended) remains the edition in force in NSW in November 2026.** The NSW Government says the current edition is NCC 2022 Amendment 2, in effect from 29 July 2025, and that NSW adopts NCC 2025 on 1 May 2027 ([Building Commission NSW – Industry changes](https://www.nsw.gov.au/departments-and-agencies/building-commission/industry-changes); [NSW ministerial release](https://www.nsw.gov.au/ministerial-releases/nsw-to-adopt-new-national-construction-code-may-2027)). The ABCB adoption table lists NSW at "1 May 2027" with transition "N/A", and records that NCC 2025 was published on 1 May 2026 ([ABCB adoption information](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/ncc-2025/ncc-2025-state-and-territory-adoption-information)). NSW has announced one waterproofing variation for NCC 2025: owners doing **remedial** work on existing apartment buildings may choose the NCC 2022 or NCC 2025 waterproofing rules. It does not cover new balconies, and the researchers could not find it in the published NSW Schedule 5 ([NSW release](https://www.nsw.gov.au/ministerial-releases/nsw-to-adopt-new-national-construction-code-may-2027); [NCC 2025 Vol One NSW Schedule 5](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/5-new-south-wales)). NSW makes no variation to the external-waterproofing clauses themselves in either edition ([NCC 2025 Vol Two NSW Part H2](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-two/5-new-south-wales/nsw-h2-damp-and-weatherproofing)). The EP&A Act was amended in 2023 to fix which NCC version applies to a CC application ([Building Defects Library v1.1](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-06/building-defects-library.pdf.pdf)). The exact rule for applications lodged around 1 May 2027 was not verified, so confirm the assessed edition with your principal certifier on any job that straddles the date.

**Class 2 (Volume One, NCC 2022).** There are two Performance Requirements. F1P2 says surface water from a storm with a 1% annual exceedance probability "must not enter the building". F3P1 says roofs and external walls, "including openings around windows and doors", must prevent water penetration that causes unhealthy conditions or undue dampness. The Deemed-to-Satisfy clause F1D5 requires a "roof, balcony, podium or similar horizontal surface" to have a membrane "consisting of materials complying with AS 4654.1; and designed and installed in accordance with AS 4654.2". F1D4 requires exposed joints to be protected per AS 4654.2 Section 2.9 and kept out from under planters. F1D2 exempts timber or perforated decking and balconies "located directly above ground" ([NCC 2022 Vol One Part F1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f1-surface-water-management-rising-damp-and-external-waterproofing)). F3D4 requires framed sliding and swinging glazed doors to meet AS 2047 water-penetration resistance ([NCC 2022 Vol One Part F3](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f3-roof-and-wall-cladding)). **The NCC 2022 text gives no figure for falls, step-down, hob, overflow or termination height.** Every number comes from AS 4654.2. The egress clause D3D16(d) actually *allows* a door sill up to **190 mm** above an external balcony, which Building Commission NSW describes as "a concession intended for weatherproofing" ([NCC 2022 Vol One Part D3](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-one/d-access-and-egress/part-d3-construction-exits); [Building Defects Library v1.1](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-06/building-defects-library.pdf.pdf)).

**Class 1 (Volume Two).** The Performance Requirement is H2P2, which uses the same weatherproofing words as F3P1. The DTS clause H2D8 calls up AS 4654.1 and AS 4654.2 for "flat roofs, roof terraces, balconies and terraces and other similar horizontal surfaces **located above internal spaces**". It excludes "a concrete slab that has a minimum step-down of 50 mm below the internal floor level", a suspended slab over an unused subfloor with the same 50 mm step-down, and spaced timber decking ([NCC 2022 Vol Two Part H2](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-two/h-class-1-and-10-buildings/part-h2-damp-and-weatherproofing)). The result is counter-intuitive. A **level-threshold concrete balcony over a habitable room cannot use the 50 mm exclusion, so AS 4654.2 applies to it in full**, door details included. Read literally, a balcony with a 50 mm step-down is outside the DTS membrane requirement altogether; that is the researchers' reading and should be confirmed with your certifier. A balcony that is *not* above internal space, for example one over open ground or a carport, falls outside H2D8, but H2P2 still governs the door opening. Glazed doors must be designed, constructed and installed to AS 2047 under H1D8. Housing Provisions Part 8.2 also applies to installation where the building is within the AS 4055 geometric limits and the wind class is N3 or lower ([NCC 2022 Vol Two Part H1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-two/h-class-1-and-10-buildings/part-h1-structure)). Framed balconies attached to the house with a waling plate need flashings at the bottom, top and sides that extend **150 mm** beyond each side, lap **75 mm** and tuck **25 mm** under the cladding ([Housing Provisions Part 12.3](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/housing-provisions/12-ancillary-provisions/part-123-attachment-framed-decks-and-balconies-external-walls-buildings-using)). **H2D8 and H2P2 are word-for-word identical in NCC 2025**, so nothing changes for houses on 1 May 2027 ([NCC 2025 Vol Two Part H2](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-two/h-class-1-and-10-buildings/part-h2-damp-and-weatherproofing)).

Any departure from AS 4654.2 needs a **Performance Solution**, and the H2D8 explanatory note says so expressly ([NCC 2022 Vol Two Part H2](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-two/h-class-1-and-10-buildings/part-h2-damp-and-weatherproofing)). In NSW that means showing compliance with the Performance Requirements, or equivalence to the DTS Provisions, through a documented brief, analysis and report ([NCC 2025 Vol One NSW A2](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/5-new-south-wales/nsw-a2-compliance-ncc)). No Verification Method exists for balconies; F3V1, H2V1 and F1V1 cover external walls only. A level threshold justified as a Performance Solution therefore rests on Expert Judgement or comparison with the DTS.

### NCC 2025 moves the step into the slab for Class 2 concrete from 1 May 2027

NCC 2025 merges Parts F1 and F3 into a single "Part F1 Water management". The ABCB's stated reason is that most water defects come from "sub-surface water ... beneath finished surfaces of building elements such as balconies" ([ABCB – NCC 2025 water management changes](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/ncc-2025/ncc-2025-key-changes/ncc-2025-water-management-changes)). The new **F1D4** requires a concrete roof, balcony, podium or similar part to have four things. First, a drainage system "that incorporates sufficient overflow provision". Second, "its structural substrate graded with a **minimum fall of 1:80** in the plane of the substrate". Third, "a **minimum 70 mm step down** from the surface level of the internal floor structural substrate to the adjacent surface level of the external structural substrate, where applicable". Fourth, "a **monolithic hob** that is integral with the structural substrate, **at least 70 mm high**", at the outer perimeter, unless the edge drains directly to a gutter. The notes add that the overflow invert must be "not less than **20 mm below** any adjacent internal floor structural substrate". They also say "a tile bed, screed, topping or similar component is not considered a structural substrate", and that the 70 mm "is not intended to refer to the minimum waterproofing termination height specified in AS 4654.2", which "may require in some circumstances a step down greater than 70 mm". Other clauses follow the same logic. F1D7(2) puts the membrane "directly on a structural substrate". F1D10 requires finishes to be "self-draining" or "directly fixed to a membrane". F1D6 moves exposed joints to the ridge or high point. F1D14 carries over the AS 2047 door requirement. The F1D4 package applies only to **concrete** balconies, and F1D2 still exempts perforated decking and balconies directly above ground ([NCC 2025 Vol One Part F1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f1-water-management)).

For a level threshold this is a measured change, not a ban. The 70 mm is **substrate-to-substrate**, so a flush *finished* threshold still passes DTS if the external slab is set down 70 mm or more at the door and the finish is built back up on pedestals (self-draining) or on a bed bonded to the membrane. What disappears is the common builder practice of a level structural slab with falls and the step built in screed. HIA reads the rule the same way, saying it stops "the use of screeds being used to achieve level surfaces between internal and external spaces, and to create the required fall of 1:80" ([HIA – Planning for waterproofing of concrete balconies](https://hia.com.au/resources-and-advice/building-it-right/australian-standards/articles/planning-for-waterproofing-of-concrete-balconies)). The ABCB also flags the accessibility clash: D4D2 "may prevent gradients steeper than 1:40" ([NCC 2025 Vol One Part F1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f1-water-management)).

| | Class 1 house (Vol Two) | Class 2 apartment (Vol One) |
|---|---|---|
| **NCC 2022 – in force now** | H2P2; H2D8 calls up AS 4654.1/.2 for balconies above internal spaces; ≥50 mm concrete step-down and spaced decking excluded; AS 2047 via H1D8 | F1P2 (1% AEP), F3P1; F1D5 calls up AS 4654.1/.2; F1D4 joints per AS 4654.2 s 2.9; F3D4 AS 2047; **no NCC figures** for falls, step, hob or overflow |
| **NCC 2025 – NSW from 1 May 2027** | No change: H2D8 and H2P2 identical | Concrete: F1D4 1:80 structural fall, ≥70 mm structural step-down, ≥70 mm integral hob, overflow invert ≥20 mm below internal structural floor; F1D7(2) membrane on slab; F1D10 self-draining or direct-fixed finish; F1D14 AS 2047 |

## AS 4654.2 allows a flush door through one binding sentence

AS 4654.2-2012 is paywalled ([Standards Australia store](https://store.standards.org.au/product/as-4654-2-2012)). The clause wording here was checked against a full text of the 2012 edition and against two licensed public reproductions: the QBCC Technical Feature of February 2017 ([QBCC 2017](https://www.conpro.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/QBCC-Technical-Feature-Waterproofing-requirements-doorways-to-balconies-130217.pdf)) and Building Commission NSW's Building Defects Library ([Building Defects Library v1.1](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-06/building-defects-library.pdf.pdf)). No amendment has been issued. The distinction that matters to a builder is between "shall" text, which is binding when you rely on the DTS route, and notes and informative appendices, which the standard's own preface calls "only for information and guidance".

**Termination height is a performance rule with informative numbers.** Clause 2.8.1.1 says: "the finished height of the membrane above the **finished surface level** shall be sufficient to prevent water, including wind driven, flowing over the top of the membrane". The numbers sit only in **informative Appendix A, Table A1** ([Building Defects Library v1.1](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-06/building-defects-library.pdf.pdf); [QBCC 2017](https://www.conpro.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/QBCC-Technical-Feature-Waterproofing-requirements-doorways-to-balconies-130217.pdf)):

| Wind class (AS 4055) | ULS wind speed Vh,u | Table A1 upturn above **external** finished level |
|---|---|---|
| N1 | 34 m/s | 40 mm |
| N2 | 40 m/s | 50 mm |
| N3 / C1 | 50 m/s | 70 mm |
| N4 / C2 | 61 m/s | 100 mm |
| N5 / C3 | 74 m/s | 150 mm |
| N6 / C4 | 86 m/s | 180 mm |

Appendix A also allows a second method. Where stormwater retention is designed into the area, the upturn must be above the overflow level ([Local Group Fact Sheet 008](https://localgroup.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Fact-Sheet-008-Waterproofing-AS4654.pdf)). Building Commission NSW's commentary is blunt: "Appendix A is not a normative requirement ... **The height of membrane termination should be determined by the façade engineer**" ([Building Defects Library v1.1](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-06/building-defects-library.pdf.pdf)). The datum is the top of the external tile. With external and internal finishes flush, a Table A1 upturn would have to rise 40–70 mm *above the internal floor*, which cannot be done without a raised sill. **A level threshold therefore cannot comply by upturn height. It has to use clause 2.8.3(b).**

**Clause 2.8.3 is the level-threshold rule.** Its two binding sentences are: "(a) Sub-sill flashing shall be included as part of the membrane system" and "(b) Where the internal and external finished floor levels do not allow an upturn, the membranes shall be fixed under the sill and terminate in the stormwater system" ([Building Defects Library v1.1](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-06/building-defects-library.pdf.pdf)). The informative notes fill in the detail ([AS 4654.2-2012](https://store.standards.org.au/product/as-4654-2-2012)):
- Note 3: "Ideally, the deck surface should fall away from the grate, and additionally the grate should be to the width of or greater than the opening".
- Note 5: openings "should be provided with a set-down or hob".
- Note 6: "Where circumstances do not permit the inclusion of a set-down or hob (e.g., for wheelchair access), **a gutter should be formed into the substrate immediately in front of the opening**".
- Note 7: AS 2047 governs fixings to seals and frames.

**Figure 2.9** is the only level-threshold detail in the standard. It shows:
- a gutter formed in the substrate, drained to stormwater;
- a grate the width of the opening or wider, on a non-ferrous frame;
- flashing under the door sill;
- a waterstop with flexible sealant at the internal floor, with the waterstop angle returned to the external face;
- "Deck surface to fall away from grate".

It gives **no dimensions** ([QBCC 2017](https://www.conpro.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/QBCC-Technical-Feature-Waterproofing-requirements-doorways-to-balconies-130217.pdf); [NSW Guide to Standards and Tolerances 2017](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2025-07/nsw-guide-to-standards-and-tolerances-2017.pdf)). Regulators present this detail as *the* answer. QBCC says level balconies are "not prohibited" but "more problematic to construct and achieve compliance", and that the standard's answer is "a grated drain to be formed across the front of the door opening with requisite waterproofing membranes and flashing being installed under the adjoining door sill" ([QBCC 2017](https://www.conpro.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/QBCC-Technical-Feature-Waterproofing-requirements-doorways-to-balconies-130217.pdf)). The WA Building Commission repeats the gutter rule ([WA IB 037/2014](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2024-10/ib_037_waterproofing_external_above_ground_building_elements.pdf)). The NSW Guide's clause 14.6 treats a deck as defective if it lacks "a drainage system sufficient to withstand wind-driven water surging from the deck or balcony" ([NSW Guide 2017](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2025-07/nsw-guide-to-standards-and-tolerances-2017.pdf)). Strictly, the gutter sits in a "should" note, so it is not itself mandatory. But it is the only way the standard shows to "terminate in the stormwater system" at a door. **A flush threshold that relies only on the deck falling away, with no drained gutter at the door line, is not the Figure 2.9 arrangement and needs a Performance Solution.** Figure 2.8 (Options 1–3, the sub-sill, PVC sill flip and waterstop angle details) is a *step-down* detail: every option dimensions a Table A1 height above the external finished floor.

**Falls are an outcome rule with a recommended minimum.** Clause 2.5.2 makes two things binding. Water must drain to the outlet, and must not be retained "with the exception of residual water remaining due to surface tension". The fall must be "in the structural substrate, or formed by a screed over the structural substrate". The well-known 1:100 figure is only a note: "should be no flatter than 1 in 100" ([Building Defects Library v1.1](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-06/building-defects-library.pdf.pdf)). Certifiers and consultants commonly treat 1:100 as the DTS minimum, and Conpro writes it as "must" ([Conpro interpretation](https://www.conpro.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Interpretation-of-external-Std-for-Melbourne.pdf)). Build to at least 1:100 now, and to 1:80 in the structural slab for Class 2 concrete from May 2027.

**Drains and overflows are binding, but their sizes are not set here.**
- Clause 2.10: the membrane "shall be connected to the stormwater drainage system" by a turn-down into the inlet, or by a flange or clamping ring. The drain "shall have a sump, inlet pit, grate or cage". Where the finish sits above the membrane, the notes call for a variable-level inlet that drains "at the membrane level".
- Clause 2.11: "The membrane shall be turned into the overflow" and "The finished floor level shall not reduce the design flow of an outlet". Notes say overflows should direct water away from the building and sit where a blockage will be seen.
- Building Commission NSW accepts that the membrane need not be turned into the overflow if a pre-formed outlet has a flange.
- Sources: [Building Defects Library v1.1](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-06/building-defects-library.pdf.pdf); [AS 4654.2-2012](https://store.standards.org.au/product/as-4654-2-2012).

AS 4654.2 sets no overflow height relative to the door and no minimum number of outlets. At a level threshold the internal floor is effectively at the external finished level, so any ponding reaches the sill straight away. The overflow therefore has to work at channel or membrane level, or the edge has to be open. The Figure 2.9 door gutter is not said to double as the overflow, so a separate overflow below the door line is the defensible design.

**Build-up and materials rules shape the depth.** These come from [AS 4654.2-2012](https://store.standards.org.au/product/as-4654-2-2012):
- **Fillets:** mandatory at horizontal-to-vertical changes (cl 2.7). The note gives 15 × 15 mm for liquid membranes and 40 × 40 mm for sheet.
- **Bond breakers:** "shall be provided where movement between substrates is expected" (cl 2.12).
- **Joints:** a continuous membrane over a joint "shall be unbonded for the first 100 mm" (cl 2.9).
- **Bonded beds and toppings:** need sufficient movement joints, ideally 10 mm wide at 4.5 m centres (cl 2.15.2).
- **Unbonded toppings:** a structurally sufficient topping needs a double slip sheet, and the note recommends at least **50 mm** with lightweight mesh (cl 2.15.2).
- **Fixings:** fixings through the membrane "shall be sealed" (cl 2.8.4).
- **Protection:** the membrane must be protected until covered (cl 2.14).
- **Inspection:** inspection or acceptance testing is required on completion. The note suggests a dry-film-thickness test or "a controlled water test ... for a minimum duration of 24 h" (cl 2.16).
- **Materials:** must comply with AS 4654.1 and be designed as Class I, II or III movement (cl 1.6, 2.3). By elongation at break, Class I is below 60%, Class II is 60–299% and Class III is 300% or more ([Fosroc AS 4654.1 test report](https://www.fosroc.com.au/sites/default/files/resources_file_storage/Fosroc_Nitoproof_410_AS4654.1.pdf)).

**A revision is coming but changes nothing yet.** Both parts of AS 4654 are listed as pending revision ([Nexis CPD](https://nexiscpd.com.au/external-waterproofing-roofs-decks-planters/)). A redline DR AS 4654.2:2026 was *reported* to have been open for comment from 10 September to 12 November 2026; that was not verified. DR AS/NZS 4858:2026 "Waterproofing membranes for wet areas and external above-ground use" is confirmed open until 30 November 2026 ([Standards Australia comment portal](https://comment.standards.org.au/Index)). Nothing about their threshold provisions is public. A revised standard becomes mandatory only when an NCC edition calls it up, and NCC 2025 references AS 4654.2-2012.

## The door, the drain and the tiles each have their own requirement

**AS 2047 is a second, independent test at the threshold.** Framed sliding, swinging, French and bi-fold glazed doors must meet AS 2047 water-penetration resistance:
- Class 2: F3D4 under NCC 2022, F1D14 under NCC 2025. Frameless doors and one-off site-built units are excluded.
- Class 1: H1D8.

Sources: [NCC 2022 Vol One Part F3](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f3-roof-and-wall-cladding); [NCC 2025 Vol One Part F1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f1-water-management). The test pressure is "30% of the positive SLS wind pressure (min 150 Pa)" ([AGWA Compliance Guide](https://www.agwa.com.au/common/Uploaded%20files/AGWA/Technical/AGWA%20Guides/AGWA%20Compliance%20Guide%20V1%20FINAL.pdf)). AGWA reproduces AS 2047 Table 2.3 as follows ([AGWA Window and Door Selection Guide](https://www.agwa.com.au/common/Uploaded%20files/AGWA/Technical/AGWA%20Guides/AGWA-Guide-Selection.pdf)):

| Window/door rating | Non-exposed | Exposed |
|---|---|---|
| N1, N2 | 150 Pa | 200 Pa |
| N3, C1 | 150 Pa | 300 Pa |
| N4, C2 | 200 Pa | 300 Pa |
| N5, C3 | 300 Pa | 450 Pa |
| N6, C4 | 450 Pa | 600 Pa |

AGWA treats AS 4654.2 "sill installation, water-stop angles and termination heights" as a separate check. The WA bulletin says the membrane rules "may be in addition to" AS 2047 ([AGWA Compliance Guide](https://www.agwa.com.au/common/Uploaded%20files/AGWA/Technical/AGWA%20Guides/AGWA%20Compliance%20Guide%20V1%20FINAL.pdf); [WA IB 037/2014](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2024-10/ib_037_waterproofing_external_above_ground_building_elements.pdf)). Converting pressure to water head (1 Pa is about 0.1 mm of water) gives roughly 15 mm at 150 Pa, 31 mm at 300 Pa and 61 mm at 600 Pa. That is why flush sills need drained or compressed-seal designs, and why an exposed, uncovered balcony door is the hardest case. Some flush products do publish adequate ratings:
- AWS SlideMASTER recessed sill (Series 703): "Maximum Water 600Pa", with floating, self-draining tiles over the outer track ([AWS 703 product sheet](https://www.specifyaws.com.au/cdn/lhjUiWS3K4Cy/703-productsheet.pdf)).
- Alspec ProGlide UltraFlat: "630Pa water resistant sill" with an AS 1428.1 wheelchair sill ([Alspec ProGlide UltraFlat](https://alspec.com.au/_assets/products/brochures/ProGlide-UltraFlat-Brochure.pdf)).

Alspec also warns that "Door sills that comply with AS1428.1 may not meet the minimum Water Penetration Resistance in AS2047-2014" ([Alspec whitepaper](https://alspec.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2020-alspec-over-the-threshold-whitepaper-3.pdf)). Order the door against the site's wind class and exposure, and check the **label** on the actual sill configuration being installed.

**AS/NZS 3500.3 sizes the drains and overflows.** Both NCC editions require stormwater drainage to AS/NZS 3500.3 under F1D3 ([NCC 2025 Vol One Part F1](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/f-health-and-amenity/part-f1-water-management)). The 2018 edition's balcony clause (cl 3.8) requires design for a **20-year ARI** for drainage and a **100-year ARI for overflow**. Later material expresses these as 5% and 1% AEP ([BPC Victoria SD-02](https://www.bpc.vic.gov.au/resource-hub/practice-notes/SD-02-balcony-design-and-stormwater-drainage)). Which edition NSW currently calls up, and its exact clause wording, were not verified. Neither AS 4654.2 nor AS/NZS 3500.3 gives a minimum number of outlets. Regulators still enforce adequacy. A 2024 NSW rectification order treated "Only one overflow pipe has been provided for the 240 sqm balcony" as a serious defect ([Rectification Order, 41 Yattenden Cres](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2024-07/building-work-rectification-order-41-yattenden-cres-baulkham-hills.pdf)).

**Tiling and wet-area standards play smaller roles.** AS 3958.1-2007 was superseded by **AS 3958:2023** (Amd 1:2024), which now covers external tiling and floor gradients ([Standards store AS 3958:2023](https://store.standards.org.au/product/as-3958-2023); [HIA on AS 3958:2023](https://hia.com.au/resources-and-advice/building-materials-and-products/use-the-right-building-products-for-the-job/articles/updates-to-ceramic-tile-standard-as-3958-2023)). Under the 2007 numbering, external tiled floors needed intermediate movement joints at about 4.5 m centres where any dimension exceeded 4.5 m ([VBA Guide to Standards and Tolerances 2015](https://www.builderassist.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Guide-to-Standards-and-Tolerances.pdf)). That matches AS 4654.2's 4.5 m bonded-screed note. No reliable source was found for an AS 3958 external fall figure. AS 3740 covers internal wet areas only, so it matters at the door only if the room inside is a wet area.

## Councils, not the NCC, push NSW balcony doors to level

**NSW has opted out of the NCC livable housing provisions.** Part H8 (Class 1a) and Part G7 (Class 2 sole-occupancy units) have "deliberately been left blank" in the NSW schedules of both NCC 2022 and NCC 2025 ([NCC 2022 Vol Two NSW H8](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-two/5-new-south-wales/h8-livable-housing-design); [NCC 2025 Vol One NSW G7](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/volume-one/5-new-south-wales/nsw-g7-livable-housing-design)). The Government says it is monitoring other states but has not changed course ([NSW Parliament Q&A 95912](https://www.parliament.nsw.gov.au/parliamentary-business/questions-answers-tracking-details?id=95912)). Even where H8 applies, it targets "at least one level and step-free entrance door", not balcony doors ([NCC 2022 Vol Two Part H8](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2022/adopted/volume-two/h-class-1-and-10-buildings/part-h8-livable-housing-design)). The Apartment Design Guide's Objective 4Q-1 benchmark is **20% of apartments** at Livable Housing Guideline Silver level, and the ADG captions a figure "Level threshold transitions eliminate trip hazards" ([ADG Part 4](https://www.planning.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-03/apartment-design-guide-part-4-designing-the-building.pdf)). An access consultant reports that, since SEPP 65 moved into the Housing SEPP, the 20% figure is "planning guidance rather than a mandatory statutory requirement" ([Vista Access Architects](https://accessarchitects.com.au/vista-access-architects-blog/is-the-20-livable-housing-silver-level-requirement-still-mandatory-for-nsw-apartment-developments)). LHDG Silver does not list balconies among its seven core elements. At the entrance it allows "maximum vertical tolerance of 5mm" (rounded or bevelled) and a ramped threshold where the step is over 5 mm and "less than 56mm". It also advises "grated drain along threshold" and "generous cover at entries" for weather protection ([LHDG 4th ed.](https://universaldesignaustralia.net.au/wp-content/uploads/2017/08/lhaguidlineseditionno4-2017.pdf)).

**The binding drivers are council DCPs and the access reports that become consent documents.** Many councils require 10–25% of units to be **adaptable housing to AS 4299**. A 2006 survey counted 40 NSW councils with such controls ([Shelter NSW 2006](https://shelternsw.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/2006-Provisions-for-adaptable-housing-by-local-government-in-NSW-Chris-Elenor.pdf)). AS 4299 Class C asks for a "Maximum 3mm vertical step or a 5mm bevelled step at entry", with AS 1428.1 threshold ramps of "1 in 8 over a maximum 280mm" ([EMAA AS 4299 sheet](https://www.emaa.com.au/uploads/4/6/3/2/46326229/2020_draft_information_sheet_for_adaptable_housing.pdf)). Under AS 1428.1, a threshold ramp has a maximum rise of **35 mm**, length of **280 mm** and gradient of **1:8**; a step ramp has a maximum rise of 190 mm at 1:10 ([AGWA AS 1428.1 guide](https://www.agwa.com.au/common/Uploaded%20files/AGWA/Technical/AGWA%20Guides/AS1428%20Access%20and%20Mobility%20Guide%20V2.pdf)). Access reports lodged with DAs turn these into concrete commitments:
- Lane Cove: "All balcony levels to all the adaptable units will have a **10mm step down**", with threshold ramps and a **1:40** balcony fall ([Lane Cove access report](https://apps.planningportal.nsw.gov.au/prweb/PRRestService/DocMgmt/v1/PublicDocuments/DATA-WORKATTACH-FILE%20PEC-DPE-EP-WORK%20PPS-2018SNH048!20190911T044918.410%20GMT)).
- A Sydney SSD project: "maximum step downs of **35mm**" ([SSD-6387 access notes](https://majorprojects.planningportal.nsw.gov.au/prweb/PRRestService/mp/01/getContent?AttachRef=SSD-6387%2120190227T064030.022+GMT)).
- Blacktown: a "35mm threshold ramp and balcony to be sloped at max. 1:40 to achieve **100mm drop** at the end" ([Blacktown access report](https://www.blacktown.nsw.gov.au/files/assets/public/v/1/planning-neighbour-notifications/spp-20-00003/access-report-1-zoe-place-mount-druitt.pdf)).

Once in the consent, these numbers bind the builder, and the waterproofing design has to fit within them.

For **Class 2 common-area terraces and podiums**, NCC Part D4 and AS 1428.1 apply as law. Accessible doorways opening to open space need a threshold ramp or step ramp to AS 1428.1 under D3D16(c). The NCC access provisions do not apply inside sole-occupancy units ([ABCB D4D2 guide](https://www.abcb.gov.au/guide/d4d2-general-building-access-requirements); [Building Defects Library v1.1](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-06/building-defects-library.pdf.pdf)). Where an accessible route crosses the door-line grate, AS 1428.1 limits grate openings to **13 mm**, with slots not running in the direction of travel ([Alspec whitepaper](https://alspec.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/2020-alspec-over-the-threshold-whitepaper-3.pdf)).

In practice the accessible envelope is a finished-level difference of 0–5 mm, or up to 35 mm with a 1:8 threshold ramp, plus a fall away from the door no steeper than 1:40. That envelope is exactly why the clause 2.8.3(b) under-sill route with a door-line gutter becomes the default waterproofing detail. The Blacktown pattern of a small threshold ramp plus a 1:40 fall recovers freeboard quickly without a visible step.

## NSW law adds declarations and enforcement but no balcony inspection

**Class 2 work needs regulated designs and declarations.** "Waterproofing" is a building element under s 6(1) of the *Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020*. Balcony waterproofing in Class 2 buildings (and Class 3 and 9c since July 2023) needs a regulated design and a design compliance declaration, marked "W", from a registered design practitioner. The certifier "is unable to issue the certificate" without them, and a building compliance declaration must be lodged "before an application for an Occupation Certificate is made" ([Design Practitioners' Handbook, July 2023](https://nswdfsi-web.squiz.cloud/__data/assets/pdf_file/0010/1120501/Design-practitioners-handbook-final.pdf)).

The regulator's minimum content for external waterproofing has two parts. Plans must show membrane areas, "balcony/roof gradients" and the "Location of floor wastes/overflow". Sections and details must show membrane extent, "Termination of waterproofing membrane" and "Overflow details", at 1:50 with details at 1:20, 1:10 or 1:5. Door "flashings, sill heights" fall under the separate building-enclosure design, and the slab set-down and falls under the structural and architectural setout. A level threshold is the point where those three regulated designs must agree ([Design Practitioners' Handbook](https://nswdfsi-web.squiz.cloud/__data/assets/pdf_file/0010/1120501/Design-practitioners-handbook-final.pdf)). Architectural, structural and civil practitioners can declare waterproofing, and façade practitioners can declare external façade waterproofing ([NSW Government – remedial building work](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/compliance-and-regulation/professionals-working-on-regulated-buildings/design-and-building-practitioners/remedial-building-work)). There is no separate "waterproofing" class of practitioner ([ACRA fact sheet](https://acrassoc.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Fact-Sheet-Waterproofing.pdf)).

**The RAB Act has already been used on exactly this detail.** Building Commission NSW can issue stop-work, rectification and prohibition orders, and a prohibition order blocks the occupation certificate. Waterproofing failures count as "serious defects" ([Building Commission NSW – defect complaints](https://www.nsw.gov.au/departments-and-agencies/building-commission/about-us/building-defect-complaints)). The July 2024 Baulkham Hills order listed several serious defects:
- balconies with "no visible overflow provisions";
- "Water ponding as evidence of inadequate falls";
- "the absence of adequate drainage system at the door to prevent water entering the building".

It gave 60 days for designs and 120 days for rectification ([Yattenden Cres order](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2024-07/building-work-rectification-order-41-yattenden-cres-baulkham-hills.pdf)). In *The Owners – SP 92226 v 1A Eden Pty Ltd* [2026] NSWSC 563, leaks blamed on "a lack of a barrier underneath the balcony doors and inadequate overflow sizes" produced a **$2.5 m** judgment. The developer was in liquidation, the estimated dividend was **$308,406**, and the owners raised a **$4.5 m** special levy ([Lexology commentary](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=d26bc6b2-af17-4edb-be0d-4106866ccbbe)).

**Licensing, warranties and inspections apply to all classes.**
- **Licensing.** NSW Fair Trading treats waterproofing as residential trade work needing a contractor licence above **$5,000** (labour and materials, including GST). It is not on the "specialist work" list (plumbing, draining, gasfitting, electrical, air-conditioning), which needs a licence at any value ([NSW Fair Trading – licence categories](https://www.fairtrading.nsw.gov.au/trades-and-businesses/licensing-and-qualifications/licence-classes-and-qualifications/plumbing,-draining-and-gasfitting)). The stormwater connection of a door-line gutter is drainage work, so treat it as licensed specialist work regardless of value.
- **Warranties.** Waterproofing is a "major element" under the Home Building Act. A leak is a 6-year "major defect" only if it meets the s 18E consequences test; otherwise the 2-year warranty applies. *Ashton v Stevenson* held that a balcony leak with "scant" evidence of consequences was not a major defect ([Bartier Perry](https://www.bartier.com.au/insights/articles/ncat-update-what-is-a-major-defect)).
- **Inspections.** The mandatory critical stage inspections in s 61 of the EP&A (Development Certification and Fire Safety) Regulation 2021 cover waterproofing "**in a wet area**" only: every Class 1 house, and at least 10% of wet-area rooms in Class 2. They also cover stormwater drainage connections before covering. **No mandatory stage covers an external balcony membrane** ([DCFS Regulation 2021, s 61](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/sl-2021-0689); text checked against a 2021 point-in-time copy). AS 4654.2 cl 2.16 still requires inspection or acceptance testing on completion, so the builder's own flood test and photo record are the evidence trail.

**Reforms in progress.** The *Building (Approvals and Practitioners) Act 2026* received assent on 14 August 2026. Once proclaimed (no date yet), it will replace construction certificates and occupation certificates with "building approvals" and "completion approvals" and repeal the DBP Act. It keeps the non-apportionable duty of care and does not replace the Home Building Act ([Swaab](https://www.swaab.com.au/publication/nsw-building-approvals-and-practitioners-bill-2026-passes-parliament); [Design Institute of Australia](https://www.design.org.au/dianews/nsw-building-bill-what-it-means-for-interior-designers)). Separately, from **1 December 2026**, new apartment buildings with more than two units move from Home Building Compensation insurance to a strata building bond or decennial liability insurance ([Building Commission NSW – Industry changes](https://www.nsw.gov.au/departments-and-agencies/building-commission/industry-changes)). Whether that start date has held since 12 November 2026 was not confirmed.

## Builder's checklist: separate the "shall" from the "should"

The status labels below mean the following:
- **MANDATORY:** law or an NCC Performance Requirement.
- **DTS-MANDATORY:** an NCC DTS clause or AS 4654.2 "shall" text. It is binding when you rely on the DTS route; a documented Performance Solution can depart from it.
- **2027:** DTS-mandatory for NSW applications under NCC 2025 from 1 May 2027.
- **INFORMATIVE:** an AS note, informative appendix or NCC explanatory text.
- **GUIDANCE:** regulator or industry guidance.
- **MANUFACTURER:** a product instruction or claim.
- **CONSENT:** binding once written into a DA consent or approved access report.

"C1" means Class 1 and "C2" means Class 2; "FLAG" marks figures that conflict or are unverified (see the next section).

## Three level-threshold build-ups, sketched from the sources

The sketches below are **indicative, not to scale and not construction details**. Each is drawn only from what the cited sources show; any number not marked as sourced comes from the arithmetic noted. The common principle across QBCC, NSW, Swinburne/HIA, Multiplex, BRANZ and the drain makers is that **"level" describes the finished surfaces, never the structure**. Every compliant detail keeps a recess in the slab, either a gutter or a set-down.

### Sketch 1: Gutter formed in the substrate (AS 4654.2 Fig 2.9; NSW Diagram 14.06B)

```
EXTERNAL BALCONY                                                        INTERNAL
<-- finished deck falls AWAY from the grate

                      grate >= opening width        door/window sill
                     |<------------------->|        on packers           flexible sealant
                                                                            |
FFL ==tile==tile==  [F]=====================[F]  [==========SILL==========] [s] ==tile==tile== FFL
    ::::bed::::::    |                       |     ~~ sub-sill flashing ~~  W   ::::bed::::
    ---membrane---\  |                       |  /--------membrane---------- W   //////////
    ///// slab /////\|                       | ////////////////////////////////// slab ///
    /////////////////\    GUTTER FORMED      / /////////////////////////////////////////
    //////////////////\   IN SUBSTRATE      / //////////////////////////////////////////
    ///////////////////\-----membrane------/ ///////////////////////////////////////////
    ///////////////////////////|      |//////////////////////////////////////////////////
                               |      |  outlet: membrane turned down into it,
                               |      |  drained to the stormwater system

[F] non-ferrous grate frame support; ===== between the [F]s is the removable grate
W   waterstop at the inner edge of the sill, returned to the external face at the jambs
Mandatory (cl 2.8.3): sub-sill flashing within the membrane system; membrane fixed under the
sill and terminated in the stormwater system. Informative: gutter in substrate, grate >= opening,
deck falls away. Fig 2.9 gives NO dimensions.
```

This is the only standards-based level-threshold detail in Australia, and it works for Class 1 and Class 2 under both NCC editions. Under NCC 2025, a Class 2 concrete slab must *also* carry the 70 mm structural set-down and 1:80 slab fall, so this sketch becomes a gutter inside a set-down balcony. The gutter's width, depth and outlet size must come from the hydraulic designer and the drain supplier, because AS 4654.2 is silent. BRANZ (NZ) gives one external reference point: a 12 mm gap between grating and threshold, a channel fall of at least 1:200, and an exterior fall of at least 1:40 away from the channel ([BRANZ Build 151](https://buildmagazine.org.nz/assets/ebooks/B151/files/basic-html/page43.html)).

### Sketch 2: Set-down slab with raised self-draining finish (Swinburne Fig 9; Multiplex 17223; BRANZ BU583; Viking BZ03)

```
INTERNAL                           DOOR                         EXTERNAL BALCONY
                                               10-12 mm gap
FFL ==finish==finish==[s]W[======SILL======]   |  [paver ] [paver ] [paver ] [paver ]  <- paver top = FFL
    /////////////////////W~~~~membrane~~~~~|      ||         ||         ||         ||     open joints
    ///// internal slab /////////////////// |      ||         ||         ||         ||     (self-draining)
    //////////////////////////////////////// |    [ped]      [ped]      [ped]      [ped]
    //////////////////////////////////////// |\==============membrane direct on slab====================> outlet
    //////////////////////////////////////// | \/////////// external slab, falls >= 1:80 ////////////   (membrane
    //////////////////////////////////////// |  ///////////////////////////////////////////////////////   turned in)
                                          ^
                                          membrane up the set-down face; fillet/bond breaker at the base;
                                          sub-sill/tray over the membrane, waterstop W at the inner edge

Set-down at the door: >= 70 mm, internal structural substrate to ADJACENT external structural substrate
(NCC 2025 F1D4(c), Class 2-9 concrete, from 1 May 2027); deeper further out by the fall
(1:80 x 2.4 m = +30 mm). Perimeter: integral hob >= 70 mm with overflow invert >= 20 mm below the
internal floor structural substrate, or edge draining straight to a gutter.
```

This is the build-up NCC 2025 steers Class 2 concrete towards. Swinburne/HIA call an integral set-down and fall in the slab, with the overflow "below the waterproofing line", the way to a successful system ([Swinburne/HIA 2024, p.37](https://hia.com.au/-/media/files/our-industry/advocacy/projects/water-leakage-of-concrete-balconies-in-apartments---an-investigation/swinburne-university-investigation-of-water-leakage-in-residential-apartment-concrete-balconies.pdf)). The pavers sit on pedestals and pass F1D10 as "self-draining". Water reaches the membrane at the door line, so the upturn behind and under the sill does the waterproofing work, which is why the NZ details keep a 100 mm structural step even with a flush finish ([BRANZ BU583](https://d39d3mj7qio96p.cloudfront.net/media/documents/BU583-Waterproof-decks.pdf); [Viking Buzon BZ03](https://www.vikingroofspec.co.nz/media/j43n2enf/viking-buzon-details-full-set.pdf)).

Under AS 4654.2, a flush finish still means the external *finished* level sits above the membrane termination. You therefore either document the arrangement as the clause 2.8.3(b) route (membrane fixed under the sill, draining to the outlet at membrane level through a variable-level inlet) or add a door-line gutter as in Sketch 1, and have the façade engineer declare it. For a Class 1 timber-framed balcony over a habitable room, this is the only authority-style level detail found. BRANZ BU583 drops the plywood deck 100 mm below internal floor level and lays removable panels or slatted timber over the membrane; it is an NZ document, used here for the method only. Tiles bedded directly on a membrane over timber with a level threshold have no published authority detail.

### Sketch 3: Proprietary threshold drain under the door track (Stormtech TDi / TDiS type)

```
EXTERNAL BALCONY           THRESHOLD DRAIN        DOOR TRACK + SUB-SILL                     INTERNAL
                           grate on tray          on packers, fixings over-sealed
FFL ==tile==tile==tile==[c][====GRATE====][c][=========== track / sub-sill ============][r][c]==tile== FFL
    ::::: bed :::::::::  |  |            |  |  ---packers-------------------------------   |   :: bed ::
    -----membrane------\ |  |    tray    |  |/~~~~~~~~ membrane (completed FIRST) ~~~~~~~~~|  /////////
    ////// slab ////////\|  |  23-53 mm  | /////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////
    /////////////////////\  |____________|/ //////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////
    //////////////////////\===membrane to drain flange===/ ///////////////////////////////////////////////
    ///////////////////////////////|    |//////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////////
                                   |    |  primary outlet (+ separate overflow outlet on 100TDiS)

[c] caulking   [r] backing rod.  Aluminium sub-sill separated from the stainless drain by >= 2 mm nylon.
Local slab rebate = track/sub-sill depth (min 30 mm with 100 mm grate, 35 mm with 65 mm grate)
+ tray depth (23-53 mm); Stormtech quotes overall depth up to 120 mm. Channel length >= track + 6 mm.
```

Stormtech's TDi sheet says "The entire door track and TDi are set in above and separate to waterproofing. Therefore waterproofing must be completed first". The membrane is shown running into the recess and "to flange", then across under the sub-sill packers and up the rebate face to finished floor level ([Stormtech TDi](https://stormtech.com.au/documents/TDi-threshold-waterproofing_3.pdf)). The 100TDiS variant makes the drain tray itself the sub-sill, and adds an overflow outlet plumbed separately ([Stormtech 100TDiS](https://stormtech.com.au/documents/100TDiS-threshold.pdf)). These are effectively factory-made Figure 2.9 gutters. The slab still needs a local rebate of roughly 55–120 mm below finished floor level at the door (the researchers' sum of the published component depths), plus the normal set-down and falls across the rest of the balcony. Alternatives include AWS FlowTHRU/FlowTHRU Pro, "a single level set down, flush threshold drainage solution" for selected AWS doors, whose depths sit behind a login ([AWS FlowTHRU Pro](https://awsaustralia.com.au/complementary/flowthru-pro-drain/)), and Aquabocci R-47 Low Profile, which uses an EPDM sleeve under the door track ([Aquabocci R-47](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0246/5497/0958/files/J003221_Aquabocci_-_R47_Low_Profile_PDF_Spec_Sheet_ART1.pdf)). Aquabocci notes the low-profile option suited a *fully covered* balcony.

## Conclusion

The level threshold is a **slab setout decision, not a waterproofing trade problem**. Every compliant route depends on depth cast into the concrete or framing before the membrane applicator arrives: a gutter recess (AS 4654.2 Fig 2.9), a set-down under a raised finish, or a rebate for a proprietary drain. Once the slab is poured flat, no membrane product can make it compliant. NCC 2025 does not invent a new rule so much as write that logic into the DTS for Class 2 concrete. A NSW builder pricing a Class 2 job now that may run past 1 May 2027 is safest designing to the 2025 geometry (70 mm structural set-down, 1:80 slab fall, integral hob, overflow 20 mm below the internal slab) with a door-line gutter or drain on top. That satisfies both editions. Houses keep the 2022 rules, but the H2D8 wording means a level concrete balcony over a habitable room gets *more* scrutiny than a stepped one, not less.

The weak points are not in the code text. They are in the gaps between documents: no standard dimensions the door gutter, no inspection stage covers the external membrane, and three separately declared designs (waterproofing, enclosure, structure) must agree on one 100 mm-deep junction. The regulator has already shown it will treat missing door drainage and overflows as serious defects, and the 1A Eden result shows owners bearing the cost when a developer fails. A builder therefore protects itself best with paperwork it is not strictly required to keep: a façade engineer's declared termination detail, the drain and membrane makers' written acceptance of their interface, the door's labelled AS 2047 rating for the actual sill, and a dated flood test before a single tile goes down.
