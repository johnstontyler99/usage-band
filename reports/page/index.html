A level balcony door is compliant in NSW only if the slab still steps down

Under NCC 2022 (in force now) both houses (H2D8) and apartments (F1D5) send you to AS 4654.2-2012. Its one binding route for a flush door is clause 2.8.3: a sub-sill flashing inside the membrane system, and where levels leave no room for an upturn, the membrane fixed under the sill and run into the stormwater system. In practice that is the Figure 2.9 detail: a grated gutter formed in the slab across the full door width, with the deck falling away from it. From 1 May 2027, NCC 2025 also makes Class 2 concrete balconies step down 70 mm in the structure.

NCC 2022 governs until 1 May 2027, and it sets no numbers of its own

The NSW Government says the current edition is NCC 2022 Amendment 2 (from 29 July 2025), and NSW adopts NCC 2025 on 1 May 2027. The ABCB published NCC 2025 on 1 May 2026 and lists the NSW transition as "N/A"; the NSW release calls the year a "12-month transition period". Confirm the assessed edition with your certifier on any job that straddles the date. NSW makes no variation to the external waterproofing clauses. Its one announced NCC 2025 waterproofing variation lets owners doing remedial work on existing apartments choose either edition.

Class 2 (Volume One). F1P2: water from a 1% AEP storm "must not enter the building". F3P1: roofs and walls, "including openings around windows and doors", must prevent damaging water penetration. F1D5: a "roof, balcony, podium or similar horizontal surface" membrane to AS 4654.1 materials, designed and installed to AS 4654.2. F1D4: exposed joints per AS 4654.2 Section 2.9. F1D2 exempts timber or perforated decking and balconies directly above ground. D3D16(d) actually allows a sill up to 190 mm onto a balcony, which Building Commission NSW calls "a concession intended for weatherproofing".

Class 1 (Volume Two). H2P2 is the Performance Requirement. H2D8 calls up AS 4654.1/.2 for balconies "located above internal spaces", but excludes "a concrete slab that has a minimum step-down of 50 mm below the internal floor level" and spaced decking. So a level-threshold concrete balcony over a habitable room gets no exclusion and must meet AS 4654.2 in full. This is a literal reading; confirm it with your certifier. Glazed doors to AS 2047 under H1D8. H2D8 and H2P2 are word-for-word the same in NCC 2025.

Any departure from AS 4654.2 is a Performance Solution (NSW A2G2). No Verification Method covers balcony membranes, so it rests on Expert Judgement or comparison with the DTS.

NCC 2025 for Class 2–9 concrete balconies, from 1 May 2027

  • F1D4(b): structural substrate "graded with a minimum fall of 1:80".
  • F1D4(c): "a minimum 70 mm step down" from the internal floor structural substrate to the adjacent external structural substrate, "where applicable".
  • F1D4(d): a monolithic hob integral with the slab, "at least 70 mm high", at the outer perimeter, unless the edge drains to a gutter.
  • F1D4 note: overflow invert "not less than 20 mm below" the adjacent internal floor structural substrate. A tile bed, screed or topping "is not considered a structural substrate". The 70 mm is not the AS 4654.2 termination height, which "may require … a step down greater than 70 mm".
  • F1D7(2): membrane directly on the structural substrate. F1D10: finishes self-draining or directly fixed to the membrane. F1D6: exposed joints at the high point. F1D14: AS 2047 doors.

A flush finished threshold still passes, over a slab set down at least 70 mm. What disappears is the level slab with falls and the step made in screed. HIA reads it the same way. The ABCB flags the access clash: D4D2 "may prevent gradients steeper than 1:40".

AS 4654.2 allows a flush door through one binding sentence

The standard is paywalled. Clause wording here was checked against two licensed public reproductions: the QBCC Technical Feature (Feb 2017) and Building Commission NSW's Building Defects Library (v1.1, June 2025). No amendment has been issued. "Shall" text binds on the DTS route. Notes and informative appendices are "only for information and guidance".

Termination height is a performance rule. Cl 2.8.1.1: the membrane height above the finished surface level "shall be sufficient to prevent water, including wind driven, flowing over the top". The numbers are only in informative Appendix A, Table A1, measured above the external tile. Building Commission NSW: "The height of membrane termination should be determined by the façade engineer". At a flush door those heights would have to rise above the internal floor, so a level threshold cannot comply by upturn height; it has to use cl 2.8.3(b).

Cl 2.8.3, the level-threshold rule: "(a) Sub-sill flashing shall be included as part of the membrane system" and "(b) Where the internal and external finished floor levels do not allow an upturn, the membranes shall be fixed under the sill and terminate in the stormwater system". Notes: the deck "should fall away from the grate" and the grate be "to the width of or greater than the opening" (3); openings "should be provided with a set-down or hob" (5); where that isn't possible, e.g. wheelchair access, "a gutter should be formed into the substrate immediately in front of the opening" (6); AS 2047 governs fixings to seals and frames (7).

The gutter is a "should", but it is the only way the standard shows to "terminate in the stormwater system" at a door. QBCC calls level balconies "not prohibited" but "more problematic to construct and achieve compliance". A flush door that relies only on the deck falling away, with no drained gutter, needs a Performance Solution. Fig 2.8 (Options 1–3) is a step-down detail, not a level one.

Other clauses that bind

  • Falls, cl 2.5.2: no retained water except surface-tension residue; fall in the structural substrate or a screed over it. "No flatter than 1 in 100" is a note; certifiers treat it as the minimum.
  • Drains, cl 2.10: membrane connected by turn-down or flange/clamping ring; drain has a sump, pit, grate or cage; notes call for a variable-level inlet draining "at the membrane level".
  • Overflows, cl 2.11: membrane turned into the overflow; finished floor must not reduce outlet flow. No overflow height or outlet count is set.
  • Build-up: fillets at every change of plane (notes: 15 × 15 mm liquid, 40 × 40 mm sheet), cl 2.7; bond breakers where movement is expected, cl 2.12; unbonded 100 mm over joints, cl 2.9; bonded beds jointed (ideally 10 mm at 4.5 m), unbonded toppings ≥50 mm on a double slip sheet (note), cl 2.15.2; penetrating fixings sealed, cl 2.8.4.
  • Testing, cl 2.16: inspection or acceptance testing on completion; note suggests dry-film thickness or a ≥24 h controlled water test.
  • Materials: AS 4654.1, movement Class I (<60% elongation), II (60–299%) or III (≥300%).

The door, the drains and the tiles each have their own test

AS 2047 is a second, independent check. Framed sliding, swinging, French and bi-fold glazed doors need an AS 2047 water-penetration rating (Class 2: F3D4, F1D14 from 2027; Class 1: H1D8). Frameless and one-off site-built doors are excluded. The test is 30% of the positive SLS wind pressure, minimum 150 Pa. At about 0.1 mm of water head per Pa, that is roughly 15 mm at 150 Pa, 31 mm at 300 Pa and 61 mm at 600 Pa. That is why flush sills need drained or compressed-seal designs.

Published flush-sill ratings: AWS SlideMASTER recessed sill (Series 703) "Maximum Water 600Pa" with floating self-draining tiles over the track; Alspec ProGlide UltraFlat "630Pa" with an AS 1428.1 sill. Alspec itself warns sills that comply with AS 1428.1 "may not meet the minimum Water Penetration Resistance in AS2047". Passing AS 2047 does not satisfy the AS 4654.2 sill and waterstop rules. Check the label on the sill configuration actually installed.

AS/NZS 3500.3 sizes the drains (NCC F1D3). The 2018 edition (cl 3.8) designs balcony drainage for a 20-year ARI (5% AEP) and overflow for a 100-year ARI (1% AEP); which edition NSW now calls up was not verified. No standard sets a minimum number of outlets, but a 2024 NSW rectification order treated "Only one overflow pipe … for the 240 sqm balcony" as a serious defect. Do not treat the door gutter as the overflow: give enclosed balconies an independent overflow below door level.

Tiling: AS 3958:2023 (Amd 1:2024) replaced AS 3958.1-2007; external movement joints at about 4.5 m where a dimension exceeds 4.5 m (2007 numbering). No reliable AS 3958 external fall figure was found. AS 3740 matters only if the room inside is a wet area.

Councils, not the NCC, push NSW balcony doors to level

NSW has opted out of the NCC livable housing provisions. Part H8 (Class 1a) and Part G7 (Class 2 units) "have deliberately been left blank" in both NCC 2022 and NCC 2025. Even where H8 applies it targets one step-free entrance, not balcony doors. The Apartment Design Guide's 20% Livable Housing Silver benchmark (Objective 4Q-1) is planning guidance since SEPP 65 moved into the Housing SEPP. Silver covers the entrance (5 mm, or a ramped threshold under 56 mm; "grated drain along threshold" advised), not balconies.

The binding drivers are council DCPs and the access reports that become consent documents. Many councils require 10–25% of units to be adaptable to AS 4299: "Maximum 3mm vertical step or a 5mm bevelled step at entry". Examples written into consents:

  • Lane Cove: "All balcony levels to all the adaptable units will have a 10mm step down", threshold ramps, 1:40 balcony fall.
  • A Sydney SSD project: "maximum step downs of 35mm".
  • Blacktown: "35mm threshold ramp and balcony to be sloped at max. 1:40 to achieve 100mm drop at the end".

Class 2 common-area terraces and podiums are governed by NCC Part D4 and AS 1428.1 as law (threshold or step ramp under D3D16(c)). The NCC access provisions don't apply inside units.

NSW law adds declarations and enforcement, but no balcony inspection

Class 2 (and Class 3, 9c since July 2023): regulated designs. Waterproofing is a building element under s 6(1) of the Design and Building Practitioners Act 2020. Balcony waterproofing needs a regulated design and a design compliance declaration ("W") from a registered design practitioner before the CC, and a building compliance declaration before the OC application. The regulator's minimum: plans showing membrane areas, "balcony/roof gradients" and "Location of floor wastes/overflow"; sections showing membrane extent, "Termination of waterproofing membrane" and "Overflow details" at 1:50 with 1:20, 1:10 or 1:5 details. Door flashings and sill heights sit in the building-enclosure design, set-down and falls in the structural and architectural setout. A level threshold is where those three designs must agree. There is no separate waterproofing practitioner class.

The RAB Act has already been used on this detail. The July 2024 Baulkham Hills order listed "no visible overflow provisions", "Water ponding as evidence of inadequate falls" and "the absence of adequate drainage system at the door to prevent water entering the building" as serious defects. In The Owners – SP 92226 v 1A Eden [2026] NSWSC 563, leaks from "a lack of a barrier underneath the balcony doors and inadequate overflow sizes" produced a $2.5 m judgment; with the developer in liquidation the estimated dividend was $308,406 and owners raised a $4.5 m levy.

  • Licensing: waterproofing is trade work needing a contractor licence above $5,000 (labour and materials, incl. GST). The stormwater connection of a door gutter is drainage work: specialist work, licensed at any value.
  • Warranties: waterproofing is a "major element". A leak is a 6-year major defect only if it meets the s 18E consequences test; otherwise 2 years (Ashton v Stevenson).
  • Inspections: mandatory critical stage inspections (DCFS Regulation 2021 s 61) cover waterproofing "in a wet area" only, plus stormwater connections before covering. No mandatory stage covers an external balcony membrane, so set your own hold point.
  • Reforms: the Building (Approvals and Practitioners) Act 2026 (assent 14 Aug 2026, not yet proclaimed) will replace CCs and OCs with building and completion approvals and repeal the DBP Act. From 1 Dec 2026 new apartment buildings of more than two units move to a strata bond or decennial liability insurance (start not re-confirmed after 12 Nov 2026).

What not to build

The threshold is decided when the slab is set out

Every compliant route depends on depth cast into the concrete or framing before the membrane applicator arrives: a gutter recess, a set-down under a raised finish, or a rebate for a proprietary drain. Once the slab is poured flat, no membrane product can make it compliant. For a Class 2 job that may run past 1 May 2027, design to the 2025 geometry now (70 mm structural set-down, 1:80 slab fall, integral hob, overflow 20 mm below the internal slab) with a door-line gutter or drain on top. That satisfies both editions. Houses keep the 2022 rules, but H2D8 means a level concrete balcony over a habitable room gets more scrutiny than a stepped one.

Paperwork that protects the builder

  • Façade engineer's declared termination detail for the flush door.
  • Drain and membrane makers' written acceptance of their interface.
  • The door's labelled AS 2047 rating for the actual sill.
  • A dated flood test and photos before a tile goes down.
