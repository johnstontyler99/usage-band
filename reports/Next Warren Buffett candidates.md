# Buffett's Real Heirs Compound Far From Omaha

The best answer to "who is the next Warren Buffett" is **Bruce Flatt of Brookfield**, not one of the names usually offered. He is the only living investor who has all three of the things a successor needs. His own 24-year tenure compounded at **roughly 20% a year per share**. He runs a **$100bn-plus permanent-capital company** that is now adding insurance float, as Berkshire did. And at 61 he has a plausible 15-20 years of runway.

Three other names come close but miss in different ways:

- **Chris Hohn of TCI** is the closest heir as a pure stock-picker, at about 18% a year net since 2004, roughly double the S&P 500.
- **Ken Griffin's Citadel** (19.19% net a year over 35 years) and **Renaissance's Medallion fund** have the strongest raw records. Neither looks anything like Buffett in method or structure.
- **Prem Watsa's Fairfax** (19.5% a year for 40 years) is the closest existing copy of Berkshire, but Watsa belongs to Buffett's generation, not the next one.

The usual candidates mostly fail on the numbers. Pershing Square has trailed the S&P 500 since it listed in 2012. Fundsmith's lifetime lead over its index has shrunk below one point a year. Li Lu's famous 26% figure is unaudited and ends in 2010. Inside Berkshire, no one is set up to be the next stock-picking Buffett.

The real top 20 is global, and much of it is unknown to US investors. It includes Brazil's Dynamo (about 19% a year in dollars for 33 years), South Africa's Allan Gray (a 52-year record), India's Prashant Jain and China's Zhu Shaoxing. No one matches Buffett's 61-year record, which left Berkshire shareholders with **about 132 times the wealth of an index investor**. Nearly every record also fades once outside money floods in. The lasting lesson is that Buffett-like compounding comes from permanent or capped capital as much as from stock-picking genius.

## Buffett's real bar is 61 years and a 132-fold lead

**The headline record.** Berkshire's per-share market value compounded at **19.7% a year from 1965 through 2025, against 10.5% for the S&P 500 with dividends**. That is a cumulative gain of **6,099,294% against 46,061%** ([Berkshire 2025 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf)). Dividing one by the other, a Berkshire holder ended with about 132 times the wealth of an index holder (computed). Before Berkshire, the Buffett Partnership earned about 29.5% a year gross and 23.8% for limited partners from 1957 to 1969, against 7.4% for the Dow ([Picture Perfect Portfolios, secondary](https://pictureperfectportfolios.com/why-buffett-partnership-returns-were-extraordinary/)).

**Most of the edge came early.** Berkshire's own annual series shows the gap over the index shrinking from about 27 points a year in 1975-84 to slightly negative over the last decade.

| Window | Berkshire | S&P 500 total return | Gap (points a year) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1975-1984 | 41.4% | 14.6% | +26.7 |
| 1985-1994 | 31.9% | 14.4% | +17.6 |
| 1995-2004 | 15.7% | 12.1% | +3.6 |
| 2005-2014 | 9.9% | 7.7% | +2.2 |
| Last 30 years (1996-2025) | 11.1% | 10.4% | +0.7 |
| Last 20 years (2006-2025) | 11.3% | 11.0% | +0.3 |
| Last 10 years (2016-2025) | 14.3% | 14.8% | -0.5 |

*Computed from the annual table in the [Berkshire 2025 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf).*

**The statistics are solid but not overwhelming.** Annual excess returns over the full 61 years give a t-statistic of about **3.2**. The excess since 1990, taken alone, has a t-statistic of about **0.9**, which cannot be told apart from zero (computed from the same table). Buffett's achievement was to carry an early, very large edge across six decades without blowing up. He did not beat the market by much in every decade. Any successor should be compared with the early Buffett on rate and with the late Buffett on scale.

**What drove the record.** Frazzini, Kabiller and Pedersen's study "Buffett's Alpha" covers 1976 to 2017 ([Financial Analysts Journal, 2018](https://gwern.net/doc/economics/2018-frazzini.pdf)). It finds:

- a **Sharpe ratio of 0.79** against the market's 0.49, with a beta of 0.69;
- average leverage of **about 1.7 to 1**, funded by insurance float that cost about 1.72% a year, **2.97 points below Treasury bills**;
- an annual alpha that falls from **13.4% to 5.4% and loses statistical significance** once two standard factors are added: betting-against-beta (low-risk stocks) and quality-minus-junk (high-quality stocks).

In short, Buffett levered cheap, safe, high-quality stocks with cheap money that nobody could call. He also survived a **44% fall from June 1998 to February 2000 while the market rose 32%** (same source). That gives the job description for an heir:

- a real edge in finding cheap, high-quality businesses;
- cheap, permanent capital that cannot be withdrawn;
- a structure that can survive a 40-point shortfall against the market;
- decades of runway.

**How this report judges records.** A short record cannot prove that kind of edge. Take a manager with an information ratio of 0.5, which is excellent. That manager needs about **16 years** of results to reach a t-statistic of 2 (computed as T = (t/IR)²). Skill also rarely persists. S&P's persistence scorecard found that only **2% of top-quartile small-cap funds** stayed in the top quartile over five years ([S&P Dow Jones Indices](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/spiva/persistence-scorecard-year-end-2025.pdf)).

This report therefore works as follows:

- It ranks only records of roughly 20 years or more.
- Returns are net of fees wherever possible.
- Each record is compared with a local total-return benchmark and converted to dollars where the currency distorts the picture.
- It gives weight to scale, to independent verification and to how the manager performed after becoming famous.

Returns earned by actual investors matter too. ARK's ETFs posted spectacular headline returns, yet they destroyed an estimated **$14.3bn** of shareholder value over the decade to 2023, because most of the money arrived at the peak ([Reuters via Yahoo Finance](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/cathie-woods-ark-invest-destroyed-223315637.html)).

**The heir is not inside Berkshire.**

- **Greg Abel** became CEO on January 1, 2026 and took over the annual letter. In it he wrote that "at Berkshire's scale, the math of compounding works against us" ([2025 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf)). He is an operator and capital allocator, and his record cannot be separated from that of Berkshire Hathaway Energy, the group's utility arm.
- **Buffett** became chairman emeritus on September 18, 2026. **Howard Buffett** became chairman, explicitly to "guard its culture and values" ([Berkshire release](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/news/sep1826.pdf)).
- **Todd Combs** left in December 2025 to run JPMorgan's $10bn Strategic Investment Group ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/12/08/warren-buffett-berkshire-jpmorgan)).
- **Ted Weschler** manages only about 6% of Berkshire's investments ([2025 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf)). His personal retirement account grew from about $70,000 in 1989 to $264.4m by 2018 ([Business Insider, citing ProPublica](https://www.businessinsider.nl/warren-buffetts-deputy-grew-his-retirement-account-from-70000-to-264-million-in-29-years-propublica-reports/)), roughly 33% a year (computed). But that is small, tax-free, unaudited personal money, not a public record.

## The usual "next Buffetts" fail on audited numbers

**The label has a poor history.** The same pattern repeats each time. A strong 10-15 year record on modest capital earns the label and draws in money that can be withdrawn. Concentration, leverage or illiquid holdings then turn one bad bet into a collapse.

- **Eddie Lampert.** BusinessWeek asked in 2004 whether he was "The Next Warren Buffett," after his fund averaged about 29% a year from 1988 to 2004 ([BusinessWeek via Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/355817248/The-Next-Warren-Buffett-Businessweek)). Sears, where he concentrated the fund, filed for bankruptcy in 2018.
- **Bill Miller** beat the S&P 500 for 15 straight years ([Fortune](https://money.cnn.com/2008/03/05/news/newsmakers/lashinsky_miller.fortune/)), then lost about 55% in 2008 ([Baltimore Sun](https://baltimoresun.com/business/bs-xpm-2013-06-29-bs-bz-peters-20130629-story.html)).
- **Bruce Berkowitz**, Morningstar's fund manager of the decade, lost 32% in 2011 while the S&P 500 gained 2.1%. His Fairholme fund shrank from about $20bn to $2.2bn ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2017/06/29/fairholme-funds-berkowitz--abysmal-performance-this-decade.html)).
- **Neil Woodford**, "Britain's Buffett," had his flagship fund suspended in 2019 after filling a daily-dealing fund with hard-to-sell holdings ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Woodford)).
- **The Sequoia Fund**, which Buffett once recommended, let Valeant grow to about 30% of its assets ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2016/03/24/sequoia-chief-resigns-valeant/)).

**Today's favourites fail more quietly.**

- **Bill Ackman** says he wants Howard Hughes to become a "modern-day Berkshire Hathaway" ([Reuters via AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/bill-ackman-bets-2-1b-213605031.html)). It closed the $2.1bn purchase of insurer Vantage in June 2026 ([Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/2312782/howard-hughes-holdings-announces-closing-of-vantage-group-holdings-acquisition)). But Howard Hughes has returned only **5.45% a year since 2010 against 13.65%** for the S&P 500 ([totalrealreturns](https://totalrealreturns.com/n/BX,KKR,DHR,TDY,HHH,SPY)). Pershing Square's net asset value has compounded a respectable **16.2% a year since 2004 against 10.7%**. Since Pershing Square Holdings listed at the end of 2012, though, it has made **12.9% against the index's 14.9%** ([PSH 2025 annual report](https://assets.pershingsquareholdings.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/18175039/Pershing-Square-Holdings-Ltd.-2025-Annual-Report.pdf)). His whole lead was earned before he became famous.
- **Terry Smith's Fundsmith Equity** has trailed the MSCI World index for five calendar years in a row. From launch to July 2026 it made about **13.1% a year against 12.3%** (computed). Assets have fallen from £29bn to £12bn, and Smith now says he "will take more account of momentum" ([Fidelity UK](https://www.fidelity.co.uk/markets-insights/investing-ideas/funds/important-changes-at-fundsmith-equity/)).
- **Li Lu's** widely quoted 26.4% a year for 1998-2010 is unaudited and now 16 years old ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya_Capital)). Himalaya's disclosed US holdings are about $3.6bn in nine stocks ([Fintel](https://fintel.io/i/himalaya-capital-management)).
- **Mohnish Pabrai's** PIF2 fund made 12.1% net against 6.4% from 2000 to 2019. But PIF3 lost **60.9% in 2008** and PIF4 trailed the S&P 500 ([Pabrai January 2020 letter](https://snowballing-co.s3.amazonaws.com/media/l_010120.pdf)).
- **Seth Klarman's Baupost** once made about 20% a year, but has made **about 4% a year since 2014** ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-01-22/baupost-clients-pulled-7-billion-since-2021-after-poor-returns)).
- **Markel**, the archetypal "baby Berkshire," has returned 12.71% a year since 1993 against 10.58% for the S&P 500 ([totalrealreturns](https://totalrealreturns.com/n/MKL,L,WTM,BRK-A,BN,SPY)).

**Overseas, the "local Buffett" nicknames rarely rest on a record.**

- **Duan Yongping** invests his own money and publishes no return series. Apple was about 41% of his US holdings in mid-2026 ([Valuesider](https://valuesider.com/insights/2026-q2/duan-yongping-aapl-outsized-position)).
- **Zhang Lei's** oft-cited ~40% a year at Hillhouse is an unaudited claim covering only 2005-2015 ([24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2025/03/01/yale-backed-billionaire-investor-is-bullish-on-these-stocks-right-now/)). Assets in his public-equity unit's pooled funds fell 33% in 2024 ([Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/private-equity/hillhouse-public-unit-reports-33-decline-in-fund-assets-in-2024)).
- **Rakesh Jhunjhunwala's** "65 per cent" compound return describes a personal, leveraged fortune that began with ₹5,000 of borrowed money ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/opinion/rakesh-jhunjhunwala-a-perspective-122082800949_1.html)). It is not a fund record.

**Many genuine Buffett disciples only matched the market after fees.** Tom Russo's partnership made **11.5% a year net from 1990 to 2026 against 10.8%** for the S&P 500 ([Ivey 2026 deck](https://www.ivey.uwo.ca/media/txmd1swc/20-thomas-russo.pdf)). Guy Spier's Aquamarine made about **9.4% against 9.2%** before winding down ([Opalesque](https://www.opalesque.com/713551/Guy_Spier_winds_down_Aquamarine_Fund_after_28355.html)). Copying Buffett's style is not the same as copying his results.

**Who is included.** Living investors, and still-operating firms, whose records ran into this century.

**How they are ranked.** On return and lead over the benchmark across roughly 20 years or more, dollars compounded, quality of evidence, and results after the manager became famous.

**Who is treated as a yardstick instead.** Single-industry operating companies and records that ended long ago are discussed separately, not ranked.

### Eight records match Buffett's rate at institutional scale

**Medallion is the one record that beats Buffett outright.** Gregory Zuckerman's book data show a **66.1% average gross return and 39.1% net from 1988 to 2018**. Its Sharpe ratio was above 2, and $100 grew to $398.7m before fees, against $1,910 in the market ([Cornell Capital](https://www.cornell-capital.com/blog/2020/02/medallion-fund-the-ultimate-counterexample.html)). That implies a t-statistic of roughly 11 (computed), against Buffett's 3.2. But Medallion is not Buffett-like:

- it has been closed to outsiders since 1993;
- it has been kept to roughly $10bn rather than allowed to grow;
- the LCH rankings once excluded Renaissance because LCH "could not obtain audited information" ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsxp29fq05q5bscm6znk/corner-office/the-top-earning-hedge-fund-firms-of-all-time)).

**Griffin has the strongest record that is verified, open to outsiders and huge.** Citadel Wellington made **19.19% net a year from November 1990 through 2025**, according to a credit-rating agency ([KBRA](https://www.kbra.com/publications/tjxcVPXk/kbra-affirms-issuer-ratings-for-citadel-s-flagship-multi-strategy-funds-and-affirms-assigns-ratings-to-senior-unsecured-notes-issued-by-citadel-finance-llc?format=web)). That compounds to about 474 times the starting capital (computed). Over the last 30 years, Berkshire made about 11.1% a year. Citadel has made **$90.4bn for its investors, more than any other hedge fund in history** ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/chris-hohns-tci-tops-hedge-fund-gains-2025)), and was up 15.3% in 2026 through October ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/citadel-bucks-the-trend-with-october-gain)).

Griffin is not a business owner, though. Wellington is a leveraged, multi-strategy trading fund. About 70% of its investors can each withdraw a sixteenth of their capital every quarter ([KBRA 2025](https://www.kbra.com/publications/gdbMxFGn)). Its 2008 loss could not be verified for this report.

**Watsa's Fairfax is the closest thing to a second Berkshire.** Since 1985 its stock has compounded at **19.5% a year and its book value at 18.7%** (dividends included). That compares with 11.5% for the S&P 500 and 9.0% for Canada's TSX. Like Berkshire, it invests insurance float, which now totals **$40.8bn**. Fairfax says it ranks seventh of about 600 surviving US-listed companies on stock return since 1985 ([Fairfax 2025 letter](https://www.fairfax.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/FFH_Fairfax-Financials-Shareholders-Letter-2025-with-attachments-092704.pdf)). The flaws are lumpiness and age:

- book value grew only 3.8% a year in 2011-15 and 5.6% in 2016-20 (same source);
- the stock fell roughly 78% on year-end prices from 1998 to 2002 (computed);
- Watsa is 76 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prem_Watsa)).

**Leonard's Constellation Software has the highest return of any large, long record.** From the 2006 IPO the share price compounded at roughly **28.7% a year** through November 2026. That figure is computed, and the IPO price could not be confirmed. REQ Capital put total return, with dividends, at 37% a year to mid-2025 ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/tsx/CSU/); [REQ](https://req.no/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/REQ-Acquisition-driven-Compounders-July-2025.pdf)). The S&P 500 ETF made 10.6% a year over the same period ([totalrealreturns](https://totalrealreturns.com/n/TDG,SPY)).

Leonard's record is now frozen:

- he resigned for health reasons in September 2025 and left the board in May 2026 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-25/constellation-software-falls-as-founder-mark-leonard-resigns); [Ground News](https://ground.news/article/constellation-software-inc-announces-mark-leonards-decision-to-not-stand-for-re-election-to-board-of-directors));
- the shares were about 51% below their high by February 2026 ([nai500](https://nai500.com/blog/2026/02/constellation-software-plunges-51-sparking-debate-on-ai-fear-and-value/));
- third-quarter 2026 organic growth was only 2% ([Dow Jones via Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/dow-jones/2026110612836/constellation-software-reports-higher-3q-profit-boosted-by-acquisitions)).

**The next four complete the top eight.**

- **Druckenmiller** made about **30% a year from 1986 to 2010 with no losing year** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Druckenmiller)), including about +11% in 2008. He also ran George Soros's Quantum Fund from 1988 to 2000. He closed Duquesne at more than $12bn, saying that managing more than $10bn "seems to challenge my long-term standard" ([InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/alternatives/stanley-druckenmiller-closing-up-shop/30897)). The 30% figure has never been audited publicly.
- **Flatt's Brookfield** turned **$1m invested in August 1993 into $285.2m, or 19% a year against 11%** for the S&P 500 ([Brookfield Q4 2025 letter](https://bn.brookfield.com/sites/brookfield-bn-v2/files/BN-IR-Master/Letter-to-Shareholders/2026/Q4-25%20BN%20Letter%20to%20Shareholders_vF.pdf)). During his own tenure, from 2002 to April 2022, the shares returned more than 3,700% against about 500% for the index ([WealthManagement, citing Bloomberg](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/alternative-investments/brookfield-billionaire-flatt-reveals-secret-behind-3-700-return)). That is about 20% a year against 9.4% (computed).
- **Hohn's TCI** has compounded at **about 18% a year net since 2004, roughly double the S&P 500** ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bstnug68kijmzi8f50cg/culture/chris-hohn-is-a-hedge-fund-manager-like-no-other)). After a record **$18.9bn** gain in 2025, its $68.4bn of lifetime gains for investors rank fifth among all hedge funds ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/chris-hohns-tci-tops-hedge-fund-gains-2025)).
- **Tepper's Appaloosa** made about **25% a year net from 1993 to 2019** before handing back outside money, and was up 32% in the first half of 2026 ([Motley Fool](https://fool.com/investing/2026/10/17/billionaire-david-tepper-returned-32-percent-top-3/)). No primary source confirms the long-run figure, which is why it ranks below the others.

### Emerging markets hide Buffett-sized margins

**Dynamo Cougar, based in Rio, comes closest to Buffett's rate and lead when measured in dollars and net of fees.** Since September 1993 it has returned **+32,136% against +954% for Brazil's Ibovespa index**, or about **19.0% a year against 7.4%** (computed from [Dynamo Report 131](https://www.dynamo.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Dynamo-Report-131.pdf)). The gains are front-loaded: 1993-94 alone multiplied capital about 4.8 times. Even excluding those years, it made about 14.1% a year against 5.8%. Since 2007 it has made about 6.1% against -0.8% (computed, same source). The fund holds R$5.35bn and is closed to new money ([Dynamo](https://www.dynamo.com.br/en/dynamo-cougar-fif/)). When it briefly reopened in 2022, demand filled the window within minutes ([Exame](https://exame.com/insight/dynamo-capta-r-450-milhoes-em-menos-de-dois-minutos/p)).

**Allan Gray in Cape Town has the longest high-return record found anywhere.** R10,000 invested in its equity mandates in June 1974 had grown to **R518.4m by September 2026**, against R24.3m in the JSE ([Allan Gray Q3 2026 commentary](https://www.allangray.co.za/globalassets/documents-repository/manco/quarterly-commentary/Allan%20Gray%20Unit%20Trust%20Management%20Limited/Files/2026-Q3.pdf)). That is about 23.1% a year against 16.1% (computed). Three caveats apply:

- the figures are before fees, in a currency that has fallen steadily;
- on rough, unverified exchange rates, the record is about 15.5-16% a year in dollars, against about 9% for the JSE;
- the composite trailed the JSE over the latest ten years, at 9.9% against 11.5%.

The Allan Gray Equity Fund, measured after fees, has made **18.9% a year since 1998 against 14.1%** for its benchmark ([factsheet](https://www.allangray.co.za/globalassets/documents-repository/fund/factsheet/Allan%20Gray%20Equity%20Fund/Latest/Allan%20Gray%20Equity%20Fund%20Latest.pdf)).

**India's best is Prashant Jain.** He ran HDFC Prudence, now HDFC Balanced Advantage, for 28 years. Although it is a balanced fund holding both stocks and bonds, it compounded at about **17.9% a year against 9.6% for the Sensex** from 1994 to 2022 ([FLAME Investment Lab](https://www.flame.edu.in/academics/flame-investment-lab/resources/prashant-jain)). When he left HDFC he was chief investment officer over more than ₹4 lakh crore ([Cafemutual](https://cafemutual.com/news/industry/25008-prashant-jain-bids-adieu-to-hdfc-mf-after-28-remarkable-years)). HDFC Flexi Cap, which he ran until July 2022, has made **18.87% a year since 1995 against 12.50%** for its index ([HDFC presentation](https://files.hdfcfund.com/s3fs-public/Others/2025-12/HDFC%20Flexi%20Cap%20Fund%20Presentation%20(November%202025).pdf)). In dollars that is roughly 15.1% against 8.9% (computed using [RBI](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23957) and [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/capital-market-news/inr-crashes-hits-beyond-historic-rs-90-per-dollar-mark-125120300927_1.html) exchange-rate data). That fund's pre-2003 years come before his tenure.

**China's Zhu Shaoxing has stayed with one public fund for 20 years.** Fullgoal Tianhui Growth has returned roughly **15.7% a year** under him ([Eastmoney](https://caifuhao.eastmoney.com/news/20260121235319372328740)). Over nearly the same span the CSI 300 index rose 381%, about 8.3% a year (computed from [Sina](https://cj.sina.com.cn/articles/view/7459098486/1bc98cf7600101du9a)). That index figure probably excludes dividends, which flatters Zhu's lead somewhat.

**Cheah Cheng Hye has the longest verified dollar record in Greater China.** His Value Partners Classic Fund has made **13.2% a year net since April 1993, against 8.1%** for its index ([Value Partners factsheet](https://www.valuepartners-group.com/ftp/files/reports/vpaf/001_fact_sheet/eng/vpaf_fact_sheet_2026-05-01_en.pdf)).

### Boutiques and veterans round out the list

- **John Malone.** His TCI returned **30.3% a year from 1973 to 1998, against 14.3%** for the S&P 500. Liberty Media made about 24% a year against 10.8% from 2006 to 2018. These figures come from secondary summaries of William Thorndike's book *The Outsiders* and from Barron's ([Shortform](https://shortform.com/summary/the-outsiders-eight-unconventional-ceos-summary-william-thorndike); [GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/798437)). His companies have stagnated since 2020 ([Third Pillar Value](https://thirdpillarvalue.substack.com/p/what-i-learned-from-following-a-legend)).
- **Anthony Bolton** compounded Fidelity Special Situations at **19.5% a year net for 28 years** to 2007, turning £1,000 into £147,000. Over that time it became Britain's largest open-ended fund ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Bolton)).
- **Turtle Creek**, a concentrated mid-cap investor in Toronto founded in 1998, turned **$1,000 into $88,517 by September 2023** ([Value Research](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/stories/54080/the-maverick-of-value-investing/)). That is about 19.7% a year (computed), against the 8.3% for the S&P 500 that its founder, Andrew Brenton, cites ([TIP podcast](https://theinvestorspodcast.com/episodes/outperforming-the-market-managing-risk-market-inefficiencies-andrew-brenton)). It is a Buffett-sized lead, but it rests on figures the manager reports himself.
- **Ron Baron's** Baron Partners has an SEC-registered record of **15.93% a year net since 1992, against 10.22%** for its Russell benchmark. It carries 12.3% leverage, and Tesla and SpaceX make up 55% of the portfolio ([Baron Q4 2025 letter](https://www.baroncapitalgroup.com/article/quarterly-letter-baron-partners-fund-q4-2025)). That concentration breaks every Buffett-style position limit.
- **Francisco García Paramés** compounded Bestinver's Bestinfond at 15.7% a year in euros from 1993 to 2014 ([Fondium](https://fondium.com/rentabilidad-historica-de-francisco-garcia-parames-en-bestinver)). AQR's independent "superstar investors" study puts 1994-2014 at 13.5% ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/White-Papers/AQR-More-Superstar-Investors-Parames-A4.pdf?sc_lang=en)). His newer firm, Cobas, started weakly ([Alpha-File](https://alphafile.substack.com/p/francisco-garcia-parames-bestinver)).
- **François Rochon's** model portfolio made **14.7% a year against 9.9% since 1993**, before management fees, then had a poor 2025: +2.7% against +13.7% for his benchmark ([Giverny 2025 letter](https://givernycapital.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/giverny-capital-annual-letter-2025-1.pdf)).
- **Dev Kantesaria's** Valley Forge holds nine near-monopoly businesses such as FICO, Moody's and S&P Global. Barron's reports returns close to **15% a year since 2007, more than five points above the S&P 500** ([24/7 Wall St. citing Barron's](https://247wallst.com/investing/2025/03/17/hedge-fund-founder-puts-his-faith-in-these-3-stocks-to-deliver-market-beating-returns/); [Valuesider](https://valuesider.com/guru/dev-kantesaria-valley-forge-capital-management/portfolio/2025/4)). It is the youngest record on the list and the one closest to Munger's approach.

### Near misses, operators and historical yardsticks

**Near misses.** Several strong records fall just short.

- **Israel Englander's** Millennium has made about 14% a year since 1989 with one down year ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/hanktucker/2025/09/11/how-the-10-richest-american-hedge-fund-managers-got-20-billion-richer-in-a-year/)).
- **Will Danoff's** Contrafund made 14.14% a year against 11.31% while growing to $177bn ([AI-CIO](https://www.ai-cio.com/news/fidelity-contrafunds-danoff-opportunity-is-everywhere/)). Both of these are superb, but closer to "index plus a few points" than to Buffett.
- **Nick Sleep and Qais Zakaria's** Nomad made 18.4% a year after performance fees, against 6.5% for MSCI World, but for only 12 years before closing in 2014 ([Nomad 2013 letter](https://bishoprockcap.pages.dev/assets/cdn.prod.website-files.com/5b7c186c36ef662dc0a4b05a/677e820b9b0f5161f9184704_2013%20Nomad%20Annual%20Letter.pdf)).
- **Teng Ngiek Lian** in Singapore compounded 17.6% a year for 14 years, then handed about $2bn back to clients ([Asia Asset Management](https://www.asiaasset.com/analysis/target-asset-management-winds-down-in-singapore/)).
- **Quantedge**, another Singapore fund, has made roughly 20% a year since 2006, but with losing years as deep as 29% ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantedge)).
- **Ackman** misses the list despite a real 22-year record, because in the 13 years since his fund listed he has trailed the index.

**Operating companies.** These beat nearly everyone on raw numbers: HEICO returned **22.47% a year since 1993**, TransDigm **26.56% since 2006** and Kinsale **35.11% since 2016** ([totalrealreturns](https://totalrealreturns.com/n/TDG,HEI,ROP,CPRT,KNSL,SPY)). The Swedish Bergman & Beving family of companies made 20% a year from 1976, including spin-offs ([REQ](https://req.no/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/REQ-Acquisition-driven-Compounders-July-2025.pdf)). They are left off the list because these records mostly measure skill at running a business in one industry, not at allocating capital across many.

**Historical yardsticks.** These are the finished records that the living list is measured against:

- Lou Simpson made **20.3% against 13.5%** running GEICO's portfolio from 1980 to 2004 ([MoneyWeek](https://moneyweek.com/448642/the-worlds-greatest-investors-louis-simpson)).
- Walter Schloss made 15.7% a year net for 45 years ([AAII](https://www.aaii.com/journal/article/finding-value-amoung-the-lows-the-walter-j-schloss-approach)).
- Julian Robertson made 31.7% net from 1980 to 1998 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Robertson)).
- Henry Singleton made 20.4% a year at Teledyne from 1963 to 1990 ([Shortform](https://shortform.com/summary/the-outsiders-eight-unconventional-ceos-summary-william-thorndike)).

## Outside money erodes nearly every record within a decade

**The most consistent finding across every region is that records fade.**

- Allan Gray trailed the JSE over the last decade.
- Value Partners Classic made 7.2% over the five years to May 2026, against 29.3% for its index.
- Zhu Shaoxing trailed his benchmark three years running in 2022-24, with ten straight quarters of net withdrawals ([Sina](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2025-07-22/doc-infhiiyw4118526.shtml)).
- Japan's Hifumi fund trailed the TOPIX index over ten years ([Hifumi monthly report](https://apl.wealthadvisor.jp//webasp/pdf/monthly/2008100102_M_202608.pdf)).
- Platinum's since-launch return slid to 10.7% a year after Kerr Neilson stepped back ([Platinum](https://www.platinum.com.au/investments/platinum-international-strategy/)).
- Danaher has only matched the S&P 500 since 2010, at 13.88% a year against 13.65% ([totalrealreturns](https://totalrealreturns.com/n/BX,KKR,DHR,TDY,HHH,SPY)).
- Bridgewater's Pure Alpha fund has made 4.5% a year since 2005 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgewater_Associates)).

Berkshire's own lead disappeared after the mid-1990s. The mechanism is the same everywhere. Success attracts capital, capital dilutes the edge, and size pushes managers into the largest, most efficiently priced stocks.

**The records that held up had one thing in common: someone capped the capital.**

- Medallion shut out outsiders in 1993.
- Citadel handed back about $5bn of its 2025 profits ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/02/ken-griffins-flagship-hedge-fund-at-citadel-rises-10point2percent-in-volatile-2025.html)).
- TCI ran $57bn in 2024 with six employees ([Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/tci-fund-founder-takes-233m-pay-cut/)).
- Dynamo stays closed to new money.
- Druckenmiller closed rather than grow past $12bn.
- Rick Sopher of LCH, which ranks hedge funds by lifetime gains, says the top managers "carefully control capacity" and most "either limited inflows or returned capital" ([Hedge Fund Alpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/news/top-20-hedge-funds-earn-115b-in-2025/)).

The other path is permanent capital. Berkshire, Fairfax and Brookfield are listed companies, so shareholders can sell their shares but cannot pull money out of the business. Buffett noted that Berkshire's float "can never be subject to immediate demands for sums that are large compared to our cash resources" ([2014 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2014ltr.pdf)). Every collapse described earlier involved money investors could withdraw. The listed compounders went through drawdowns of 70% or more with no redemptions to meet.

**Several of these investors have already beaten Buffett over the decades they overlapped.** That undercuts the whole "next Buffett" framing. From January 1993 to November 2026, Berkshire's A shares returned **13.44% a year**, while Brookfield returned **19.09%** and HEICO **22.47%** ([totalrealreturns](https://totalrealreturns.com/n/MKL,L,WTM,BRK-A,BN,SPY)). Over the 20 years to 2025, Fairfax's stock compounded at 17.1% a year ([Fairfax](https://www.fairfax.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/FFH_Fairfax-Financials-Shareholders-Letter-2025-with-attachments-092704.pdf)), against Berkshire's 11.3% (computed). The part nobody can repeat is the early Buffett, who earned more than 40% a year on a small base in 1975-84.

## Flatt passes every successor test; Hohn is the purest heir

Applying the structural traits from "Buffett's Alpha", plus runway, to the leading living candidates gives a clear verdict.

| Test | Flatt | Hohn | Griffin | Watsa | Prashant Jain | Kantesaria |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Own record of ~20 years with a Buffett-like lead | ~20% vs ~9.4% (2002-22) | ~18% vs ~9% (2004-) | 19.19% net (1990-2025) | 19.5% vs 11.5% (1985-2025) | ~17.9% vs 9.6% (1994-2022) | ~15% vs ~10% (2007-24) |
| Permanent capital that cannot be withdrawn | Listed company plus insurance float | Hedge fund; investors can redeem | Hedge fund; quarterly redemptions | Listed company plus $40.8bn float | Mutual fund and portfolio service | Private fund |
| Scale | $103bn market cap | $57-77bn assets | $77bn assets | ~C$55bn market cap (computed) | ₹16,269 crore at 3P | $4.4bn |
| Owns businesses for decades | Yes | Yes, through concentrated stakes in listed companies | No; multi-strategy trading | Yes | Partly | Yes, through stakes in listed companies |
| Runway | Age 61; planning for the "next ±25 years" | Age not verified | Age not verified | Age 76 | Age not verified | Harvard medical degree in 1998 |
| Verdict | Passes all five | Fails on structure | Fails on method and structure | Fails on runway | Fails on scale | Fails on length and scale |

*Sources for the table: Brookfield's [Q4 2025 letter](https://bn.brookfield.com/sites/brookfield-bn-v2/files/BN-IR-Master/Letter-to-Shareholders/2026/Q4-25%20BN%20Letter%20to%20Shareholders_vF.pdf) and [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Flatt); TCI's [Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/tci-fund-founder-takes-233m-pay-cut/) and [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Children%27s_Investment_Fund_Management) entries; Citadel's [KBRA](https://www.kbra.com/publications/gdbMxFGn) report; Fairfax's [2025 letter](https://www.fairfax.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/FFH_Fairfax-Financials-Shareholders-Letter-2025-with-attachments-092704.pdf); 3P's [Tickertape](https://www.tickertape.in/stocks/collections/3p-india-equity-fund-portfolio) page; and Valley Forge's [Value Investor Insight](https://valueinvestorinsight.com/Content_Free/investor-insight-standing-out-october-2019.aspx) profile.*

**Why Flatt.** He is the only candidate who passes every test.

- His own tenure compounded at about 20% a year, roughly double the index.
- Brookfield is permanent, listed capital with a **$103bn market cap and $1.18tn of assets under management**.
- It is building insurance float through Brookfield Wealth Solutions, which has won approval for the UK annuity insurer Just Group and has entered Japan.
- At 61, Flatt has handed the asset-management chief executive role to Connor Teskey while staying CEO of the parent company. He describes this as preparing the next generation "for the next ±25 years" ([Brookfield letter](https://bn.brookfield.com/sites/brookfield-bn-v2/files/BN-IR-Master/Letter-to-Shareholders/2026/Q4-25%20BN%20Letter%20to%20Shareholders_vF.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Flatt)).

**The case for Flatt has real weaknesses.**

- Brookfield fell **72.5% in 2007-09**, far worse than Berkshire's 51% ([totalrealreturns](https://totalrealreturns.com/n/MKL,L,WTM,BRK-A,BN,SPY)).
- Its returns are leveraged and partly come from fees charged on other people's money, which Berkshire never did.
- Over the last 15 years its lead narrowed to **17% against 14%**.

If Brookfield cannot hold a lead of three to five points a year over the next decade, the title passes to someone else.

**The strongest counter-argument is Hohn.** If "the next Buffett" means a stock-picker who buys a handful of businesses with durable competitive advantages and holds them, Hohn is the purest heir. At the end of 2021 TCI held 13 US-listed stocks, with the top five making up two-thirds of assets ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bstnug68kijmzi8f50cg/culture/chris-hohn-is-a-hedge-fund-manager-like-no-other)). He has a Buffett-sized lead after fees over 22 years, and his best results came after he became famous: **$40bn of gains for investors in 2023-25** ([Hedge Fund Alpha](https://hedgefundalpha.com/news/top-20-hedge-funds-earn-115b-in-2025/)). What keeps him second is structure. TCI lost **43% in 2008**. A hedge fund, however long its lock-ups, lacks the permanent capital that let Buffett hold on through 1974 and 1999.

**The others fall short for different reasons.**

- Griffin has better numbers but runs a trading business.
- Watsa has the most Berkshire-like structure but is Buffett's contemporary.
- Prashant Jain and Kantesaria are the most promising heirs among conventional fund managers, but they manage only a few billion dollars. On the arithmetic above, Kantesaria's record will not be statistically conclusive until the 2030s.

## Conclusion

The search shows that Buffett's role has split into pieces that no single successor holds:

- Hohn has the stock-picking.
- Watsa and Flatt have the structure: permanent capital funded by insurance float.
- Constellation and HEICO have the decentralised acquisition culture.
- Medallion and Citadel have the raw compounding rate.

Flatt is the best bet to put the pieces back together. The deeper lesson is that the next Berkshire is more likely to be a structure than a genius. Capped or permanent capital is the common thread in every record that survived. Money investors could withdraw is the common thread in every collapse.

Two practical points follow. First, the best current records are mostly closed to outside investors: Medallion, Dynamo, Duquesne and Appaloosa. The heirs most people can actually own are listed permanent-capital companies, whose returns reach shareholders without being eroded by redemptions. Second, the true next Buffett cannot be identified yet. An excellent manager needs about 16 years to show statistical significance, so someone who starts compounding at 20% today will not be provable before the 2040s. Most numbers quoted for "the Chinese Buffett" or "the Indian Buffett" would not survive a basic audit. The filter that works is simple: 20 years of results, after fees, against a local benchmark, judged after the manager became famous. It removes most of the famous names and points to Rio, Cape Town, Mumbai and Toronto instead.
