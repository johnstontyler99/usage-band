Pool alfresco gable: window layout concepts

Draft 2: a glazed wall that replaces the glass pool fence on the same line and becomes the pool barrier. There are double pivot doors in the centre and two windows either side. Steel support posts sit between the windows, at the door jambs and at each brick end. Each window is 1,200 of solid glass with louvres above, behind fixed security screens, up to the beam. The doors open into a small square glass-fenced lobby on the pool side, with a self-closing gate out to the pool. The opening is 11,844 wide, and the 3,110 plate height is floor to the top of the steel beam, where the 9.7° rake starts (apex about 4,120). Gable posts line through above. 5 · Design notes

Alignment logic

  • The wall sits on the pool fence line, so the new glazing reads as the fence carried up to the ceiling.
  • Two horizontal datums run right across: the 1200 fence top (option C's fixed glass base) and the door head (2400), with a transom through every louvre bay.
  • The verticals line through. Each gable post sits directly over a lower post or mullion, so nothing above the beam floats. The tool snaps the gable posts to the nearest lower vertical automatically.
  • High louvres vent heat. Louvred highlights let hot air out from under the raked ceiling. Set them to fixed glass if you'd rather.
  • Frames in charcoal to match the existing window frames and fence posts. Paint the steel the same colour.

Structure (engineer to confirm)

  • The lower glazing head is the plate height less the beam depth. Change the beam depth above and the drawings follow.
  • Steel posts at the door jambs break the 11.8 m beam into shorter spans (listed in the chips at the top). They also give the doors a rigid frame to hang on.
  • The gable posts bear on the beam. Over the doors that's a short span. Out in the side bays, let the engineer know there are point loads, or treat those posts as glazing mullions only.
  • At 9.7° the gable glazing is only about 1.0 m tall at the centre and tapers to nothing at the ends. Consider cladding the last 400 to 600 mm of each end triangle rather than glazing a sliver.

Louvres

  • Every louvre gets a fixed security screen (stainless mesh, e.g. Crimsafe or similar), so the louvres can open fully and still meet the barrier rules. Fit the screens on the pool side, so the louvre handles stay reachable from inside the alfresco. They must only come off with a tool.
  • Glass louvre blades are usually limited to about 1,200 mm long, so any wider bay is split into galleries with a mullion. The "max gallery width" setting controls that.
  • The door-head transom keeps gallery heights around 1.2 to 2.4 m, within what most louvre systems cover. Confirm with the supplier (e.g. Breezway or similar).
  • For option C, keep the narrow and wide rhythm from your sketch by changing which bays are louvres and which are fixed later. The grid stays the same.

Pool barrier: wall + lobby

The wall replaces the glass fence, so it is the pool barrier (NSW Swimming Pools Act and Regulation, AS 1926.1). How the draft meets that:
  • Doors open into a fenced lobby, not the pool area. The square glass fence on the pool side, with its gate, separates the doors from the pool, so the pivot doors sit outside the barrier.
  • Gate: self-closing and self-latching, with the latch at 1,500 or higher, opening into the lobby (away from the pool), as in your sketch. The lobby needs to be deep enough for the gate to swing clear (the page warns you if it isn't).
  • Fixed glass from the floor to 1,200 along the rest of the wall is non-climbable and matches the fence height.
  • The louvres have fixed security screens, so they can open fully without restrictors. The screens must be fixed so they can't be removed without tools (AS 1926.1 and the NSW rules). Give the certifier the screen product's specification.
  • Keep the inside of the lobby clear of anything climbable within 900 of the fence, which is easy because the doors and walls are glass. The bench seat is on the pool side, which is fine.
  • The old fence's glass panels and spigots could be reused for the lobby.
Get the certifier to confirm the lobby arrangement before detailing.

Still to confirm

  • The lobby size. Draft 2 assumes 2,200 wide, so the lobby fence returns onto the door-jamb steel posts, and 1,500 deep, with a 900 gate. Are those right?
  • The steel post size (100 SHS assumed). There are six posts: one at each brick end, two between the windows and two at the door jambs. The gable posts sit over the centre of the doors and over the two posts between the windows.
  • Pivot doors: full height (shown) or to the transom; the pivot hardware brand and leaf weight. At about 960 × 2,860 with 10.38 laminated glass, each leaf is roughly 70–80 kg. Should they open both ways, and is a floor spring wanted?
  • Is the width at floor level the same as at the plate (are the brick returns plumb?), and what's the slab fall at the fence line? This matters for the pivot floor boxes and sill drainage.
  • The steel beam size from the engineer. The page assumes 200 deep within the 3,110.
